# Brief: ST-7 — Time series I: stationarity, autocovariance, AR/MA/ARMA, Yule–Walker, forecasting, ARIMA, unit roots
Folder: curriculum/03-statistical-modeling/07-time-series-1/ · ~12 hours · prereqs ST-3, F5 (Markov chains, covariance), LA-7 (eigenvalues; companion matrices) · unlocks ST-8
Pair with: Shumway & Stoffer *Time Series Analysis and Its Applications* ch. 1–3 (free "EZ" edition online); Brockwell & Davis *Introduction to Time Series and Forecasting* ch. 1–6 (cleaner theory); Hyndman & Athanasopoulos *Forecasting: Principles and Practice* (free online) ch. 8–9 for the applied side; Hamilton *Time Series Analysis* ch. 1–5 for reference.
Conventions: {x_t}, t ∈ Z; mean μ, autocovariance γ(h) = Cov(x_{t+h}, x_t), autocorrelation ρ(h) = γ(h)/γ(0); white noise w_t ~ WN(0, σ²); backshift B x_t = x_{t−1}; AR(p): φ(B)x_t = w_t with φ(z) = 1 − φ₁z − … − φ_pz^p; MA(q): x_t = θ(B)w_t with θ(z) = 1 + θ₁z + … + θ_qz^q; ARMA(p,q); ARIMA(p,d,q) with ∇^d = (1−B)^d; the sample ACF ρ̂(h); PACF.

§1: Yule (1927) invented autoregression to model sunspots — the first stochastic model of oscillation driven by noise, replacing deterministic periodicities; Slutsky (1927) showed moving averages of noise produce cycles that look real; Wold (1938) proved every stationary process is an infinite MA plus a deterministic part; Box and Jenkins (1970) made ARIMA an engineering discipline; Granger and Engle (1980s) found that regressing one random walk on another gives spurious results, and cointegration; the forcing problem: ST-3's regression assumes independent errors, and data indexed by time almost never has them — ignoring the dependence gives wrong standard errors, wrong forecasts, and spurious relationships, so dependence has to be modeled, and the natural models are linear filters driven by white noise. §2: a stationary series is one whose statistical character doesn't drift — it has a mean to return to and a correlation structure that depends only on lag; the autocorrelation function is the series' memory; an AR process is "today is a weighted sum of recent days plus a shock" (a linear recurrence — LA-7's companion matrix, so stability is an eigenvalue condition), an MA process is "today is a weighted sum of recent shocks"; forecasting is conditional expectation (F5), which for these models is linear; differencing turns a random walk into noise, which is why ARIMA has the "I".

Topics in order: examples of time series and what distinguishes them from iid data (trend, seasonality, persistence, volatility clustering); the white-noise, random-walk, and moving-average processes; STATIONARITY: strict vs weak (second-order), the autocovariance function and its properties (γ(0) ≥ 0, |γ(h)| ≤ γ(0), even — prove; nonnegative definiteness and LA-8's PSD matrices: the autocovariance matrix of n consecutive values is a Toeplitz PSD matrix — make this link), the autocorrelation function; estimating μ and γ(h) from one realization (ergodicity assumed; the sample ACF and its bias), the sampling distribution of ρ̂(h) under white noise (≈ N(0, 1/n) — the Bartlett bound; derive at the heuristic level) and the ±2/√n bands; the random walk is not stationary (derive Var(x_t) = tσ²) and the sample ACF of a random walk decays slowly — the diagnostic; LINEAR PROCESSES x_t = Σψ_jw_{t−j} with Σ|ψ_j| < ∞: mean and autocovariance (derive γ(h) = σ²Σψ_jψ_{j+h}); THE AR(1) PROCESS in full: the recursion, the stationarity condition |φ| < 1 (derive by iterating backward; the explosive case), the MA(∞) representation, γ(h) = σ²φ^{|h|}/(1−φ²) (derive), the ACF's geometric decay, the connection to CM-1's damped system driven by noise and to F5's Markov chains (AR(1) is a Gaussian Markov process; the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process as its continuous-time limit, stated); THE MA(1) PROCESS: γ(h) (derive; zero beyond lag 1), invertibility (|θ| < 1 so that w_t can be recovered from the x's; the non-identifiability θ vs 1/θ — derive), the general MA(q) ACF cutoff; AR(p): the characteristic polynomial, STATIONARITY iff all roots of φ(z) lie outside the unit circle (derive via the companion-matrix eigenvalues — LA-7: the recurrence is stable iff eigenvalues are inside the unit circle, and they are the reciprocals of the roots of φ; make this the derivation), the Yule–Walker equations γ(h) = Σφ_jγ(h−j) (derive by multiplying by x_{t−h} and taking expectations), solving them for φ given γ (LA-2: a Toeplitz linear system) — the Yule–Walker estimator, the AR(2) case fully (the region of stationarity in the (φ₁, φ₂) plane — derive the triangle; complex roots ⇒ quasi-periodic ACF, Yule's sunspots), the PARTIAL AUTOCORRELATION FUNCTION (the correlation between x_t and x_{t+h} after regressing out the intermediate values — ST-3's FWL; derive that the PACF of an AR(p) cuts off after lag p, the dual of the MA ACF cutoff), the Durbin–Levinson recursion stated (and the fact that the PACF at lag h is the last Yule–Walker coefficient of the AR(h) fit); ARMA(p,q): the model, causality and invertibility conditions, the ψ-weights by equating coefficients (derive for ARMA(1,1)), the ACF of ARMA(1,1) (derive), parameter redundancy (common factors), identification from ACF/PACF (the table: AR cuts PACF, MA cuts ACF, ARMA tails off in both); ESTIMATION: Yule–Walker (method of moments — ST-1) for AR, conditional least squares, MAXIMUM LIKELIHOOD for ARMA via the Gaussian likelihood (the innovations form: the likelihood as a product of one-step prediction densities; §8 honest: stationarity is the whole subject and students can't define it after a month — the autocovariance-depends-only-on-lag definition must be automatic; the sample ACF of a random walk looks "significant" at every lag and beginners read it as structure; AR stationarity is an eigenvalue condition and the companion-matrix derivation is the one that makes it stick (LA-7 pays off here); the MA invertibility/identifiability issue is where estimation code silently returns the wrong θ; the exact Gaussian likelihood is a Toeplitz-covariance MVN and students don't recognize F5's formula in it; the Dickey–Fuller distribution is non-standard for reasons this module can only sketch; spurious regression is the single most important practical lesson — regression on time series without checking for unit roots is malpractice; Box–Jenkins identification by eyeballing ACF/PACF is an art the module can only start.
