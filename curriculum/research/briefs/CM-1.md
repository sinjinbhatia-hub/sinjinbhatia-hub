# Brief: CM-1 — Newtonian mechanics revisited: conservation laws, and the problem constraints create
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/a-classical-mechanics/01-newtonian-mechanics-revisited/ · ~6 hours · prereq ODEs · unlocks CM-2
Pair with: Taylor ch. 1–4; Morin ch. 1–5; Feynman Lectures I ch. 9–14. Conventions: SI; bold vectors; ẍ for time derivatives; F = ṗ.

A short, sharp review whose purpose is to set up CM-2's question. §1: Newton (1687) is complete in principle, but constrained systems force you to solve for constraint forces you don't care about in coordinates that don't fit; d'Alembert, Euler, Lagrange spent a century fixing this. Make that pain concrete. §2: forces change momentum; conservation laws as symmetries (teaser); phase space; a constraint as a surface the motion is glued to.

Topics: Newton's laws stated carefully (first = inertial frames; second F = ṗ; third and its failure for magnetic forces); Galilean relativity; center-of-mass theorem DERIVED via the third law; angular momentum and τ = L̇ for a system (derive; role of central internal forces); work–energy theorem (derive); conservative forces: three equivalent conditions (prove what's provable here; EM-1 Helmholtz for the rest); energy conservation; 1D motion from energy: turning points, period integral, reading phase portraits from V(x), the pendulum separatrix; small oscillations ω² = V''/m (derive); linear and quadratic drag (terminal velocity; closed-form projectile with linear drag); damped driven oscillator complete review (transient + steady state, resonance, Q, phase lag; F3 link); rocket equation with the sign error called out; THE CONSTRAINT PAIN, three Newtonian examples: (a) pendulum in Cartesian with tension unknown, (b) bead on y = kx² with normal force eliminated, (c) Atwood; after each: "CM-3 does this in three lines"; generalized coordinates informally; numerical integration: Euler fails on oscillators (energy grows), RK4, symplectic preview (CM-8).

Worked examples (≥5): exploding shell CM; pendulum period as elliptic integral with T ≈ T₀(1 + θ₀²/16) derived; projectile with linear drag; driven oscillator m = 1 kg, k = 100 N/m, b = 2 kg/s, F₀ = 1 N amplitude/phase at resonance; bead on the parabolic wire with the normal force at the bottom.

Project: nonlinear pendulum with Euler, RK4 (solve_ivp), hand-written symplectic Euler; energy vs time over 100 periods for each; phase portrait with separatrix; period vs amplitude: numerical vs ellipk vs small-angle correction; quadratic-drag projectile; driven oscillator steady-state amplitude vs frequency over the analytic curve; bead on wire: integrate and compute the normal force; Atwood numerically. "Your turn": Coulomb friction oscillator (linear amplitude decay); period in V = x⁴ vs amplitude; rocket vs closed form.

§8 honest: admitting you don't fully understand things you passed exams on; conservative forces and work–energy misstated routinely; "internal forces cancel" hand-waved; rocket sign; Euler looks fine for 10 periods and is garbage at 100; the constraint examples are deliberately painful.
