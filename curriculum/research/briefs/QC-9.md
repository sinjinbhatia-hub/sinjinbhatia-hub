§1: Feynman (1982) proposed quantum computers to simulate quantum physics, because the state of n interacting spins needs 2ⁿ numbers classically; Lloyd (1996) showed that local Hamiltonians can be simulated efficiently by splitting the evolution into small steps (Trotter 1959, Suzuki 1976 — the same splitting as CM-8's symplectic integrators); Abrams and Lloyd (1999) and Aspuru-Guzik et al. (2005) combined simulation with phase estimation (QC-5) to compute molecular energies; when real hardware arrived without error correction (2016–), Peruzzo et al. (2014) and Farhi et al. (2014) proposed hybrid algorithms — a short quantum circuit with tunable parameters, optimized by a classical computer — VQE for chemistry (the variational principle of QM-8) and QAOA for optimization (a Trotterized adiabatic path); Preskill (2018) named the era NISQ; since then barren plateaus, shot noise, and classical simulation advances have tempered the hopes, and the most credible near-term value remains simulating quantum physics itself. The forcing problem: chemistry, materials, and condensed-matter physics need ground-state energies and dynamics of interacting quantum systems that are exponentially hard classically (QM-4/QM-7: the Hilbert space grows as 2ⁿ), and the fault-tolerant algorithms (QC-5 phase estimation) are far away; this module covers both the long-term route (Trotter + phase estimation) and the near-term attempts (variational), with an honest account of what is known to work. §2: e^{−i(A+B)t} ≠ e^{−iAt}e^{−iBt} when A and B don't commute (LA-7/LA-11's e^{A+B} warning), but for small steps the error is the commutator times Δt², so many small alternating steps approximate the true evolution — CM-8's Störmer–Verlet is exactly this splitting for H = T + V; once you can apply e^{−iHt}, phase estimation reads off its eigenvalues (energies); a variational algorithm is QM-8's variational principle run on hardware: guess a parametrized state, measure its energy, adjust the parameters downhill (ML-2) — the quantum computer only prepares and measures; QAOA alternates the problem Hamiltonian and a mixing Hamiltonian, which is a discretized adiabatic sweep (QM-8's adiabatic theorem: go slowly enough and you stay in the ground state, where "slowly" is set by the minimum gap).
