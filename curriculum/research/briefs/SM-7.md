# Brief: SM-7 — Bridge: statistical mechanics ↔ probabilistic machine learning
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/c-thermo-statistical-mechanics/07-stat-mech-ml-bridge/ · ~8 hours · prereqs SM-4, SM-6, ST-6 (variational inference, MCMC) helpful, F5 · unlocks ML-10
Pair with: MacKay *Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms* ch. 31 (Ising models), 41–43 (Hopfield, Boltzmann machines, variational methods); Sethna ch. 8; Mehta et al., "A high-bias, low-variance introduction to Machine Learning for physicists" (Physics Reports 2019) §XVI–XVII; Hinton's Boltzmann-machine papers (1985) for history; Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015) for diffusion models.
Conventions: energy E(x) and the Boltzmann/Gibbs distribution p(x) = e^{−E(x)}/Z with β absorbed; log-partition function ln Z = log-sum-exp; softmax; KL divergence D(q‖p); variational free energy / negative ELBO; Langevin dynamics dx = −∇E dt + √2 dW.

§1: the same mathematics was discovered twice — by Boltzmann and Gibbs for gases, and by Hopfield (1982), Hinton and Sejnowski (1985), Jordan and colleagues (1990s variational methods), and the diffusion-model authors (2015–2020) for inference and learning; Hopfield's 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics for a neural network is the institutional admission that these are one subject; the problem that forced the bridge: computing Z (or a posterior normalizer) is intractable in both fields, and both fields invented the same two workarounds — sampling (Metropolis/MCMC) and variational approximation (mean-field/ELBO). This module exists so the student never has to learn the same idea twice under different names. §2: a dictionary, built progressively: energy ↔ negative log-probability (or loss); Boltzmann distribution ↔ softmax; partition function ↔ normalizer; free energy ↔ negative log-likelihood (or ELBO); temperature ↔ inverse sharpness; mean-field theory ↔ variational inference with a factorized family; Metropolis ↔ MCMC; Langevin ↔ noisy gradient descent / diffusion; Ising model ↔ Boltzmann machine / Markov random field; ferromagnet below T_c ↔ a memory with stable attractors.

Topics in order: THE DICTIONARY as a table, then each row derived: (1) softmax IS the Boltzmann distribution: p_i = e^{z_i}/Σe^{z_j} with z_i = −βE_i; temperature scaling of logits ("softmax temperature" in LLM sampling is literally β⁻¹); the log-sum-exp as ln Z and its derivatives giving the mean (∂ ln Z/∂z_i = p_i — SM-4's ⟨E⟩ = −∂ln Z/∂β in disguise; derive both and match them); the cross-entropy loss of ML-3 as −log p and its gradient p − y (LA-14) reinterpreted as "the model's thermal average minus the data" — the exact form of the Boltzmann machine learning rule; (2) FREE ENERGY AND THE ELBO: the Gibbs–Bogoliubov–Feynman variational principle F ≤ F_q = ⟨E⟩_q − TS[q] for any trial distribution q (derive via D(q‖p) ≥ 0, F5); translate: ln p(data) ≥ E_q[ln p(data, z)] − E_q[ln q(z)] = ELBO, i.e., the ELBO is minus a variational free energy and the gap is exactly D(q‖p(z|data)) (derive both and align term by term); MEAN-FIELD THEORY IS VARIATIONAL INFERENCE with a factorized q: derive SM-6's self-consistency equation m = tanh(β(zJm + h)) by minimizing F_q over product distributions q(s) = Π q_i(s_i) — the same equation, now as an optimization (this is the module's showpiece derivation); the coordinate-ascent variational inference update as the mean-field self-consistency iteration (ST-6 link); (3) THE ISING MODEL IS A BOLTZMANN MACHINE / MARKOV RANDOM FIELD: p(s) ∝ exp(Σ J_ij s_is_j + Σ h_i s_i) with learned J and h; the maximum-likelihood gradient ∂ln p(data)/∂J_ij = ⟨s_is_j⟩_data − ⟨s_is_j⟩_model (derive — the "positive phase minus negative phase" rule; the negative phase requires sampling the model, i.e., Metropolis/Gibbs); contrastive divergence in one paragraph; restricted Boltzmann machines and why the bipartite structure makes Gibbs sampling easy (derive the conditional distributions as sigmoids — the sigmoid is the two-state Boltzmann factor, SM-4's paramagnet); (4) HOPFIELD NETWORKS: the energy E = −½Σ J_ij s_is_j with Hebbian J_ij = (1/N)Σ_μ ξ_i^μ ξ_j^μ; the stored patterns as energy minima (derive the stability condition for a stored pattern and the crosstalk term); the asynchronous update as zero-temperature Metropolis (prove E never increases); the capacity ~0.138N stated (the Amit–Gutfreund–Sompolinsky result from spin-glass theory, one paragraph); the modern dense associative memory / attention connection in one paragraph (Ramsauer et al. 2020: the update rule of a modern Hopfield network is softmax attention — the ML-9 link, stated with the formula); (5) MCMC IN BOTH LANGUAGES: Metropolis–Hastings (ST-6) is Metropolis (SM-6) with a general proposal; Gibbs sampling is the heat-bath algorithm; detailed balance is the shared foundation (F5); why Bayesian posteriors are Boltzmann distributions with E = −ln p(θ) − ln p(data|θ) and T = 1; tempering/annealing as literally varying β (parallel tempering, annealed importance sampling for estimating Z/evidence — the thermodynamic integration identity ln Z(β) − ln Z(0) = −∫⟨E⟩_β dβ, derive it; this is how physicists compute free energies and how ML computes marginal likelihoods); (6) LANGEVIN DYNAMICS AND DIFFUSION MODELS: the overdamped Langevin equation dx = −∇E(x)dt + √(2T)dW samples the Boltzmann distribution (state the Fokker–Planck equation and verify that e^{−E/T} is its stationary solution — derive this one-dimensional check); stochastic gradient descent as Langevin dynamics with an effective temperature set by the learning rate and batch noise (the argument, one page); score matching: the score ∇_x ln p = −∇E; diffusion models in one page: forward noising as a diffusion to a Gaussian, reverse process requires the score at each noise level, learned by a network — so a diffusion model is a learned Langevin sampler (Sohl-Dickstein's original paper is titled after non-equilibrium thermodynamics for this reason); (7) ENTROPY AND INFORMATION everywhere: maximum entropy (SM-4/F5) as the principle behind exponential-family models (ST-4: GLMs are max-ent models with sufficient-statistic constraints — derive the correspondence); the Fisher information as a metric and the thermodynamic susceptibilities as the same second derivatives of ln Z (derive: Var(E) = ∂²ln Z/∂β² is the Fisher information of the Boltzmann family in β — one line, big implication); (8) PHASE TRANSITIONS IN LEARNING: the "grokking"/sudden-generalization phenomena and the statistical-physics theory of learning curves (Gardner, Seung–Sompolinsky–Tishby) in one honest paragraph as pointers, not claims; the replica method named but not done.

Worked examples (≥6): softmax at three temperatures on fixed logits, with ln Z and the mean; the variational free energy of a product-state q for a 2-spin Ising model vs the exact F (showing F_q ≥ F and the gap as KL); the mean-field equation rederived as variational inference for a 3-spin ring; the Boltzmann-machine gradient for a 2-unit model fit to a given data distribution (closed form); a Hopfield network with N = 8 storing two patterns: energies of the patterns, a corrupted input converging; the Fokker–Planck stationarity check for a double-well E.

Project (numpy only, plus optional sklearn for a baseline nowhere): softmax/log-sum-exp/temperature demo with the derivative identities asserted numerically against finite differences; variational mean-field vs exact for small Ising systems (exhaustive enumeration up to 16 spins): F_q − F plotted vs T (assert ≥ 0), and mean-field m vs exact m; train a tiny Boltzmann machine (visible units only, 6–8 spins) by maximum likelihood with exact gradients (enumeration) and with Metropolis-sampled negative phase, compare learned J to the generating J; a Hopfield network storing 3 patterns of 100 bits, recall from 20% corrupted inputs, capacity curve (fraction recalled vs number of stored patterns) showing the ~0.14N breakdown; Langevin sampling of a double-well with the histogram vs e^{−E/T} (assert) at two temperatures; thermodynamic integration to estimate ln Z of a small Ising system vs the exact value (assert); a 1D toy diffusion model: forward noising of a bimodal dataset, the exact score computed, reverse Langevin sampling recovering the bimodal distribution (no neural network needed; say that ML-10 replaces the exact score by a learned one); exponential-family/max-ent: fit a Boltzmann distribution to match a given mean and variance on a discrete support by solving for the multipliers (ST-4 preview). §8 honest: the dictionary is exact in places (softmax = Boltzmann) and only analogical in others (SGD ≈ Langevin) — the module marks each row; "free energy" means four slightly different things across the two fields and the sign conventions are a minefield; mean-field = variational inference is the deepest row and worth deriving twice; Hopfield capacity theory is spin-glass physics the module can only point to; diffusion models are presented in their simplest discrete-time form and the continuous SDE formulation is left to ML-10; physicists and ML researchers use "temperature" with opposite intuitions about what "high" means for a model (sharper vs flatter) — be explicit about β vs T everywhere.
