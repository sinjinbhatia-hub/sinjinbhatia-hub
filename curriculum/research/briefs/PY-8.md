§1 (the problem): the Lagrangian of a double pendulum has Euler–Lagrange equations no one wants to derive by hand twice, and once derived they need to be integrated numerically with controlled error and plotted. That is three tools — a symbolic engine for the algebra, an ODE solver for the dynamics, and the array/plot stack from PY-6/7 — and the skill is the pipeline between them. The forcing problem: derive the equations of motion of a pendulum symbolically, turn them into fast numerical functions, integrate them, check energy conservation, and fit a measured period to extract g. §2 (the intuition): SciPy is a cabinet of well-tested numerical algorithms behind uniform interfaces (pass a function, get a result object — read it); SymPy manipulates expression trees exactly, which is slow and precise, and `lambdify` compiles a tree into a NumPy function, which is fast and approximate; the pipeline is symbolic for structure, numeric for numbers.

Topics in order: SciPy — reading a result object (`OptimizeResult`, `OdeResult`: `.success`, `.message`, `.x`/`.y`, `.nfev`); `optimize`: `root_scalar` (brentq, newton; compare with PY-1's), `root` for systems, `minimize` (Nelder–Mead, BFGS with and without a gradient, bounds, constraints in one paragraph), `minimize_scalar`, `curve_fit` (returns covariance — turn it into parameter uncertainties and say what they assume), `least_squares` and robust loss, local vs global and `differential_evolution` named; `integrate`: `quad` (and its error estimate, `points` for kinks, infinite limits), `dblquad`, `solve_ivp` in depth — writing the RHS with the state vector, `method` (RK45 default, DOP853 for accuracy, Radau/LSODA for stiff — show a stiff example where RK45 crawls), `rtol`/`atol` meaning and the default's inadequacy for energy conservation, `t_eval` vs `dense_output`, `events` (zero crossings; the pendulum period from events), `max_step`, first-order reduction of second-order ODEs, systems; `linalg` beyond numpy: `lu`, `qr`, `svd`, `eigh`, `expm` (the matrix exponential solving linear ODEs exactly — LA-7/LA-11 preview), `solve_banded`, `solve_triangular`, `null_space`, `orth`; `sparse`: CSR/CSC/COO and when each, building the 1D Laplacian with `diags`, `spsolve`, `eigsh` for a few eigenvalues, dense vs sparse memory and time at n = 10⁴ (the QM-3 finite-difference Schrödinger solver is exactly this); `interpolate`: `interp1d` vs `CubicSpline`, `splrep`/`splev` named, interpolation vs fitting, Runge's phenomenon in one figure; `special`: `gamma`, `erf`, `erfc`, `legendre`/`eval_legendre`, `sph_harm`, Bessel `jv` (EM-3, QM-5/6 destinations); `stats`: the distribution API (`norm`, `t`, `chi2`, `poisson`: `pdf`/`pmf`, `cdf`, `ppf`, `rvs(random_state=rng)`, `fit`), `stats.describe`, a two-sample `ttest_ind` as a preview of ST-2; `fft`: `fft`, `fftfreq`, `rfft`, the convention and `fftshift` (F3's conventions box); `signal` in one paragraph; `constants`. SymPy — `symbols` with assumptions, expressions as trees (`srepr`), `simplify` family (`expand`, `factor`, `collect`, `cancel`, `trigsimp`, `together`, `apart`), `subs` and `evalf`/`N`, exact rationals (`sp.Rational(1, 3)` vs `1/3` — the number-one gotcha), `Eq`, `solve` vs `solveset` vs `nsolve`, `diff` (partial, higher), `integrate` (definite, indefinite, when it fails), `limit`, `series`, `summation`, `Matrix` (det, inv, eigenvals, eigenvects, nullspace, rref — LA-2/LA-7 checks), `Function` and `dsolve` for ODEs with closed forms, `lambdify` and its `modules`, `cse` for common subexpressions, printing (`pprint`, `latex`, `init_printing`), performance (SymPy is slow; never in an inner loop; lambdify).

Build-it-yourself sequences: (1) the pendulum pipeline — Lagrangian in SymPy → Euler–Lagrange via `diff` → solve for θ̈ → `lambdify` → `solve_ivp` → energy check → period from events vs the elliptic-integral formula (`special.ellipk`) — the module's spine; (2) the double pendulum the same way (equations too long to derive by hand — that is the point), with chaos shown by divergence of nearby initial conditions; (3) `rtol`/`atol` experiment: energy drift vs tolerance over 100 periods, log-log; (4) stiff vs non-stiff: the Robertson or a simple stiff decay with RK45 (`nfev` huge) vs Radau; (5) `curve_fit` for the period vs length data with noise → g with uncertainty from the covariance; (6) the 1D Laplacian sparse vs dense: build, solve Poisson with a known source, time both at n = 10³ and 10⁴; (7) `expm` vs `solve_ivp` on a linear system — exact vs numerical; (8) SymPy sanity: `Rational` vs float, `simplify` on a trig identity, `solve` returning a list vs `solveset` a set, `lambdify` benchmark vs `subs`+`evalf` (1000× faster).

§8 honest: default `solve_ivp` tolerances (rtol 1e-3) are fine for a picture and wrong for a physics claim — every student plots a slowly spiraling "conserved" energy once; the result object has a `.success` and you will ignore it until a silent failure costs you a day; `1/3` in SymPy is a float and poisons the expression — `Rational` or `S(1)/3`; `solve` returns a list, `solveset` a set, and both can return nothing for equations with solutions; `lambdify` is the bridge and forgetting `modules="numpy"` gives you `math.sin` that cannot take arrays; sparse matrices are not arrays — `*` is matrix multiply for some formats and elementwise for others, use `@`; `curve_fit` covariance assumes the model is right and the noise is Gaussian — say so when you quote the uncertainty; SymPy is slow and students put it in loops.
