# Brief: F2 — Fourier series
Folder: curriculum/00-foundations/02-fourier-series/ · ~10 hours · prereqs LA-5, LA-8 · unlocks F3, EM-3, QM-3, ST-8
Pair with: Osgood *Lectures on the Fourier Transform and Its Applications* ch. 1; Stein & Shakarchi *Fourier Analysis* ch. 1–3; MIT 18.06SC "Fourier series" unit.

CONVENTIONS (box at top, repeat at use): on [−π, π] real form f ~ a_0/2 + Σ(a_n cos nx + b_n sin nx), a_n = (1/π)∫f cos nx, b_n = (1/π)∫f sin nx; complex form c_n = (1/2π)∫f e^{−inx}; general period T uses e^{2πint/T} and 1/T. State the a_n,b_n ↔ c_n relation and that other books move 2π and 1/2 around.

§1: Fourier's 1807 heat memoir, Lagrange/Laplace objections, 1811 prize, Dirichlet 1829; the earlier Bernoulli–Euler–d'Alembert vibrating-string dispute (1750s) is the same question. The forcing problem: heat/wave equations on an interval have simple solutions for sinusoidal data; linearity gives sums; is every initial condition such a sum? §2: periodic functions are vectors in an infinite-dimensional space; {1, cos nx, sin nx} are perpendicular; a Fourier coefficient is the shadow of f on one — LA-5's projection onto an orthonormal basis with the dot product replaced by an integral. Reader should guess the coefficient formulas before §3.

Topics in order: inner product ⟨f,g⟩ = ∫ f̄g on piecewise-continuous functions (axioms; the measure-zero subtlety in one sentence); orthogonality of {1, cos nx, sin nx} — DERIVE every integral; {e^{inx}} one line; norms; COEFFICIENT FORMULAS DERIVED as orthogonal projection; S_N f minimizes ‖f − g‖ over trig polynomials of degree N (prove — LA-5 projection theorem); complex form, c_n = (a_n − ib_n)/2, c_{−n} = c̄_n for real f (derive); even/odd, cosine/sine series; FULLY COMPUTED: square wave, sawtooth f(x)=x, triangle |x|, x² on [−π,π]; Bessel's inequality (derive) and Parseval (state; prove assuming mean-square convergence); Σ1/n² = π²/6 from x (full) and Σ1/n⁴ = π⁴/90 from x²; Riemann–Lebesgue lemma from Bessel; DECAY RATE: k continuous periodic derivatives ⇒ |c_n| ≤ C/n^{k+1} by integration by parts; the examples' 1/n, 1/n², 1/n³ patterns; Dirichlet kernel D_N(x) = sin((N+½)x)/sin(x/2) DERIVED; S_N f as convolution with D_N (derive); Dirichlet's theorem stated precisely with an honest proof sketch; GIBBS: overshoot ≈ 9% of the jump, does not vanish — derive the limit via Si(π)/π − ½ ≈ 0.0895; uniform convergence when Σ|c_n| < ∞ (M-test; C¹ periodic ⇒ uniform); termwise differentiation (when) and integration (always; derive); PDE APPLICATIONS: heat equation on [0,L] with zero BC — separation of variables, sine series, mode decay e^{−k(nπ/L)²t} (full), high modes die first; vibrating string — standing waves, mode frequencies nπc/L (CM-6 preview: continuum limit of the coupled-oscillator eigenproblem); EIGENFUNCTION EXPANSION: d²/dx² with periodic BC is self-adjoint on this inner product (prove via integration by parts), eigenfunctions e^{inx}, eigenvalues −n², and LA-8's spectral theorem is why the Fourier basis is orthogonal — §5 centerpiece; Sturm–Liouville preview (Legendre, Bessel, Hermite in EM-3/QM-3/QM-6); DFT preview (sample at N points; coefficient integrals become sums; F3).

Worked examples (≥5): the four standard waves with every integral; Basel via Parseval with arithmetic; heat on a rod with a step initial temperature, first three terms and the mode time scale; string plucked at center (triangle), amplitudes ∝ 1/n²; the Gibbs overshoot computation.
