# Brief: QM-3 — One-dimensional problems: infinite well, harmonic oscillator (analytic and ladder operators), free particle, delta and finite wells, scattering and tunneling
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/d-quantum-mechanics/03-one-dimensional-problems/ · ~16 hours · prereq QM-2 · unlocks QM-4, SM-5 (box states; oscillator), CM-6 (quantized modes)
Pair with: Griffiths & Schroeter ch. 2; MIT 8.04 lectures 7–16; Zwiebach *Mastering QM* ch. 6–9; Shankar ch. 5, 7 (the oscillator by operators); Townsend ch. 6–7.
Conventions: ℏ explicit; well on [0, a] (Griffiths) with ψ_n = √(2/a) sin(nπx/a); oscillator ladder operators a_± = (1/√(2ℏmω))(∓ip + mωx) with [a_−, a_+] = 1 (Griffiths' notation; note Shankar's a, a†); transmission T = |F/A|²; k = √(2mE)/ℏ, κ = √(2m(V₀ − E))/ℏ.

§1: the first problems Schrödinger solved (1926: hydrogen, oscillator); the particle in a box as the model for electrons in metals (Sommerfeld 1927, SM-5), quantum dots, and conjugated molecules; Gamow and Gurney–Condon (1928) explaining alpha decay by tunneling — the first quantum prediction of a rate; the scanning tunneling microscope (1981) as tunneling made into an instrument; the problem that forced each solution: discrete spectra needed a mechanism (confinement quantizes), and barrier penetration needed an explanation classical physics flatly forbids. §2: a confined wave must fit — standing waves on a string (F2/CM-6) give discrete frequencies, and here frequencies are energies; curvature of ψ is set by E − V: oscillatory where E > V, exponentially decaying where E < V, so a wavefunction can leak into a forbidden region; the oscillator's ladder operators as "raise/lower the energy by one quantum" — the algebraic trick that makes QM-5's angular momentum and QC's photons work.

Topics in order: THE INFINITE SQUARE WELL: solve, impose boundary conditions, get k_n = nπ/a and E_n = n²π²ℏ²/2ma² (derive), normalize, the four properties (alternating parity, increasing nodes, orthonormality — prove, completeness — stated, F2's Fourier sine series IS this), the general time-dependent solution with c_n = ⟨ψ_n|Ψ(0)⟩ (Fourier's trick, LA-5), ⟨H⟩ = Σ|c_n|²E_n, a worked example with Ψ(0) ∝ x(a − x) (compute c_n and the probability of E₁ ≈ 0.9986); the "sloshing" superposition and revival times; THE HARMONIC OSCILLATOR, algebraic method: factor Ĥ using a_±, derive [x, p] = iℏ (the canonical commutator from p̂ = −iℏ∂_x, with the test-function argument), derive Ĥ = ℏω(a_+a_− + ½), the ladder property Ĥ(a_±ψ) = (E ± ℏω)(a_±ψ) (prove), the ground state from a_−ψ₀ = 0 (solve the first-order ODE → Gaussian), E_n = (n + ½)ℏω, normalization constants a_+ψ_n = √(n+1)ψ_{n+1}, a_−ψ_n = √n ψ_{n−1} (derive via ⟨ψ_n|a_−a_+|ψ_n⟩ and Hermitian conjugation), the number operator, orthogonality via the ladder (prove ⟨ψ_m|ψ_n⟩ = 0 by the a_+a_− trick), expectation values ⟨x⟩, ⟨p⟩, ⟨x²⟩, ⟨p²⟩, ⟨T⟩ = ⟨V⟩ via ladder operators (derive — the virial theorem for the oscillator, CM-4) and σ_xσ_p = (n + ½)ℏ (derive); the analytic method: the Hermite equation by power series, the termination argument that quantizes E (do it — it's the F2/EM-3 Frobenius argument again, say so), the Hermite polynomials, a plot description of ψ_n and |ψ_n|² vs the classical probability distribution (CM-8's WKB page); why the oscillator matters (CM-6: every normal mode; SM-5: phonons and photons; the ground-state energy ½ℏω as zero-point motion); THE FREE PARTICLE (recap of QM-2; scattering states vs bound states defined); THE DELTA-FUNCTION POTENTIAL V = −αδ(x): the boundary condition on ψ' derived by integrating the Schrödinger equation across the delta (F3: δ as a functional), the single bound state E = −mα²/2ℏ² (derive), scattering: R and T derived, T = 1/(1 + mα²/2ℏ²E), the fact that the barrier (+α) and well (−α) have identical T (and why: the formula depends on α²); THE FINITE SQUARE WELL: bound states by matching at the walls, the even/odd solutions, the transcendental equation tan z = √((z₀/z)² − 1) and its graphical solution (derive), the limits (wide/deep → infinite well; shallow → exactly one bound state always exists in 1D — prove), scattering off the well and the transmission resonances T = 1 at E + V₀ = n²π²ℏ²/2ma² (derive; the Ramsauer–Townsend effect); THE STEP AND THE BARRIER: the potential step (E > V₀: partial reflection — compute R and T with the probability-current definition, derive R + T = 1 and why T ≠ |F/A|² naively — the current, not the amplitude, carries the flux; E < V₀: total reflection with penetration), the RECTANGULAR BARRIER and TUNNELING: derive T = [1 + (V₀² sinh²(κa))/(4E(V₀ − E))]^{−1} fully (the 2×2 matching algebra; present it as a transfer-matrix product — LA-4's composition — so the student sees the method scales), the thick-barrier approximation T ≈ 16(E/V₀)(1 − E/V₀)e^{−2κa} and the exponential sensitivity, the WKB tunneling formula T ≈ e^{−2∫κ dx} stated (QM-8 derives); applications with numbers: alpha decay (Gamow: the half-life range of 10²⁴ from a factor of 2 in energy — do the estimate), the STM (current vs gap distance), field emission; THE TRANSFER MATRIX METHOD generalized to any piecewise-constant potential (derive; the project uses it); THE DOUBLE WELL and tunneling splitting (qualitative with the two lowest states; the ammonia molecule; the energy splitting ∝ e^{−κa} — the model for QC-9's qubits and for the two-level system of QM-4); a page of general 1D theorems: no degeneracy of bound states in 1D (prove), the node theorem (state), parity (prove ψ(−x) are solutions for even V and can be chosen with definite parity); PERIODIC POTENTIALS in one page: Bloch's theorem stated, the Dirac comb's band structure (Griffiths §5.3 material, moved here as the natural end of the 1D story), bands and gaps as the origin of conductors/insulators (SM-5's electron gas refined) — derive the band condition cos(ka) = cos(Ka) + (mα/ℏ²K) sin(Ka) and describe the bands.

Worked examples (≥7): the box with Ψ(0) ∝ x(a − x): c_n, P(E₁); the oscillator's ⟨x²⟩ and σ_xσ_p in state n via ladders; the Hermite-series termination for n = 2 explicitly; the delta well's bound state and the transmission at E = |E_bound|; the finite well with z₀ = 8: the bound-state energies from the transcendental equation (numerically solved, numbers quoted); the barrier: T for an electron with E = 5 eV, V₀ = 10 eV, a = 0.5 nm, and the change when a → 1 nm; the step with E = 2V₀: R and T via currents.

Project: a finite-difference eigenvalue solver for the 1D time-independent Schrödinger equation (LA-8/13: the tridiagonal Hamiltonian is symmetric; eigh) — infinite well, oscillator, finite well, double well: eigenvalues vs analytic/transcendental (assert), eigenfunctions plotted with the potential; the oscillator ladder operators as matrices in the number basis (assert [a_−, a_+] = 1, Ĥ diagonal, the √n factors) and a coherent-state time evolution as the QC-9/optics preview; the Hermite polynomials via recurrence vs scipy.special.hermite; the finite-well transcendental equation solved graphically and numerically; the transfer-matrix scattering code for arbitrary piecewise-constant V: T(E) for the barrier vs the closed form (assert), resonances for the well, the double barrier (resonant tunneling peaks), and the Dirac comb's band structure (T(E) showing bands as transmission windows; the Bloch condition plotted); the tunneling exponential: ln T vs barrier width (slope −2κ, assert); a wave packet (QM-2's split-operator solver) hitting a barrier: reflected and transmitted parts with the integrated probabilities vs the stationary T(E) averaged over the packet's energy distribution; Gamow's alpha-decay estimate as a function of energy (the 10²⁴ range reproduced). "Your turn": the half-oscillator (infinite wall at x = 0); a triangular well (the Airy function appears — scipy.special.airy); the two-delta double well's splitting vs separation.

§8 honest: the oscillator ladder algebra is the most important technique in the module and feels like a trick the first time — redo it until a_±ψ_n's normalization is automatic; the finite-well transcendental equation can't be solved in closed form and students waste hours trying; "T = |F/A|²" is wrong when the wave speeds differ (the step) — use currents; the barrier matching algebra is where arithmetic errors live, which is why the transfer matrix is taught; the Frobenius termination argument quantizes energy for the oscillator and ℓ for Legendre (EM-3) and the student should notice it's the same argument; Bloch's theorem is stated, not proved — it's QM-4's symmetry story applied to translations.
