# Brief: ST-4 — Generalized linear models: exponential families, link functions, logistic and Poisson regression, IRLS, deviance, overdispersion
Folder: curriculum/03-statistical-modeling/04-generalized-linear-models/ · ~12 hours · prereq ST-3 (and ST-1's exponential-family section) · unlocks ST-5, ST-9, ML-3
Pair with: Agresti *Foundations of Linear and GLMs* ch. 4–7; McCullagh & Nelder *Generalized Linear Models* ch. 2 (the original; dense); Stanford STATS 305B; Dobson & Barnett *An Introduction to GLMs* for a gentler route; Gelman, Hill & Vehtari ch. 13–15 for applied logistic regression.
Conventions: response y_i with mean μ_i; linear predictor η_i = x_iᵀβ; link g(μ) = η; canonical link when η = the natural parameter; exponential dispersion family f(y; θ, φ) = exp[(yθ − b(θ))/a(φ) + c(y, φ)] with μ = b'(θ), Var(y) = a(φ)b''(θ) = a(φ)V(μ); deviance D = 2[ℓ(saturated) − ℓ(model)]; IRLS = iteratively reweighted least squares; working weights W, working response z.

§1: Fisher (1935) fit a probit by hand with iterative weighting; Berkson (1944) proposed the logit; Nelder and Wedderburn (1972) saw that logistic, Poisson, Gamma, and Normal regression are one algorithm applied to one family of distributions, and that the algorithm is weighted least squares repeated — the GLM was born as a unification, not an invention; the forcing problem: linear regression's assumptions (Normal errors, constant variance, unbounded response) are violated by binary outcomes, counts, and positive skewed quantities, and ad hoc transformations (arcsine, log) fix some symptoms badly; a principled generalization needed a family of distributions with a shared structure — the exponential family of ST-1. §2: a GLM is a regression on a transformed mean: the link function maps the mean's natural range (a probability in (0,1), a rate in (0,∞)) onto the whole real line where a linear predictor lives; the variance is tied to the mean by the distribution (V(μ)), so observations are automatically weighted; fitting is Newton's method (LA-14), and each Newton step is a weighted least squares problem (ST-3) — so everything from ST-3 (leverage, residuals, projections) survives with weights; the deviance is the generalization of RSS.

Topics in order: why linear regression fails for binary and count data (predictions outside range, heteroscedasticity by construction, non-normal errors — demonstrate each); THE EXPONENTIAL DISPERSION FAMILY (ST-1's exponential family with a dispersion parameter): the form, the mean and variance identities μ = b'(θ) and Var = a(φ)b''(θ) (derive via the score identities of ST-1), the variance function V(μ), and the table: Normal (b = θ²/2, V = 1), Bernoulli/Binomial (b = log(1 + e^θ), V = μ(1−μ)), Poisson (b = e^θ, V = μ), Gamma (b = −log(−θ), V = μ²), inverse Gaussian — derive each b, μ(θ), V(μ); THE THREE COMPONENTS of a GLM (random component, linear predictor, link); the CANONICAL LINK (θ = η; logit for Bernoulli, log for Poisson, identity for Normal, inverse for Gamma — derive from the table) and why it's special (the sufficient statistic is Xᵀy;
