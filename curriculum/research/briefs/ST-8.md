# Brief: ST-8 — Time series II: spectral analysis, state-space models and the Kalman filter, volatility models
Folder: curriculum/03-statistical-modeling/08-time-series-2/ · ~12 hours · prereqs ST-7, F3 (Fourier transform, DFT, periodogram basics), F5 (MVN conditionals), LA-10 · unlocks ML-4 (Gaussian processes as state-space models), control-theory pointers for an ME
Pair with: Shumway & Stoffer ch. 4 (spectral) and 6 (state space); Brockwell & Davis ch. 4, 8; Durbin & Koopman *Time Series Analysis by State Space Methods* ch. 2–4 (the Kalman filter derivations); Särkkä *Bayesian Filtering and Smoothing* (free PDF) ch. 4–8; Welch & Bishop, "An Introduction to the Kalman Filter" (free); Tsay *Analysis of Financial Time Series* ch. 3 for GARCH.
Conventions: spectral density f(ω) = Σ_h γ(h)e^{−2πiωh} for ω ∈ [−½, ½] (cycles per sample; F3's convention), so γ(h) = ∫f(ω)e^{2πiωh}dω; periodogram I(ω_j) = |Σx_te^{−2πiω_jt}|²/n at Fourier frequencies ω_j = j/n; state-space model: state α_t, observation y_t, α_{t+1} = Tα_t + Rη_t (η ~ N(0,Q)), y_t = Zα_t + ε_t (ε ~ N(0,H)); filtered mean a_{t|t}, covariance P_{t|t}; predicted a_{t+1|t}, P_{t+1|t}; Kalman gain K_t; innovations v_t = y_t − Za_{t|t−1} with variance F_t; GARCH(1,1): σ_t² = ω + αε_{t−1}² + βσ_{t−1}².

§1: Schuster (1898) invented the periodogram to look for hidden periodicities in sunspots and earthquakes; Wiener (1930) and Khinchin (1934) proved that the autocovariance and the spectral density are a Fourier pair, so ST-7's time-domain theory and the frequency domain are the same theory; Tukey (1950s–60s, with Blackman) made spectral estimation practical and co-invented the FFT (F3); Kalman (1960) solved the filtering problem recursively and the Apollo guidance computer used it to reach the moon; Engle (1982) invented ARCH to model the fact that financial volatility clusters, and got a Nobel for it. The forcing problem: (i) oscillatory data (vibrations, tides, EEG, business cycles) is naturally described by frequency content, and the ACF hides it; (ii) ST-7's ARMA models can't handle missing data, time-varying parameters, or unobserved components (a true position behind noisy sensor readings), and the general tool for "estimate a hidden state from noisy observations" is the state-space model with the Kalman filter — which for an ME student is the core of navigation, control, and sensor fusion; (iii) ST-7 modeled the mean's dynamics and assumed constant variance; financial returns have a nearly unpredictable mean and a highly predictable variance. §2: the spectral density is the ACF's Fourier transform (F3), and tells you how the variance is distributed over frequencies — a sharp peak is a periodicity, white noise is flat, AR(1) is low-pass; the periodogram is the DFT's squared magnitude and is a noisy estimate that must be smoothed; the Kalman filter is F5's Gaussian conditioning formula applied recursively — predict with the dynamics, then correct with the observation, weighting by relative uncertainty (the gain) — and it is exactly the posterior of ST-5 updated one observation at a time; GARCH is an ARMA model for the squared residuals.

§8 honest: the spectral density is a Fourier series of the ACF (not a transform), and the F3 convention table must be in front of you — every factor of 2π error lives here; the periodogram is inconsistent and almost everyone has computed one and read structure into its noise; the Kalman filter is F5's conditioning formula and students who derive it the third time finally see that; the gain's interpretation as relative precision is the whole filter; covariance updates lose symmetry/positive-definiteness in floating point (LA-13) and real implementations use square-root forms; the state-space form of ARMA has several equivalent conventions and books disagree; GARCH's QML standard errors need the sandwich (ST-3) when errors aren't Normal; "volatility" is latent and never observed, so model checks are indirect.
