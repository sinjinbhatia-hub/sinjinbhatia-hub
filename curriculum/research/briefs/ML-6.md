Conventions: data X (n×d), centered; cluster assignments z_i ∈ {1..K}, centroids μ_k; GMM with weights π_k, means μ_k, covariances Σ_k; responsibilities γ_ik; complete-data log-likelihood; the ELBO/free energy F(q, θ); principal components v_j (right singular vectors of centered X), scores Xv_j, explained variance σ_j²/(n−1); graph Laplacian L = D − W, normalized L_sym = I − D^{−1/2}WD^{−1/2}; the Fiedler vector.

§1: Pearson (1901) invented PCA for fitting lines and planes to points; Hotelling (1933) named it; Lloyd (1957, at Bell Labs, for pulse-code modulation) wrote the k-means algorithm; Dempster, Laird and Rubin (1977) unified a dozen iterative algorithms as EM and proved its monotonicity; Fiedler (1973) connected a graph's second eigenvector to its connectivity, and Shi & Malik (2000) made spectral clustering an image-segmentation tool; Tenenbaum (Isomap, 2000) and Roweis & Saul (LLE, 2000) launched manifold learning; van der Maaten & Hinton's t-SNE (2008) and McInnes' UMAP (2018) became the default visualizers. The forcing problem: most data has no labels, and the questions are "what groups are there", "what are the important directions", and "what does this high-dimensional data actually look like" — and the curse of dimensionality (ML-1) means the answers must find low-dimensional structure that is only visible after the right transformation. §2: k-means is "guess centers, assign points, move centers to the mean, repeat", and it's coordinate descent on a sum of squared distances; a Gaussian mixture is k-means with soft assignments and shapes, and EM is the same two steps on expected log-likelihoods; PCA finds the directions of maximum variance — LA-9's right singular vectors — and the best low-rank approximation (Eckart–Young); spectral clustering cuts a graph where it's thin, which is the small eigenvalues of the Laplacian (CM-6's spring network!); manifold methods assume the data lies on a curved low-dimensional surface and try to unroll it.

§8 honest: k-means converges to a local minimum and the only defense is restarts — students run it once; EM is derived for GMMs by every course and understood by few because the general bound (log p = F + KL) is skipped — here it's the center, and it's the same identity as ST-6/SM-7's; PCA on unstandardized data is PCA on units, not on structure; "the first two PCs explain 40% of the variance" is rarely the useful statement; the Laplacian derivation's relaxation step (indicator → real vector) is where the method becomes heuristic, and the CM-6 picture is what makes the eigenvectors meaningful; t-SNE is a visualization tool that people read as a measurement, and the controlled demos exist to break that habit; every clustering algorithm finds clusters, including in noise.
