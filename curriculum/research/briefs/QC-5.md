# Brief: QC-5 — The quantum Fourier transform and Shor's algorithm: QFT, phase estimation, order finding, factoring, RSA
Folder: curriculum/05-quantum-computing/05-qft-phase-estimation-shor/ · ~14 hours · prereqs QC-4 (Fourier sampling over Z₂ⁿ, hidden subgroups), F3 (DFT, FFT, unitarity of F/√N, roots of unity), QC-2 (controlled-U, phase kickback, universality) · unlocks QC-9 (phase estimation for energies), the cryptography context
Pair with: Mermin ch. 3; Nielsen & Chuang ch. 5 and Appendix 4 (number theory) and 5 (RSA); Preskill ch. 6; Shor (1994/1997) "Polynomial-time algorithms for prime factorization and discrete logarithms on a quantum computer"; Kitaev (1995) for phase estimation; Gidney & Ekerå (2021) for the modern resource estimate (20 million noisy qubits for RSA-2048) and Gidney's 2025 update (under one million) as the current context; Hardy & Wright for continued fractions (or any number-theory text).
Conventions: N = 2ⁿ for the QFT size on n qubits; QFT|j⟩ = N^{−1/2}Σ_k e^{2πijk/N}|k⟩ (note the + sign: the QFT is the INVERSE DFT of F3's NumPy convention up to normalization — state the correspondence and the reason, and stick to it); binary fractions 0.j₁j₂…j_m = Σj_l2^{−l}; controlled phase gates R_k = diag(1, e^{2πi/2^k}); phase estimation with t counting qubits estimating φ in U|u⟩ = e^{2πiφ}|u⟩; modular exponentiation U_a|y⟩ = |ay mod M⟩ for the number M to be factored; the order r of a mod M; gcd; continued-fraction convergents p/q.

§1: Shor (1994) found that factoring — the problem RSA's security rests on — reduces to period finding, and that period finding is a hidden-subgroup problem over Z that a quantum Fourier transform solves in polynomial time; the result made quantum computing a field, a funding priority, and a cryptographic threat (NIST's post-quantum standards, 2024); Coppersmith (1994) gave the efficient QFT circuit; Kitaev (1995) reframed the core as phase estimation, which became the subroutine behind quantum chemistry (QC-9) and HHL; the largest number honestly factored by Shor's algorithm on hardware is still tiny (21, with compiled circuits that cheat — say so), and the resource estimates for RSA-2048 have fallen from billions to under a million physical qubits. The forcing problem: QC-4's Fourier sampling worked over Z₂ⁿ; integers have a richer group structure (Z_N), and periodicity in Z_N — "f(x + r) = f(x)" — is exactly what number theory needs for factoring; the DFT of F3 detects periods, the FFT computes it in N log N classically, and the QFT does it in (log N)² quantum gates on an exponentially large vector. §2: the QFT is F3's DFT applied to the amplitude vector; it maps a periodic state (amplitude on x₀, x₀ + r, x₀ + 2r, …) to a state concentrated near multiples of N/r (F3: the DFT of a comb is a comb); phase estimation is "put an eigenvector in, let the controlled-U powers kick back phases 2πφ·2^k onto a counting register, and inverse-QFT reads off φ in binary" (QC-2's phase kickback, iterated); Shor's algorithm is phase estimation on the modular-multiplication unitary, whose eigenphases are s/r, plus continued fractions to recover r, plus a classical reduction from factoring to order finding.

Topics in order: THE QFT: definition, unitarity (F3's proof of F/√N unitary; restate), the product representation QFT|j₁…j_n⟩ = N^{−1/2}(|0⟩ + e^{2πi0.j_n}|1⟩)⊗(|0⟩ + e^{2πi0.j_{n−1}j_n}|1⟩)⊗…⊗(|0⟩ + e^{2πi0.j₁…j_n}|1⟩) — DERIVE it completely (write k in binary, factor the exponential, the integer parts of the phases vanish), the circuit read off from the product form (a Hadamard then controlled-R_k's on each qubit, then a reversal of qubit order — derive why the reversal is needed), gate count n(n+1)/2 + ⌊n/2⌋ swaps = O(n²) (derive), vs the classical FFT's O(N log N) = O(n2ⁿ) operations (F3), and the HONEST CAVEAT: the QFT cannot be used to compute a classical Fourier transform faster, because the output amplitudes cannot be read out — only sampled; approximate QFT (drop R_k for large k; the error bound O(n²/2^m) for keeping m levels — state, explain why tiny rotations barely matter); the QFT on a periodic state: the comb-to-comb property (derive: for amplitudes on x₀ + jr, j = 0..A−1 with r | N, the QFT gives amplitude only on multiples of N/r, with phases from x₀ — F3's Poisson-summation/comb fact in discrete form), and the non-dividing case (r ∤ N: peaks near multiples of N/r;
