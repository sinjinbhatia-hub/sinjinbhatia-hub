Conventions: data x, latent z; encoder q_φ(z|x), decoder p_θ(x|z), prior p(z) = N(0, I); ELBO(θ, φ) = E_q[log p_θ(x|z)] − KL(q_φ(z|x)‖p(z)); the reparametrization z = μ + σ ⊙ ε; diffusion forward process q(x_t|x_{t−1}) = N(√(1−β_t)x_{t−1}, β_tI), α_t = 1 − β_t, ᾱ_t = Πα_s; the noise-prediction network ε_θ(x_t, t); score s(x) = ∇_x log p(x); energy E_θ(x) with p = e^{−E}/Z; PAC: (ε, δ); VC dimension d_VC; Rademacher complexity named.

§1: the generative dream — a model that can produce new data like the data it saw — goes back to Boltzmann machines (SM-7; Hinton 1985); Kingma & Welling and Rezende et al. (2013–14) made it tractable with the variational autoencoder, which is ST-6's variational inference with a neural network for q and the reparametrization trick for gradients; Goodfellow et al. (2014) found the adversarial game; Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015) borrowed non-equilibrium thermodynamics (SM-7) to invent diffusion models, which Ho et al. (2020) and Song et al. (2021) made the state of the art for images and audio; on the theory side, Valiant (1984) defined learnability, Vapnik and Chervonenkis gave the capacity measure, and Zhang et al. (2017) showed that deep networks memorize random labels perfectly and still generalize on real data — so the classical theory doesn't explain deep learning, and the module ends on that honest note. The forcing problem: discriminative models (ML-3 to ML-9) learn p(y|x); modeling p(x) itself lets you generate, detect anomalies, fill in missing data, and learn representations without labels — but p(x) over images has a normalizing constant no one can compute (SM-4's Z), so every generative method is a way around Z: variational bounds (VAE), adversarial games (GAN), invertible maps (flows), or score functions that never need Z (diffusion/EBMs). §2: a VAE is an autoencoder whose code is a probability distribution, trained to reconstruct well while keeping codes near a simple prior — and the training objective is ST-6's ELBO, which is SM-7's negative variational free energy; a diffusion model learns to reverse the process of gradually adding noise, and the reverse step needs only the score (the gradient of the log density — SM-7's Langevin force), which can be learned by denoising — no Z anywhere; an energy-based model is SM-4's Boltzmann distribution with a learned energy, trained by SM-7's "data minus model" gradient, sampled by Langevin; learning theory asks how much data guarantees generalization, and the answer (VC dimension) is right for simple classes and uninformative for deep nets.
