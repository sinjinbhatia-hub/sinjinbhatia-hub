# Brief: ST-6 — Bayesian computation: Metropolis–Hastings, Gibbs sampling, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, diagnostics, hierarchical models, variational inference
Folder: curriculum/03-statistical-modeling/06-bayesian-computation/ · ~14 hours · prereqs ST-5, F5 (Markov chains, detailed balance), CM-8 (Hamiltonian dynamics, Liouville, symplectic integrators) for HMC · unlocks SM-7, ML-10, ST-8

Conventions: target density π(θ) ∝ p(y|θ)p(θ) (unnormalized is fine); proposal q(θ'|θ); acceptance probability α = min(1, π(θ')q(θ|θ')/(π(θ)q(θ'|θ)); chain θ^{(1)}, …, θ^{(S)}; warm-up/burn-in; thinning; effective sample size n_eff; R̂; HMC with position θ, momentum ρ, Hamiltonian H = −log π(θ) + ½ρᵀM⁻¹ρ, leapfrog step ε, L steps; variational family q_φ, ELBO.

§1: Metropolis et al. (1953, SM-6) sampled the Boltzmann distribution of a hard-sphere gas on the MANIAC; Hastings (1970) generalized it; Geman and Geman (1984) invented Gibbs sampling for image restoration; Duane et al. (1987) invented hybrid/Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for lattice QCD; Gelfand and Smith (1990) showed statisticians that these physics algorithms solved the Bayesian computation problem, and the field exploded; Stan (2012) made HMC the default; variational inference came from physics too (mean-field, SM-6/SM-7) via Jordan, Hinton, and Blei. The forcing problem: ST-5's conjugate models are the only ones with closed-form posteriors; every realistic model (logistic regression, hierarchical models, anything with more than a few parameters) has a posterior known only up to a normalizing constant in a high-dimensional space, and the only general methods are to sample from it (MCMC) or to approximate it with a tractable family (VI). §2: MCMC is a random walk designed so that the fraction of time spent near θ equals the posterior density there — F5's detailed balance is the design principle, SM-6's Metropolis is the first instance; Gibbs updates one coordinate at a time from its exact conditional; HMC gives the walker momentum and lets it coast along contours of the posterior using CM-8's Hamiltonian dynamics, so it explores far instead of diffusing; diagnostics ask whether the walker has forgotten where it started and how many effectively independent draws you have; hierarchical models are the reason you need all this — they have one parameter per group plus shared hyperparameters, and their posteriors are both high-dimensional and shaped like funnels; variational inference replaces sampling with optimization: find the closest tractable distribution, where "closest" is KL (F5).

Topics in order: why sampling solves the problem (Monte Carlo estimates of posterior expectations, credible intervals, and predictive distributions from draws; the normalizing constant cancels); rejection and importance sampling in one page (their failure in high dimensions — the curse of dimensionality for a Gaussian in d dimensions quantified); SM-6), random-walk Metropolis with a Gaussian proposal, the independence sampler, the proposal-scale trade-off (too small: slow diffusion; too large: rejections) with the optimal acceptance rate ~0.234 in high dimensions stated (Roberts–Gelman–Gilks) and the ~0.44 for one dimension, adaptive scaling during warm-up; GIBBS SAMPLING: the algorithm (sample each θ_j from p(θ_j|θ_{−j}, y)), the proof that it's a Metropolis–Hastings sampler with acceptance 1 (derive), when the full conditionals are available (conjugate pieces inside a non-conjugate whole — the Normal with unknown mean and variance done as a Gibbs sampler; §8 honest: detailed balance is one line and the whole subject — if F5's Markov-chain section is shaky, redo it; students think a sampler that "runs" is a sampler that "works", and the diagnostics section exists to kill that; HMC requires CM-8, genuinely — the leapfrog's volume preservation and reversibility are the whole argument; the funnel in hierarchical models is not a toy problem, it's the normal case, and the non-centered trick is mandatory knowledge; VI is fast and biased, MCMC is slow and exact in the limit, and knowing which bias you can tolerate is the craft; R̂ ≈ 1 does not prove convergence, it fails to disprove it; "burn-in" and "thinning" are folklore with partial justification; implementing these samplers once from scratch is the point — using Stan afterwards is fine, using it before is not.
