# Brief: EM-5 — Magnetostatics: Biot–Savart, Ampère, the vector potential, magnetic materials
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/b-electromagnetism/05-magnetostatics/ · ~12 hours · prereq EM-4 · unlocks EM-6
Pair with: Griffiths ch. 5–6; Purcell & Morin ch. 6, 11 (the relativistic origin of magnetism — read for intuition); MIT 8.07 notes lectures 10–14.
Conventions: SI; μ₀; B in tesla; current density J; magnetization M; H = B/μ₀ − M; bound currents J_b = ∇×M, K_b = M × n̂; Coulomb gauge ∇·A = 0; Lorentz force F = q(E + v × B).

§1: Oersted (1820) saw a compass needle move near a current; within weeks Ampère had the force law between currents and Biot and Savart the field law; Faraday's induction (1831) and Maxwell's displacement current (1861) came later (EM-6); the vector potential was Maxwell's "electrotonic state", despised as unphysical for a century until Aharonov–Bohm (1959) showed quantum mechanics sees it — QM-4/QC hardware context; the forcing problem: magnetic forces are velocity-dependent and do no work, the field lines never end, and no magnetic charge exists — a fundamentally different structure from electrostatics that needed its own potential (a vector) and its own integral theorem (Ampère's). §2: B curls around currents the way E diverges from charges; the right-hand rule; magnetic field lines are closed loops (∇·B = 0); the vector potential is to B what the scalar potential is to E, except the roles of div and curl swap; magnetization is to bound currents what polarization is to bound charges.

the infinite limit μ₀nI); force between parallel wires (derive, with the definition of the ampere); THE DIVERGENCE AND CURL OF B: derive ∇·B = 0 and ∇×B = μ₀J from Biot–Savart (the computation with the product rules of EM-1 — do it fully, including the surface term that vanishes and the delta function that appears);
