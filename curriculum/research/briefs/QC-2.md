# Brief: QC-2 — Many qubits: tensor products, entanglement, CNOT, universal gate sets, the circuit model, no-cloning
Folder: curriculum/05-quantum-computing/02-many-qubits-and-universal-gates/ · ~10 hours · prereqs QC-1, LA-12 (tensor products, Kronecker products, Schmidt decomposition), QM-7 (entanglement, Bell states) · unlocks QC-3 through QC-9
Pair with: Mermin ch. 1–2; Nielsen & Chuang ch. 4 (quantum circuits) and §2.2.8; Preskill ch. 2 and 6; Barenco et al., "Elementary gates for quantum computation" (1995); Kitaev, Shen & Vyalyi for the Solovay–Kitaev theorem (optional).
Conventions: n qubits live in (C²)^⊗n of dimension 2ⁿ; |x⟩ for x ∈ {0,1}ⁿ with QUBIT 0 THE LEFTMOST factor (so |q₀q₁…q_{n−1}⟩, and the integer label x = Σq_i2^{n−1−i} — big-endian; state this in the box, and warn that Qiskit is little-endian); Kronecker product ⊗ (np.kron order matches); CNOT with control c and target t; the Bell states Φ±, Ψ±; the SWAP gate; the Toffoli (CCNOT); gate counts and depth; universal set {H, T, CNOT}; Clifford set {H, S, CNOT}.

§1: Deutsch (1985) and Barenco et al. (1995) worked out which gate sets suffice; Wootters and Zurek, and Dieks (1982) proved no-cloning; Bennett and others realized entanglement is a resource; the forcing problem: QC-1's single qubit is a classical coin with extra bookkeeping; the exponential state space (2ⁿ amplitudes for n qubits) and the entangled states that fill most of it are what make quantum computation different from classical, and the module's job is to make 2ⁿ-dimensional spaces concrete enough to compute in by hand for n = 2, 3 and by code for n = 10–20. §2: two qubits' state space is the tensor product (LA-12) — 4 amplitudes, not 2 + 2; a product state is a rank-1 coefficient matrix, an entangled state isn't (LA-12's Schmidt decomposition = QM-7); a gate on one qubit of two is I ⊗ U; CNOT is the controlled bit flip and the thing that creates entanglement from products; a quantum circuit is a sequence of such gates on a 2ⁿ-vector, and the "exponential speedup" question is whether the final measurement statistics answer something useful faster than classical algorithms could.
