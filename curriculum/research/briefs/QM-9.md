# Brief: QM-9 — Symmetry, the classical limit, and path integrals: Ehrenfest, Noether in QM, Feynman's sum over paths, decoherence

Pair with: Shankar ch. 8 (path integrals) and 11 (symmetries); Feynman & Hibbs *Quantum Mechanics and Path Integrals* ch. 1–3; Feynman's *QED* (popular, but the path-integral chapter is exact); Zurek, "Decoherence and the transition from quantum to classical — revisited" (free, arXiv quant-ph/0306072); Schlosshauer's review (free); Sakurai & Napolitano ch. 2.6, 4.
Conventions: the propagator K(x_b, t_b; x_a, t_a) = ⟨x_b|e^{−iĤ(t_b − t_a)/ℏ}|x_a⟩; the action S[x(t)] = ∫L dt (CM-2); Feynman's measure 𝒟x; the reduced density matrix ρ̂_S = Tr_E ρ̂; decoherence time τ_D; symmetry operators Û commuting with Ĥ.

§1: three loose ends from the course get tied. (i) Symmetry: Wigner (1931) showed that every symmetry is a unitary (or antiunitary) operator and that group representations organize spectra — QM-5's ladder was the SU(2) case; Noether's theorem (CM-4) becomes a one-line statement about commutators. (ii) The classical limit: Ehrenfest (1927) showed averages obey Newton; the WKB/Hamilton–Jacobi link (QM-2, QM-8) showed ℏ → 0 gives classical mechanics; Feynman (1948) found the formulation where the classical path is literally where the phases add up — the sum over histories, which became the language of QFT and of statistical mechanics (the imaginary-time trick that turns it into SM-4's partition function — the deepest row of the SM-7 dictionary). (iii) Measurement: why don't we see superpositions of macroscopic objects? Zeh (1970), Zurek (1981): the environment measures everything constantly; interference terms are destroyed at rates that scale with size; the classical world is the decohered reduced description. None of this is settled philosophy, but all of it is physics with numbers, and the module gives the numbers. §2: symmetry → conserved generator (CM-4 with hats); the classical limit as "the phase e^{iS/ℏ} oscillates so fast off the stationary path that only the neighborhood of δS = 0 survives" — the stationary-phase picture of F3 made into physics; decoherence as "entangle with the environment, trace it out, watch the off-diagonals die" — QM-7's last example made quantitative.
