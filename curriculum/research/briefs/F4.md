# Brief: F4 — Probability I: foundations, random variables, named distributions, expectation
Folder: curriculum/00-foundations/04-probability-1/ · ~14 hours · prereq F1 · unlocks F5, SM-3, ST-1
Pair with: Blitzstein & Hwang *Introduction to Probability* 2nd ed. (free) ch. 1–6; Stat 110 lectures 1–15; Ross for drills.

Blitzstein style: every named distribution gets a story, PMF/PDF derived from the story, E and Var derived. §1: Pascal–Fermat 1654 problem of points; Bernoulli's LLN 1713; Laplace; frequentist vs degree-of-belief; Kolmogorov 1933; gambling → insurance → physics (Maxwell 1860, Boltzmann 1877) → inference. §2: probability as area/mass; conditioning = restrict and renormalize; a random variable is a function; E[X] is the centroid, Var(X) the second moment about the centroid — make the ME analogy explicit.

Topics in order: sample spaces, events, set ops as logic; counting: multiplication rule, permutations, binomial coefficients, binomial theorem (prove), story proofs for C(n,k)=C(n,n−k), Vandermonde, hockey stick (count two ways), sampling table; naive probability; birthday problem derived (+ e^{−x} approximation); Kolmogorov axioms and derived properties (complement, monotonicity, inclusion–exclusion for 2 and 3, general stated; de Montmort matching → 1 − 1/e); conditional probability, chain rule, BAYES derived, total probability, extra-conditioning versions; independence (pairwise vs mutual; vs disjoint), conditional independence ≠ independence (two-coins example); the traps with full solutions: Monty Hall (Bayes and strategy conditioning), base-rate fallacy (99% test, 1-in-10,000 disease → posterior ≈ 1%), two-children, Simpson's paradox table, prosecutor's fallacy; random variables as functions; PMF, CDF, support; NAMED DISCRETE with story → PMF → E, Var: Bernoulli, Binomial (E via indicators AND PMF; Var via independence), Geometric (E by series AND first-step conditioning), Negative Binomial, Hypergeometric (E via indicators; finite-population correction), Poisson (DERIVE as Binomial(n, λ/n) limit; Poisson paradigm; E = Var = λ), Discrete Uniform; EXPECTATION: definition, LINEARITY proved (no independence needed), fundamental bridge E[I_A] = P(A), indicator trick ×3 (expected fixed points of a random permutation = 1; birthday matches; Binomial mean), LOTUS (prove discrete), Var = E[X²] − (EX)², Var(aX+b), variance of independent sums (covariance introduced minimally), st. Petersburg; CONTINUOUS: PDF, CDF, P(X=x)=0, "density is not probability", Uniform, UNIVERSALITY OF THE UNIFORM (F^{−1}(U) ~ F and F(X) ~ Unif, both proved; inverse-CDF sampling), Exponential (memorylessness derived AND characterizes it via g(s+t) = g(s)g(t)), Exponential ↔ Poisson process (state; F5), NORMAL: standard density, DERIVE the normalizing constant by polar coordinates, E = 0, Var = 1 by parts, N(μ,σ²) by location-scale, standardization, 68–95–99.7, CLT preview, Φ has no closed form; Gamma and Beta introduced (densities, Γ(n) = (n−1)! proved, Γ(½) = √π), full treatment in F5; MGFs: definition, M^{(n)}(0) = E[X^n], MGFs of Bernoulli/Binomial/Poisson/Normal/Exponential, product rule for independent sums, uniqueness stated, Binomial+Binomial via MGF, Poisson limit via MGF; a boxed "how to solve a probability problem".

Worked examples (≥7): birthday with 23 exact and approximate; medical-test Bayes; Monty Hall two ways; matching via inclusion–exclusion; expected fixed points via indicators; Poisson limit numerically n = 1000, p = 0.003 vs Poisson(3), k = 0..5; inverse-CDF for Exponential (X = −ln(1−U)/λ); the Gaussian integral; an ME example (defective parts: Hypergeometric vs Binomial; or Exponential time-to-failure) with a number.

Project: Monte Carlo for birthday (curve vs exact), Monty Hall (10^5 trials, assert within 3σ of 2/3 and 1/3), matching (mean fixed points → 1; P(none) → 1/e), base-rate example; exact PMFs from formulas asserted vs scipy.stats; empirical histograms vs PMFs; Poisson approximation error vs n; inverse-CDF sampling for Exponential and a custom piecewise CDF with ECDF overlay and a loose kstest assertion; Gaussian constant by numerical integration; 68–95–99.7 check; sample-MGF finite-difference moments for a Poisson; running means for Bernoulli (LLN) and Cauchy (fails); linearity of expectation on dependent RVs. "Your turn": two-children both phrasings; P(birthdays within 1 day); coupon collector via indicators/geometrics.

§8 honest: conditional-probability intuition is broken in everyone; independent vs disjoint; RV is a function; density vs probability; the Normal integral trick must be memorized; MGFs look like busywork until F5's CLT proof; counting problems stay hard.
