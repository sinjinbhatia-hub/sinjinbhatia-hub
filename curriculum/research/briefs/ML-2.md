# Brief: ML-2 — Optimization for machine learning: convexity, gradient descent with proofs, SGD, momentum, Adam, Newton, Lagrangian duality and KKT
Folder: curriculum/04-machine-learning/02-optimization/ · ~14 hours · prereqs LA-14 (gradients, Hessians, chain rule), LA-8 (spectral theorem, PSD), CM-3 (Lagrange multipliers) · unlocks ML-3, ML-4, ML-7
Pair with: Boyd & Vandenberghe *Convex Optimization* (free) ch. 2–5 and 9; Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong *Mathematics for Machine Learning* (free) ch. 7; Bubeck *Convex Optimization: Algorithms and Complexity* (free) ch. 1, 3, 6 for the proofs; Nocedal & Wright ch. 2–3; Ruder, "An overview of gradient descent optimization algorithms" (blog) for the adaptive methods; Goodfellow et al. ch. 8.
Conventions: minimize f(θ), θ ∈ R^d; gradient ∇f, Hessian H; L-smooth: ‖∇f(x) − ∇f(y)‖ ≤ L‖x − y‖ (equivalently H ⪯ LI); μ-strongly convex: H ⪰ μI; condition number κ = L/μ; step size η; iterates θ_t; stochastic gradient g_t with E[g_t] = ∇f(θ_t); momentum β; Adam's (β₁, β₂, ε); Lagrangian L(x, λ, ν) = f₀ + Σλ_if_i + Σν_ih_i; dual function g(λ, ν) = inf_x L.

§1: Cauchy (1847) proposed gradient descent for astronomical calculations; Newton's method is older still; Robbins and Monro (1951) invented stochastic approximation; Polyak (1964) found the heavy-ball momentum method and Nesterov (1983) the accelerated version with its surprising √κ rate; Karush (1939), Kuhn and Tucker (1951) gave the conditions for constrained optima; Boyd's course (2000s) made convex optimization an engineering tool; Kingma and Ba's Adam (2014) became the default for deep learning. The forcing problem: every learning algorithm from ML-3 onward is "minimize a loss over parameters", with d from thousands to billions and n too large to touch every example per step; understanding which problems are easy (convex), how fast each method converges, and why stochastic methods win at scale is what separates tuning from guessing. §2: a convex function has a single valley, and gradient descent walks downhill with a step size set by the steepest curvature (L); the condition number κ is the ratio of the steepest to the gentlest curvature (LA-8/9's eigenvalues of the Hessian), and it controls how badly gradient descent zigzags along a narrow valley; momentum is a heavy ball rolling down (CM-1's dynamics with friction); SGD trades exact gradients for cheap noisy ones and the noise acts like temperature (SM-7); Newton's method rescales the valley to a bowl using the Hessian; Lagrange multipliers (CM-3) turn constraints into prices, and duality says the price-setting problem is a lower bound that is often tight.

§8 honest: the convergence proofs are short and nobody reads them, and then nobody knows why their learning rate must be < 2/L; κ is the single number that explains most optimization pain and standardizing features is the cheapest fix; momentum's √κ is magic until you do the 2×2 eigenvalue computation; SGD's noise ball means you never converge with a constant step and that's fine; Adam is a default, not a theorem, and its convergence proofs are weaker than its reputation; AdamW vs L2 matters and few know why; KKT's complementary slackness is the thing students skip and then can't identify support vectors; the SVM dual derivation is long and is done here so ML-4 can spend its time on kernels.
