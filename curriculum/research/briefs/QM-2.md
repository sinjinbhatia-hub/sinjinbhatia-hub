# Brief: QM-2 — The wavefunction and the Schrödinger equation: probability, operators, expectation values, uncertainty, momentum space
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/d-quantum-mechanics/02-wavefunction-schrodinger/ · ~12 hours · prereqs QM-1, F3 (Fourier transform, Gaussian, uncertainty principle) · unlocks QM-3
Pair with: Griffiths & Schroeter ch. 1; MIT 8.04 (Zwiebach) lectures 1–6 and his *Mastering Quantum Mechanics* ch. 3–5; Shankar ch. 4 (for the formal view, optional now); Feynman Lectures III ch. 16.
Conventions: ℏ explicit; Ψ(x,t) with |Ψ|² dx the probability of finding the particle in dx; normalization ∫|Ψ|² = 1; the Fourier convention for momentum space is the symmetric one Φ(p) = (1/√(2πℏ))∫Ψ(x)e^{−ipx/ℏ}dx (state the F3 conversion); p̂ = −iℏ∂/∂x; time dependence e^{−iEt/ℏ} (F1's warning about the physics sign).

§1: Schrödinger (1926) wrote his equation by analogy with optics (Hamilton–Jacobi theory, CM-8: the eikonal equation of waves reduces to H–J, so a wave equation whose short-wavelength limit is mechanics must exist — tell this story because the student has CM-8); Born (1926) gave |Ψ|² its probabilistic meaning in a footnote; the problem that forced the wavefunction: de Broglie's waves (QM-1) needed an equation, and the equation had to reproduce E = p²/2m + V. §2: the wavefunction as a complex-valued field whose squared modulus is a probability density; a wave packet as a sum of plane waves (F3) — narrow in x means wide in p, so the uncertainty principle is just the Fourier uncertainty principle of F3 with ℏ attached; the Schrödinger equation as the statement that the energy operator (iℏ∂_t) equals the Hamiltonian operator (p̂²/2m + V); expectation values as averages weighted by |Ψ|²; the student should guess p̂ = −iℏ∂_x from "a plane wave e^{ikx} has momentum ℏk".

Topics in order: THE SCHRÖDINGER EQUATION iℏ∂Ψ/∂t = −(ℏ²/2m)∂²Ψ/∂x² + VΨ, motivated (not derived — say so) by requiring plane waves e^{i(kx−ωt)} with E = ℏω, p = ℏk, E = p²/2m + V; the statistical interpretation; normalization and its preservation in time (PROVE d/dt∫|Ψ|² = 0 using the equation and its conjugate; the probability current J = (ℏ/2mi)(Ψ*∂Ψ − Ψ∂Ψ*) and the continuity equation ∂ρ/∂t + ∂J/∂x = 0 derived; EM-6's continuity equation again); the requirement that V be real for this to hold; EXPECTATION VALUES ⟨x⟩ = ∫x|Ψ|²dx; the velocity d⟨x⟩/dt computed from the Schrödinger equation (integration by parts — do it carefully) giving ⟨p⟩ = ∫Ψ*(−iℏ∂_x)Ψ dx — the operator for momentum DERIVED, not postulated (Griffiths' route); the general rule: ⟨Q(x,p)⟩ = ∫Ψ*Q(x, −iℏ∂_x)Ψ dx; EHRENFEST'S THEOREM d⟨p⟩/dt = ⟨−∂V/∂x⟩ (derive) — Newton's law for averages; HERMITIAN OPERATORS: p̂ is Hermitian under the L² inner product (prove via integration by parts; LA-8's adjoint in function space), so are x̂ and Ĥ; why observables must be Hermitian (real expectation values — the LA-8 theorem); THE UNCERTAINTY PRINCIPLE: standard deviations σ_x, σ_p; the de Broglie argument (narrow packet needs many wavelengths); the precise statement σ_xσ_p ≥ ℏ/2 — derive it HERE via F3's Fourier uncertainty theorem with Ψ and Φ a Fourier pair (the general operator derivation waits for QM-4); the minimum-uncertainty Gaussian; FREE PARTICLE AND WAVE PACKETS: separable solutions e^{i(kx − ℏk²t/2m)} are not normalizable; the general solution as a Fourier integral Ψ(x,t) = (1/√2π)∫φ(k)e^{i(kx − ωt)}dk with ω = ℏk²/2m; phase vs group velocity (v_g = ℏk/m = classical velocity, v_p = half of it — derive; EM-7's dispersion); the GAUSSIAN WAVE PACKET solved exactly (the F3 Gaussian integral): |Ψ(x,t)|² stays Gaussian with width growing as √(1 + (ℏt/2ma²)²) — derive, and compute the spreading time for an electron localized to 1 Å and for a dust grain; MOMENTUM SPACE: Φ(p,t) as the Fourier transform of Ψ, |Φ|² as the momentum probability density (motivate via the plane-wave decomposition; prove ∫|Φ|² = ∫|Ψ|² by Plancherel, F3), ⟨p⟩ = ∫p|Φ|²dp consistent with the operator form (verify), the position operator in momentum space x̂ = iℏ∂_p (derive by the same route); the symmetry between the two representations (LA-4: the same vector in two bases; F3's transform is the change-of-basis unitary — make this explicit as a QM-4 preview); THE TIME-INDEPENDENT SCHRÖDINGER EQUATION: separation of variables Ψ = ψ(x)φ(t) gives φ = e^{−iEt/ℏ} and Ĥψ = Eψ (derive); the three properties of stationary states (time-independent |Ψ|² and ⟨Q⟩; definite energy with σ_H = 0 — prove; the general solution as a superposition Σc_n ψ_n e^{−iE_nt/ℏ} with |c_n|² the energy probabilities and ⟨H⟩ = Σ|c_n|²E_n — derive); the theorems about energy eigenfunctions in 1D: they can be taken real (prove), E must exceed V_min (prove via ⟨H⟩), the eigenfunctions are orthogonal (LA-8: Hermitian Ĥ) and complete (stated; F2's Sturm–Liouville context); a two-state superposition's oscillating |Ψ|² with the beat frequency (E₂ − E₁)/ℏ (derive; this is how transitions radiate — QM-8); the Hamilton–Jacobi connection made explicit (CM-8): write Ψ = √ρ e^{iS/ℏ}, derive the real part of the Schrödinger equation as H–J plus the quantum potential term −(ℏ²/2m)(∇²√ρ)/√ρ, and discuss the ℏ → 0 limit (this is the derivation CM-8's WKB page promised; QM-8 uses it); units and orders of magnitude: ℏ, the electron's Compton wavelength, when quantum effects matter (λ_th vs size; SM-5's criterion).

Worked examples (≥6): normalize Ψ(x,0) = A(a² − x²) on [−a, a], find ⟨x⟩, ⟨x²⟩, σ_x, and ⟨p⟩, ⟨p²⟩, σ_p and check σ_xσ_p ≥ ℏ/2; the probability current for a plane wave and for a standing wave (zero); the Gaussian packet's spreading time for an electron (Δx = 10⁻¹⁰ m) and a 1 μg grain; Φ(p) for a Gaussian Ψ and the uncertainty product exactly ℏ/2; a superposition of the two lowest states of some Ĥ with given E₁, E₂: |Ψ(x,t)|² oscillation frequency and ⟨H⟩; the Ehrenfest check for V = ½mω²x².

Project: a 1D Schrödinger solver with the split-operator (FFT) method — derive the e^{−iV dt/2ℏ}e^{−ip²dt/2mℏ}e^{−iV dt/2ℏ} scheme in a markdown cell (F3: p̂ is diagonal in momentum space), check norm conservation (assert) and energy conservation; the free Gaussian packet: numerical |Ψ(x,t)|² vs the analytic spreading formula (assert the width), the momentum-space density staying fixed (assert); phase vs group velocity visible in the real part vs the envelope (figure grid); the probability current computed and the continuity equation verified on the grid; Ehrenfest's theorem checked for a packet in a harmonic and in an anharmonic potential (⟨p⟩ vs m d⟨x⟩/dt and d⟨p⟩/dt vs ⟨−V'⟩); the uncertainty product σ_xσ_p tracked in time for the free packet (grows) and the minimum-uncertainty state (= ℏ/2, assert); a packet hitting a potential step (QM-3 preview: partial reflection even when E > V₀); the Hamilton–Jacobi check: compute S = ℏ arg Ψ and ρ from the numerical Ψ and verify the modified H–J equation including the quantum potential term. §8 honest: the Schrödinger equation is not derived and students want it to be — it's a postulate that worked; the sign e^{−iEt/ℏ} and the F3 convention conversion will cost an hour; integration-by-parts derivations drop boundary terms that vanish only for normalizable Ψ; "the wavefunction is the particle" is a common misreading — it encodes probabilities; the Gaussian-packet algebra is long and the completing-the-square step is where errors live; momentum space feels redundant until QM-4 shows it's the same vector in a different basis; the split-operator method's time step must resolve the fastest phase (LA-13-style stability thinking).
