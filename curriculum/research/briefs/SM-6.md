# Brief: SM-6 — Interacting systems and phase transitions: the Ising model, mean-field theory, Landau theory, Metropolis Monte Carlo
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/c-thermo-statistical-mechanics/06-phase-transitions-ising/ · ~14 hours · prereqs SM-4, F5 (Markov chains, detailed balance) · unlocks SM-7, ST-6 (MCMC), ML-10 (energy-based models)
Pair with: Schroeder ch. 8; Sethna ch. 8, 12 (and his free simulation exercises); Kardar *Statistical Physics of Fields* ch. 1–2 (the serious version; optional); Newman & Barkema *Monte Carlo Methods in Statistical Physics* ch. 3–4; Krauth *Statistical Mechanics: Algorithms and Computations* ch. 5.
Conventions: Ising Hamiltonian H = −J Σ_{⟨ij⟩} s_is_j − h Σ_i s_i with s_i = ±1 and ⟨ij⟩ nearest neighbors; coordination number z; magnetization per spin m; critical temperature T_c; reduced temperature t = (T − T_c)/T_c; critical exponents α, β, γ, ν, δ with standard definitions; the Metropolis acceptance min(1, e^{−βΔE}).

§1: van der Waals (1873) showed a single equation of state could have a liquid and a gas; Pierre Curie (1895) found iron loses its magnetism above 1043 K; Lenz and Ising (1925) proposed the simplest model and Ising proved (wrongly generalizing) that it has no transition; Peierls (1936) showed it does in 2D; Onsager (1944) solved it exactly — one of the hardest calculations in physics; Metropolis et al. (1953) invented the algorithm that lets a computer do what Onsager did by hand, and it became the most-used algorithm in science; Wilson (1971) explained universality with the renormalization group. The forcing problem: everything in SM-1 to SM-5 was non-interacting (Z factorizes); real matter interacts, and interactions produce the most dramatic phenomena — abrupt phase changes and singular behavior at critical points — which no non-interacting theory can give. §2: energy wants spins aligned (low T), entropy wants them random (high T); free energy F = U − TS decides, and the balance tips at T_c; mean-field theory is "replace your neighbors by their average and solve the resulting one-spin problem self-consistently"; the Metropolis algorithm is a random walk through configurations whose stationary distribution is Boltzmann's — F5's detailed balance in action; near T_c the correlation length diverges, so the microscopic details stop mattering — universality.

Topics in order: THE ISING MODEL defined (lattice gas and binary alloy as the same model; the mapping derived); the 1D model solved exactly by the transfer matrix (derive: Z = Tr T^N, T the 2×2 transfer matrix, Z ≈ λ_max^N; LA-7's largest eigenvalue; the free energy, m(h, T), and the proof that there is no phase transition in 1D at h = 0 — Ising's result); the correlation function in 1D and the correlation length ξ = −1/ln(tanh βJ) (derive); why 1D fails and 2D doesn't: the Peierls domain-wall argument (energy of a domain wall ∝ its length vs entropy ∝ length too — sketch the argument carefully); MEAN-FIELD THEORY: replace s_j by ⟨s⟩ = m for the neighbors, derive the effective field h_eff = h + zJm, the self-consistency equation m = tanh(β(zJm + h)) (derive), its graphical solution, the critical temperature k_BT_c = zJ (derive from the slope condition), the spontaneous magnetization below T_c with the exponent β = 1/2 (derive by expanding tanh), the susceptibility χ ∝ |t|^{−1} (γ = 1, derive), the heat capacity jump (α = 0), the critical isotherm m ∝ h^{1/3} (δ = 3, derive); the mean-field free energy F(m) derived (the Bragg–Williams entropy of mixing −k_B[(1+m)/2 ln((1+m)/2) + (1−m)/2 ln((1−m)/2)] plus the energy −zJm²/2) and its double-well shape below T_c; mean-field's failures: it predicts a transition in 1D (wrong), T_c = 4J in 2D vs the exact 2.269J, and β = 1/2 vs the exact 1/8 — honest table; the van der Waals gas as a mean-field theory of the liquid–gas transition (derive the law of corresponding states and the mean-field exponents; Maxwell construction recap from SM-2); LANDAU THEORY: expand F in the order parameter by symmetry alone, F = a(T)m² + bm⁴ (+ hm), a ∝ (T − T_c), derive the same exponents and the point that they're universal to any system with this symmetry; first-order transitions from a cubic term or negative b (derive the metastability and hysteresis picture); the order parameter concept generalized (density difference, superconducting gap, nematic director) in one page; CRITICAL PHENOMENA: the definitions of the exponents, the exact 2D Ising values quoted (β = 1/8, γ = 7/4, ν = 1, α = 0 (log)), universality (the liquid–gas critical point has the same exponents as the 3D Ising model — the experimental fact that forced the RG), the correlation length and scaling hypothesis, the Rushbrooke and Josephson scaling relations stated and checked against the exponents; the RENORMALIZATION GROUP idea in two pages: block spins/decimation, the 1D Ising decimation done exactly (derive the recursion for K = βJ: tanh K' = tanh²K and its only fixed points at 0 and ∞ — no transition), the 2D qualitative picture with an unstable fixed point at K_c, and the derivation of ν from the linearized recursion (state the formula ν = ln b / ln λ); Kadanoff and Wilson in one paragraph; MONTE CARLO: the Metropolis algorithm derived from detailed balance (F5): propose a spin flip, accept with min(1, e^{−βΔE}); prove the resulting Markov chain has the Boltzmann distribution as its stationary distribution; ergodicity; equilibration and autocorrelation times; critical slowing down (τ ∝ ξ^z) and why single-spin-flip Metropolis fails near T_c; the Wolff/Swendsen–Wang cluster idea in one paragraph; measuring observables and their errors (binning/blocking) — the ST-6 craft previewed; finite-size scaling: how to extract T_c and exponents from finite lattices (the Binder cumulant method stated; the data-collapse idea); heat-bath/Gibbs sampling as the alternative (ST-6 link); SIMULATED ANNEALING as the optimization spin-off (one page; the ML-2 connection); applications paragraph: Ising models of neurons (Hopfield — SM-7), of opinions, of binary alloys; the Hopfield preview sentence.

§8 honest: mean-field theory is wrong in detail and right in spirit, and knowing when to trust it is the skill; the exact 2D solution is not derivable here and the module says so (Onsager's calculation is graduate-level); critical exponents from simulations of L ≤ 64 are rough — don't expect β = 0.125 to three digits; Metropolis near T_c is slow and students think their code is broken; detailed balance is sufficient, not necessary, for the right stationary distribution; the RG is the deepest idea in the course and two pages can only show its shape; equilibration time must be measured, not guessed.
