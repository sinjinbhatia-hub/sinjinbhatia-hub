§1: Balmer's formula (1885), Bohr's model (1913, QM-1) getting the levels right and the physics wrong, Schrödinger's first paper (1926) solving hydrogen exactly and recovering Balmer from a wave equation — the moment quantum mechanics was taken seriously; the forcing problem: the discrete spectrum and the stability of the atom (EM-8's collapse) had to come out of a dynamical theory, not a rule; and the solution's structure (n, ℓ, m; the degeneracy) explains the periodic table once spin and the exclusion principle are added. §2: the problem is CM-5 with ℏ: separate the center of mass, use the angular momentum conservation (QM-5's Y_ℓ^m) to reduce to a radial problem with an effective potential V + ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1)/2mr², and solve a 1D problem (QM-3) on the half-line; bound states fit standing waves into the Coulomb well, and the number of radial nodes plus ℓ sets the energy; the accidental degeneracy in ℓ is the same hidden symmetry as CM-4's Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector.
