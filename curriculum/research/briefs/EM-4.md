# Brief: EM-4 — Electric fields in matter: polarization, D, dielectrics
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/b-electromagnetism/04-electric-fields-in-matter/ · ~8 hours · prereq EM-3 · unlocks EM-5, EM-7 (refractive index)
Pair with: Griffiths ch. 4; Purcell & Morin ch. 10; Feynman Lectures II ch. 10–11.
Conventions: SI; polarization P (dipole moment per volume); bound charges σ_b = P·n̂, ρ_b = −∇·P; D = ε₀E + P; linear media P = ε₀χ_eE, ε = ε₀(1 + χ_e), ε_r = ε/ε₀.

§1: Faraday (1837) found that inserting glass between capacitor plates increases the capacitance, and invented the word "dielectric"; Mossotti and Clausius (1850s–1879) connected the macroscopic constant to molecular polarizability; the forcing problem: inside matter there are ~10²⁹ charges per m³ and Coulomb's law applied literally is hopeless, so one needs averaged fields and a way to separate "free" charge you control from "bound" charge the material produces in response. §2: a neutral atom in a field stretches into a tiny dipole; a polar molecule rotates to align; a block of such dipoles has uncancelled charge only where P changes or at the surface — the bound charges; D is the bookkeeping field whose divergence only sees the charge you put there.

Topics in order: induced dipoles in atoms and the atomic polarizability α (p = αE); the crude model of an atom as a uniformly charged sphere of electron cloud giving α = 4πε₀a³ (derive); polar molecules: torque and alignment, the field of a dipole recap; the force on a dipole in a nonuniform field (p·∇)E; POLARIZATION P defined; THE FIELD OF A POLARIZED OBJECT: derive V(r) = (1/4πε₀)∫P·𝓇̂/𝓇² dτ' and transform it (using ∇'(1/𝓇) = 𝓇̂/𝓇² and the product rule and the divergence theorem) into the potential of a surface charge σ_b = P·n̂ plus a volume charge ρ_b = −∇·P — full derivation, this is the module's central result; the physical interpretation of bound charge (uncancelled ends of dipole chains; derive σ_b = P·n̂ by counting charge at a tilted surface); the field INSIDE a dielectric (the averaging subtlety: Griffiths' argument that the macroscopic field is the average over a sphere, and the result that the average field due to a uniformly polarized sphere is −P/3ε₀ — derive via the uniformly polarized sphere); the uniformly polarized sphere solved (σ_b = P cos θ, interior field −P/3ε₀, exterior pure dipole — derive via EM-3's cos θ shell); GAUSS'S LAW IN DIELECTRICS: ∇·E = (ρ_f + ρ_b)/ε₀ ⇒ ∇·D = ρ_f with D = ε₀E + P (derive); the integral form; what D is and isn't (its curl need not vanish; no "Coulomb's law for D"; the parallel with H in EM-5 flagged); BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: D_above⊥ − D_below⊥ = σ_f and E_∥ continuous (derive); LINEAR DIELECTRICS: susceptibility, permittivity, dielectric constant with a table of values; D = εE; for linear media ρ_b = −(χ_e/(1 + χ_e))ρ_f so a dielectric with no free charge inside has no bound volume charge (derive); worked problems: a metal sphere surrounded by a dielectric shell (D from Gauss, then E, P, bound charges — derive, check total bound charge is zero); the parallel-plate capacitor with a dielectric slab (C increases by ε_r; partial filling in series and in parallel configurations — derive both); a dielectric sphere in a uniform field (the self-consistent argument: the field inside is uniform, E_in = 3E₀/(ε_r + 2) — derive via the polarized-sphere result and a self-consistency equation; and the EM-3 separation-of-variables route as a check); a point charge embedded in a dielectric (field reduced by ε_r); ENERGY in dielectric systems W = ½∫D·E dτ (derive for linear media by charging up; discuss what it includes vs (ε₀/2)∫E²); FORCES on dielectrics: the slab pulled into a capacitor (derive F = −dW/dx at fixed Q and the fixed-V version with the battery's work; resolve the apparent paradox); the fringing-field explanation; the CLAUSIUS–MOSSOTTI relation linking χ_e to α and the number density (derive via the local field E + P/3ε₀); why ε_r depends on frequency (preview: EM-7 dispersion, the Lorentz oscillator model stated); ferroelectrics and the hysteresis picture in one paragraph; the ME/engineering angle in one paragraph: dielectric strength and breakdown, capacitors in power electronics, piezoelectricity as a coupled mechanical–electrical constitutive law (stress tensor meets P; LA-12).

Worked examples (≥5): the uniformly polarized sphere (all fields and bound charges); the metal sphere in a dielectric shell with all bound charges and a check they sum to zero; the capacitor half-filled two ways; the dielectric sphere in a uniform field with the self-consistency derivation and the numbers for ε_r = 4; Clausius–Mossotti for a gas (estimate χ_e for N₂ at STP from a plausible α and compare with the tabulated ~5.5 × 10⁻⁴).

Project: bound charges from a given P(r) computed symbolically (sympy ∇·P) and numerically on a grid, with a check that total bound charge vanishes for a bounded object; the uniformly polarized sphere: build it as a lattice of dipoles (or two offset uniformly charged spheres) and compute the field numerically inside (uniform, −P/3ε₀ — assert within discretization) and outside (dipole); a 2D finite-difference solver for ∇·(ε∇V) = −ρ_f with a dielectric slab inside a capacitor (interface conditions handled by the harmonic-mean ε on faces), compare E in the dielectric and gap with the analytic result, visualize D (continuous normal component) vs E (jumps); the dielectric sphere/cylinder in a uniform field via the same solver vs the closed form 3E₀/(ε_r + 2) (or the 2D analogue 2E₀/(ε_r + 1)); energy W = ½∫D·E vs ½CV² for the slab capacitor; force on a partially inserted slab from the numerical energy vs position compared with the analytic result. "Your turn": a coaxial cable with two concentric dielectrics; Clausius–Mossotti vs χ_e for a dense material where it fails; a piezoelectric constitutive relation applied to a bar under stress (compute the induced surface charge).

§8 honest: D is a bookkeeping device and students want it to be "the real field in the material" — it isn't, E is; the "field inside a dielectric" averaging argument is genuinely subtle and most courses hand-wave it; the ½ in the energy and which energy (with or without the work to assemble the dipoles) is included; the force-on-a-slab paradox (fixed Q vs fixed V) catches everyone; signs of bound charges; the dielectric-sphere self-consistency argument is circular-looking until you see the two routes agree.
