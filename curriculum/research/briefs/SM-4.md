# Brief: SM-4 — Canonical ensemble: the Boltzmann factor derived, partition function, free energy, equipartition; stat mech as inference (maximum entropy)
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/c-thermo-statistical-mechanics/04-canonical-ensemble/ · ~14 hours · prereqs SM-3, F5 (entropy, Lagrange multipliers, maximum entropy) · unlocks SM-5, SM-6, SM-7, ML-10
Pair with: Schroeder ch. 6; Sethna ch. 3, 6; Kittel & Kroemer ch. 3; Jaynes, "Information Theory and Statistical Mechanics" (Phys. Rev. 1957) — read §1–3; MacKay ch. 22 (maximum entropy); Feynman *Statistical Mechanics* ch. 1–2.
Conventions: β = 1/k_BT; partition function Z = Σ_s e^{−βE_s}; probability P(s) = e^{−βE_s}/Z; F = −k_BT ln Z; ⟨·⟩ for ensemble averages; single-particle partition function Z₁; N distinguishable vs indistinguishable with the 1/N!.

§1: Boltzmann's 1868 distribution for gas molecules in a gravitational field; Gibbs's 1902 "canonical ensemble" — the realization that fixing the temperature (contact with a reservoir) rather than the energy makes everything easier; Jaynes (1957) showed the whole thing is a theorem of inference: the Boltzmann distribution is the least-biased assignment of probabilities consistent with a known average energy; the forcing problem: SM-3's microcanonical counting works for toy models but is hopeless for anything realistic (counting states of fixed total energy is hard); the canonical ensemble replaces counting with summing a weight, and the sum (Z) turns out to contain all of thermodynamics. §2: a small system in contact with a huge reservoir; the probability of a small-system state is proportional to the number of reservoir states compatible with it, which is e^{S_R/k_B}, and S_R drops linearly with the energy the small system took — hence e^{−E/k_BT}; equivalently (Jaynes): if all you know is the mean energy, the honest probability assignment is the one that maximizes entropy, and that's the exponential; Z is the normalization constant that secretly knows everything; free energy is −k_BT ln Z because it balances energy against entropy.

Topics in order: the setup (system + reservoir, total energy fixed); THE BOLTZMANN FACTOR DERIVED (route 1, Schroeder/Kittel): P(s₁)/P(s₂) = Ω_R(U − E₁)/Ω_R(U − E₂) = exp([S_R(U−E₁) − S_R(U−E₂)]/k_B), Taylor-expand S_R using ∂S_R/∂U = 1/T, get e^{−(E₁−E₂)/k_BT} — every step, with the justification of dropping second-order terms (reservoir is huge); THE BOLTZMANN FACTOR DERIVED (route 2, Jaynes/F5): maximize S = −k_B Σ p_s ln p_s subject to Σp_s = 1 and Σp_sE_s = U using Lagrange multipliers; the exponential form appears with β as the multiplier; identify β = 1/k_BT by matching ∂S/∂U — and discuss what this second derivation means: statistical mechanics as the rules of inference applied to incomplete information (this is the SM-7/ML-10 bridge; say so); THE PARTITION FUNCTION: Z as normalization; ⟨E⟩ = −∂ ln Z/∂β (derive); fluctuations ⟨E²⟩ − ⟨E⟩² = ∂²ln Z/∂β² = k_BT²C_V (derive; so energy fluctuations are ~1/√N relatively — the canonical and microcanonical ensembles agree for large N, derive the scaling); F = −k_BT ln Z (derive from S = −k_BΣp ln p with the Boltzmann p: S = (U − F)/T); then P = −∂F/∂V, S = −∂F/∂T, μ = ∂F/∂N from SM-2 — Z gives everything; the two-state system (paramagnet) in the canonical ensemble: Z = 2cosh(βμB), ⟨E⟩, M, C_V with the Schottky anomaly (derive and compare with SM-3's microcanonical result — they agree); the EINSTEIN SOLID canonically: Z₁ = 1/(1 − e^{−βε}) (geometric series), ⟨E⟩ = ε/(e^{βε} − 1) — Planck's formula appears (derive); the quantum harmonic oscillator's thermodynamics complete; the CLASSICAL LIMIT: sums → integrals over phase space with 1/h per (q,p) pair (motivate), Z₁ for a free particle in a box = V/λ_th³ with the thermal de Broglie wavelength λ_th = h/√(2πmk_BT) (derive via the Gaussian integral), the N-particle ideal gas Z = Z₁^N/N! and the recovery of PV = Nk_BT, U = (3/2)Nk_BT, and Sackur–Tetrode (derive; compare with SM-3's counting — same answer, far less work; this is why the canonical ensemble wins); THE EQUIPARTITION THEOREM proved: for each quadratic degree of freedom in E, ⟨E_q⟩ = ½k_BT (derive via the Gaussian integral); its failures (quantum freeze-out) explained via the Einstein solid: equipartition holds when k_BT ≫ level spacing; THE MAXWELL–BOLTZMANN SPEED DISTRIBUTION derived from the Boltzmann factor for kinetic energy and the density of states in velocity space (4πv² dv), with v_rms, ⟨v⟩, v_mp computed (SM-1's stated result now derived); the barometric formula and the Boltzmann distribution in a gravitational field (derive; Perrin's measurement of Avogadro's number in one paragraph); ROTATIONAL AND VIBRATIONAL partition functions of diatomic molecules: Z_rot = Σ(2j+1)e^{−βj(j+1)ε}, the high-T limit (equipartition) and low-T freeze-out, Z_vib from the oscillator, the resulting C_V(T) staircase for H₂ with the characteristic temperatures (derive and compare with data); THE FREE ENERGY AS THE THING MINIMIZED: at fixed T the equilibrium macrostate minimizes F = U − TS — derive from maximizing S_total = S + S_R and show it's the competition between energy and entropy (the "energy vs entropy" theme that organizes SM-6's phase transitions); the Gibbs entropy S = −k_BΣp ln p as the general definition and its equivalence to k ln Ω in the microcanonical case; the "stat mech as inference" synthesis: Jaynes's view, what it explains (why the Boltzmann distribution is universal; why maximum entropy with other constraints gives other distributions — F5's Gaussian for fixed variance, exponential for fixed mean), what it doesn't (the dynamics; why systems actually equilibrate), and the honest controversy (one paragraph); the softmax preview: P(s) = e^{−βE_s}/Z is the softmax function of ML-3 with logits −βE, and ln Z is the log-sum-exp — state it, SM-7 develops it; the Gibbs paradox revisited.

Worked examples (≥7): the Boltzmann-factor derivation with a concrete reservoir (an Einstein solid with N_R = 10⁶ oscillators, showing the Taylor expansion's accuracy numerically); the two-state paramagnet's complete thermodynamics with the Schottky peak location; the oscillator's ⟨E⟩ at k_BT = ε/2, ε, 5ε vs the classical k_BT; Z₁ and λ_th for helium at 300 K (and the check that λ_th ≪ interparticle spacing, so the classical limit holds — and when it fails: SM-5); v_rms, ⟨v⟩, v_mp for nitrogen at 300 K; the rotational characteristic temperature of H₂ and the fraction of molecules in j = 0, 1, 2 at 100 K; the barometric scale height of Earth's atmosphere.

§8 honest: the two derivations of the Boltzmann factor answer different questions ("what does a reservoir do" vs "what should you believe") and students think one must be wrong; the Taylor-expansion step hides the assumption that the reservoir's heat capacity is huge; Z for indistinguishable particles needs the 1/N! and students apply it to distinguishable spins; the classical phase-space measure with h is a quantum fact and the module doesn't pretend otherwise; equipartition is a theorem with hypotheses (quadratic, classical) that students apply everywhere; the Jaynes view is persuasive and not universally accepted — know both sides.
