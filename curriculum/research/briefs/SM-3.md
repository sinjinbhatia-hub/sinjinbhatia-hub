# Brief: SM-3 — Statistical foundations: microstates, multiplicity, Boltzmann entropy, Einstein solid, two-state systems, large numbers
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/c-thermo-statistical-mechanics/03-statistical-foundations/ · ~12 hours · prereqs SM-2, F4 (counting, binomial, Stirling preview), CM-8 (Liouville) helpful · unlocks SM-4
Pair with: Schroeder ch. 2–3; Kittel & Kroemer ch. 1–3; Sethna *Statistical Mechanics: Entropy, Order Parameters, and Complexity* (free PDF) ch. 3–5; Feynman *Statistical Mechanics* ch. 1 (for the ambitious); MIT 8.044 lectures on the microcanonical ensemble.
Conventions: Ω for multiplicity (number of microstates), S = k_B ln Ω; microcanonical ensemble = all microstates of fixed (U, V, N) equally likely; the fundamental assumption stated as a postulate; Stirling's approximation ln N! ≈ N ln N − N (and the next term when needed).

§1: Boltzmann (1877) wrote S = k ln W on nothing but the conviction that atoms exist and that the second law is statistics, and was fought for it until his death in 1906; Gibbs (1902) generalized to ensembles; Einstein (1907) applied quantum counting to a solid and explained the failure of Dulong–Petit (SM-1's crack); the forcing problem: thermodynamics (SM-2) says S exists but can't compute it or say what it is; counting microstates does both, and derives the second law as "overwhelmingly probable" rather than "absolute". §2: a macrostate (what you can measure) corresponds to many microstates (what the atoms are doing); if every microstate is equally likely, the system spends almost all its time in the macrostate with the most microstates, because for 10²³ particles "most" means "all but a fraction e^{−10²³}"; entropy is the log of the count because logs turn products (independent systems) into sums (additive entropy); temperature is what equalizes when two systems share energy so as to maximize the total count — and that's 1/T = ∂S/∂U, which SM-2 defined thermodynamically. The student should guess S = k ln Ω from "additive and a function of the count" before §3.

Topics in order: microstates vs macrostates with the two-state paramagnet (N spins up/down in a field) as the first example: Ω(N, N↑) = C(N, N↑) (F4's binomial), the multiplicity's sharp peak, the Gaussian approximation via Stirling (derive: Ω ≈ Ω_max e^{−2x²/N}, width ~√N, relative width ~1/√N); the EINSTEIN SOLID: N oscillators with q energy quanta, Ω(N, q) = C(q + N − 1, q) (derive by the stars-and-bars argument), the high-temperature and low-temperature limits via Stirling; INTERACTING SYSTEMS: two Einstein solids sharing q total quanta, Ω_total = Σ Ω_A Ω_B, the most probable split, and the demonstration that for large N the peak is so sharp that the equilibrium macrostate is effectively certain — compute the width and do the numbers for N ~ 10²³ (the "large numbers" section: very large and very very large numbers, why ln(10^{10²³}) ≈ 10²³ and why adding a mere 10²³ doesn't matter); the FUNDAMENTAL ASSUMPTION (equal a priori probabilities) stated as a postulate, with its justification sketched: Liouville's theorem (CM-8) says a uniform density on the energy surface is stationary, and ergodicity (stated, not proved — honest about its status); THE SECOND LAW as statistics: the system moves to the macrostate of maximal Ω because that's where almost all microstates are; irreversibility as overwhelming probability, not law; the entropy of the Einstein solid and paramagnet; ENTROPY S = k_B ln Ω: motivate from additivity (S_AB = S_A + S_B requires a log) and derive that maximizing S_total at fixed U_total gives ∂S_A/∂U_A = ∂S_B/∂U_B, hence the identification 1/T = ∂S/∂U (match SM-2's thermodynamic identity — this is the module's central argument; do it slowly); P = T ∂S/∂V and μ = −T ∂S/∂N likewise (derive from maximizing S with respect to volume and particle exchange); the Einstein solid's U(T) and heat capacity — derive C_V(T) in the high-T limit (Dulong–Petit recovered: equipartition from counting!) and the low-T exponential freeze-out (the quantum explanation of SM-1's failure — SM-5 refines to Debye); the paramagnet's thermodynamics: S(U), T(U), the magnetization M(T) (Curie's law derived), NEGATIVE TEMPERATURE and what it means (population inversion; the honest explanation); the IDEAL GAS counted: the number of microstates of N particles in a box with energy ≤ U as the volume of a 3N-dimensional sphere (derive the hypersphere volume via the Gaussian-integral trick from F4), the 1/N! for indistinguishability and the h^{3N} for the phase-space cell (motivated by the uncertainty principle; QM-2), giving the SACKUR–TETRODE equation S = Nk_B[ln(V/N (4πmU/3Nh²)^{3/2}) + 5/2] (derive), from which PV = Nk_BT and U = (3/2)Nk_BT follow (derive both) — thermodynamics recovered from counting; the Gibbs paradox resolved (the 1/N!); the entropy of mixing derived from Ω; a short reflection on what the h and the N! are telling you (classical stat mech knows it needs quantum mechanics); ENTROPY AS INFORMATION in one page: Shannon's H (F5) with p_i = 1/Ω gives k_B ln Ω; the general S = −k_B Σ p_i ln p_i as the definition that survives when probabilities aren't equal (SM-4 uses it); the deep sentence "entropy measures what you don't know about the microstate" and Maxwell's demon in one paragraph (Landauer's bound stated).

Worked examples (≥6): Ω for N = 4, 10, 100 two-state spins at half magnetization, with the Gaussian approximation's accuracy; two Einstein solids with N_A = 300, N_B = 200, q = 100: the most probable split and the width; a 10²³-oscillator solid: the ratio of Ω at the peak to Ω at a 10⁻¹⁰ relative displacement from it (the number is e^{−10³}-ish — compute); the Einstein solid's heat capacity at T = ε/k_B and the Dulong–Petit limit; the paramagnet's magnetization at B = 1 T, T = 300 K for electron spins (Curie's law numbers); Sackur–Tetrode for helium at STP (the entropy per atom in units of k_B, compared with the measured value ≈ 15.2 k_B/atom; honest about agreement).

§8 honest: "equally likely microstates" is a postulate, and the ergodic justification is incomplete even today — the module says so; Stirling's approximation is used in two different forms and the dropped terms matter for the width but not the peak; students compute Ω and forget the log turns 10^{10²³} into 10²³; the 1/N! and the h³ are quantum facts smuggled into a classical calculation — Gibbs's paradox is the symptom; negative temperature is not hotter than infinity in any ordinary sense, but it is hotter than any positive temperature; the hypersphere derivation uses F4's Gaussian integral trick and students who skipped it will be stuck.
