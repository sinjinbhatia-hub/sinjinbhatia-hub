# Brief: QC-3 — Protocols: superdense coding, teleportation, BB84 quantum key distribution, the CHSH game
Folder: curriculum/05-quantum-computing/03-protocols/ · ~8 hours · prereqs QC-2 (Bell states, CNOT, no-cloning, partial measurement), QM-7 (Bell inequality, no-signaling) · unlocks QC-7 (channels), QC-8 (teleportation-based gadgets named)
Pair with: Mermin ch. 6; Nielsen & Chuang §1.3.6–1.3.7 (superdense coding, teleportation), §12.6 (QKD); Preskill ch. 4 (teleportation, superdense coding) and ch. 5 (QKD); Bennett & Brassard (1984) BB84; Bennett et al. (1993) teleportation; Cleve, Høyer, Toner & Watrous (2004) for nonlocal games.
Conventions as QC-2 (qubit 0 leftmost; Bell states |Φ±⟩ = (|00⟩ ± |11⟩)/√2, |Ψ±⟩ = (|01⟩ ± |10⟩)/√2); Alice holds the left qubit(s), Bob the right; classical bits m₁m₂; Pauli corrections X^{m₂}Z^{m₁}; the Bell measurement as CNOT then H on the control then Z-measure; quantum bit error rate (QBER).

§1: Bennett and Wiesner (1992) found that one qubit plus prior entanglement can carry two classical bits; Bennett, Brassard, Crépeau, Jozsa, Peres and Wootters (1993) found that two classical bits plus prior entanglement can carry one qubit — teleportation, demonstrated in 1997 and over 1,400 km to a satellite in 2017; Bennett and Brassard (1984) built a key-distribution protocol whose security rests on no-cloning and measurement disturbance rather than on computational hardness; Ekert (1991) based one on Bell's inequality; the CHSH game (Clauser–Horne–Shimony–Holt 1969, recast as a game by Cleve et al.) makes Bell's theorem a statement about winning probabilities. The forcing problem: QC-2 established that entanglement and no-cloning exist; this module shows they are resources with operational value — you can trade them against classical communication — and that the trades are exact and bounded (no protocol beats two bits per qubit; no protocol sends information faster than light). §2: entanglement is a pre-shared correlation that is not information by itself (no-signaling) but becomes information when combined with a classical or quantum channel; superdense coding and teleportation are mirror images (two classical bits ↔ one qubit, given one ebit); in BB84, an eavesdropper who doesn't know the basis must guess, and wrong guesses disturb the state detectably; the CHSH game is a test that classical strategies win at most 75% of the time and quantum strategies win 85%.

Topics in order: the resource inequalities in words (1 ebit + 1 qubit ≥ 2 bits; 1 ebit + 2 bits ≥ 1 qubit) and the Holevo bound (n qubits carry at most n bits without entanglement — stated, with the sentence that this is why superdense coding is surprising); THE BELL BASIS: the four Bell states as an orthonormal basis of C²⊗C² (prove), the Bell measurement circuit (CNOT then H ⊗ I then measure both — derive that it maps each Bell state to a distinct computational basis state, and the inverse circuit prepares them), the local Pauli operations that permute the Bell states (X ⊗ I, Z ⊗ I, XZ ⊗ I applied to |Φ⁺⟩ give the other three — compute all four); SUPERDENSE CODING: the protocol (Alice applies I, X, Z, or ZX to her half according to two bits, sends her qubit, Bob does a Bell measurement), correctness proof (direct computation for all four messages), what's consumed (one ebit and one qubit of communication), why it doesn't violate Holevo (the ebit was pre-shared; counting the total qubits that crossed between the parties, including the entanglement distribution, gives two), security note (an interceptor of the single qubit learns nothing — its reduced state is maximally mixed, QM-7 — derive); QUANTUM TELEPORTATION: the setup (Alice has |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ and half of |Φ⁺⟩; Bob has the other half), the full algebra (rewrite |ψ⟩|Φ⁺⟩ in the Bell basis of Alice's two qubits — derive the identity |ψ⟩_A|Φ⁺⟩_{A'B} = ½Σ_{m₁m₂}|β_{m₁m₂}⟩_{AA'} ⊗ X^{m₂}Z^{m₁}|ψ⟩_B with each coefficient computed), the measurement outcomes uniformly random (prove — so Alice learns nothing about α, β), Bob's correction X^{m₂}Z^{m₁}, the circuit with classically controlled gates, the deferred-measurement version (QC-2: replace classical control by quantum control and measure at the end — derive equivalence), consistency with no-cloning (Alice's copy is destroyed by her measurement) and with no-signaling (before the bits arrive Bob's reduced state is I/2 — compute), what is and isn't teleported (the state, not the particle; the bits carry no information about the state individually), entanglement swapping as teleportation of one half of an entangled pair (derive the outcome — Bob's and Charlie's qubits end up entangled though they never interacted), gate teleportation in one paragraph (applying a Clifford gate by teleporting through a modified resource state; the QC-8 magic-state connection named); QUANTUM KEY DISTRIBUTION: the key-distribution problem and why classical public-key cryptography rests on unproven hardness (and on factoring — QC-5's threat named), the one-time pad (perfect secrecy if the key is random, secret, and used once — prove Shannon's perfect-secrecy property for XOR), BB84 in full: Alice sends random bits in random bases (Z or X), Bob measures in random bases, they publicly compare bases and keep matching ones (sifting, ~50% kept — derive), they sacrifice a random subset to estimate the error rate, then error correction and privacy amplification produce the final key; THE INTERCEPT–RESEND ATTACK analyzed: Eve measures each qubit in a random basis and resends — derive that she causes a 25% error rate on the sifted key (half the time wrong basis × half the time Bob's result flips), so a QBER near zero certifies no such attack; the general security statement (QBER below ≈ 11% allows a secure key via the Shor–Preskill bound — state, with the binary-entropy formula r = 1 − 2h(e) derived for the rate given the Shor–Preskill result), the information–disturbance trade-off as the physical principle (no-cloning made quantitative — one paragraph), practical issues in one paragraph (photon-number-splitting attacks and decoy states; device-independent QKD as the Ekert/CHSH route); E91: entanglement-based QKD with security from a Bell-inequality violation (one page; links to the next section); THE CHSH GAME: the rules (referee sends random bits x, y; Alice and Bob, who cannot communicate, return a, b; they win iff a ⊕ b = x ∧ y), the classical bound 3/4 (prove: deterministic strategies suffice by convexity; enumerate or argue that at most 3 of 4 constraints can hold), the quantum strategy with a shared |Φ⁺⟩ and measurement angles 0, π/4 for Alice and π/8, −π/8 for Bob (derive the winning probability cos²(π/8) ≈ 0.854 from the correlation E = cos 2(θ_a − θ_b)), Tsirelson's bound (quantum strategies cannot exceed cos²(π/8) — state; prove via the CHSH operator's norm ‖A₀⊗(B₀+B₁) + A₁⊗(B₀−B₁)‖ ≤ 2√2 using the operator Cauchy–Schwarz / the square of the CHSH operator — derive the identity S² = 4I − [A₀,A₁]⊗[B₀,B₁]), the PR box (no-signaling allows 100%; quantum mechanics is in between — one paragraph), the connection to QM-7's CHSH inequality |S| ≤ 2 vs 2√2 (the game's win probability = ½ + S/8 — derive), device-independent certification (winning the game certifies entanglement without trusting the devices — one paragraph); a closing page: what these protocols teach (entanglement is a resource that is consumed; classical communication is always needed to unlock it; no-cloning is a feature for cryptography) and the forward links (QC-7 channels and noisy teleportation; QC-8's teleportation-based error correction).

Worked examples (≥8): the Bell measurement circuit mapping |Ψ⁻⟩ to |11⟩ step by step; superdense coding of "10" end to end; the teleportation identity coefficient for the outcome m₁m₂ = 11 computed term by term; Bob's reduced state before the classical bits arrive (I/2); entanglement swapping's final Bell state for one outcome; the intercept–resend QBER of 25% derived by enumerating the cases; the secure key rate 1 − 2h(e) at e = 2%, 5%, 11%; the CHSH quantum winning probability cos²(π/8) from the correlation formula at the four angle pairs.

Project (numpy only; reuse QC-2's state-vector simulator): the Bell basis and Bell-measurement circuit asserted (each Bell state → a unique basis state); superdense coding simulated for all four messages with 10³ shots each (assert 100% decoding); teleportation simulated for 200 random input states: the fidelity of Bob's corrected state = 1 (assert), the outcome distribution uniform (chi-square test — ST-2), Bob's pre-correction reduced state = I/2 for every outcome average (assert), and the deferred-measurement circuit equivalent (assert); entanglement swapping simulated (Bob–Charlie Schmidt rank 2 after Alice's Bell measurement — assert); BB84 simulated with 10⁴ qubits: sifting fraction ≈ 50%, QBER ≈ 0 without Eve and ≈ 25% with intercept–resend (assert within 3σ), QBER vs fraction of qubits Eve intercepts (linear, slope 1/4 — assert), a simple error-correction (parity-check/cascade-lite) and privacy-amplification (random universal hash via a random binary matrix — ST-style) stage producing a shorter shared key, and the key-rate curve 1 − 2h(e) plotted; the CHSH game: classical strategies enumerated (all 16 deterministic pairs — assert max win rate 3/4), the quantum strategy simulated with 10⁵ rounds (assert ≈ 0.854 within 3σ), the win probability as a function of Bob's angle (the cos² curve, maximum at π/8), the CHSH-operator norm computed numerically for random measurement choices (assert ≤ 2√2) and the S² identity asserted; noisy entanglement (a Werner state p|Φ⁺⟩⟨Φ⁺| + (1−p)I/4) and the threshold p > 1/√2 for a CHSH violation (derive and assert) — the bridge to QC-7. §8 honest: teleportation's algebra is a single re-expansion in the Bell basis and students get lost in the 8 terms — write the identity once with every coefficient and the rest is bookkeeping; "teleportation is faster than light" is the misreading to kill, and computing Bob's pre-correction state I/2 kills it; the Holevo bound and superdense coding look contradictory until the ebit is counted; BB84's security proof is far beyond this module, which only analyzes one attack — the 11% threshold is quoted, not derived; Tsirelson's bound derivation is short and elegant and rarely shown — do it; real QKD's weaknesses are in the devices, not the physics, and the module says so.
