# Brief: QC-7 — Noise and open systems: density matrices, quantum channels, Kraus operators, decoherence (T₁, T₂), fidelity and distance measures
Folder: curriculum/05-quantum-computing/07-noise-and-channels/ · ~10 hours · prereqs QC-2, QM-7 (density operators, partial trace, purity, Bloch ball), QM-9 (decoherence model), LA-8 (PSD operators, trace), F5 (entropy, KL) · unlocks QC-8
Pair with: Nielsen & Chuang ch. 8 (quantum noise and operations) and 9 (distance measures), §11.3 (von Neumann entropy); Preskill ch. 3 (foundations II: measurement and evolution) — the best treatment; Wilde *Quantum Information Theory* ch. 4–5 (free arXiv version); Krantz et al., "A quantum engineer's guide to superconducting qubits" (2019) §IV for the T₁/T₂ hardware picture.
Conventions: density operators ρ (Hermitian, PSD, trace 1); Bloch-ball representation ρ = (I + r·σ)/2; quantum channel E as a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map; Kraus representation E(ρ) = ΣK_iρK_i† with ΣK_i†K_i = I; the Choi matrix J(E) = Σ_{ij}|i⟩⟨j| ⊗ E(|i⟩⟨j|); standard channels with parameter p or γ: bit flip, phase flip, depolarizing E(ρ) = (1 − p)ρ + pI/2 (state which convention — N&C's (1 − p)ρ + (p/3)(XρX + YρY + ZρZ) vs the "replace with I/2" form, and give the conversion), amplitude damping (γ), phase damping (λ); T₁ (energy relaxation), T₂ (dephasing; T₂ ≤ 2T₁), T_φ; trace distance D(ρ, σ) = ½‖ρ − σ‖₁; fidelity F(ρ, σ) = (Tr√(√ρσ√ρ))² (state the squared convention; N&C uses the unsquared one — give both); diamond norm named.

§1: real qubits are not isolated: they leak energy into their environment (T₁), and their phases are randomized by fluctuating fields (T₂); in 1999 the best superconducting qubits kept coherence for nanoseconds and today for ~100 microseconds to a millisecond, and every quantum algorithm must finish (or be error-corrected) within that window; von Neumann (1927) and Landau (1927) invented the density matrix for exactly this situation, Kraus (1971) and Choi (1975) characterized the most general physical evolutions, and Lindblad (1976) wrote the master equation for continuous-time open systems; the forcing problem: QC-1–QC-6 assumed perfect unitary gates and pure states, and no hardware provides them; before error correction (QC-8) can be designed, noise must be described precisely — which operations are physically possible on a subsystem, how they degrade a state, and how to measure how much — and the answer is a clean piece of linear algebra (CPTP maps) that generalizes unitaries. §2: a mixed state is a point inside the Bloch ball rather than on its surface (QM-7); noise is a map that shrinks and shifts the ball — depolarizing shrinks it uniformly toward the center, dephasing squashes it onto the z-axis, amplitude damping pulls it toward |0⟩ at the north pole; the most general physical map comes from coupling to an environment, evolving unitarily, and tracing the environment out (QM-7's partial trace), and every such map can be written with Kraus operators; T₁ and T₂ are the exponential time constants of the north-pole pull and the equatorial squash.

§8 honest: complete positivity is the subtle condition everyone skips, and the transpose example is the only way to see why it's needed; the Kraus representation is not unique, and students compare their Kraus operators with a book's and think they're wrong; depolarizing-channel conventions differ by a factor of 4/3 between sources — always convert; T₂ vs T₂* vs T_φ are three different things and the hardware literature uses them loosely; fidelity has a squared and an unsquared convention — check which before comparing numbers; average gate fidelity understates coherent errors, which is why fault-tolerance thresholds are stated in the diamond norm; this module is the vocabulary of QC-8 and of every hardware paper — it's dry and indispensable.
