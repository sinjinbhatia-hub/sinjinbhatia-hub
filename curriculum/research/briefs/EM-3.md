# Brief: EM-3 — Boundary-value problems: separation of variables, method of images, multipole expansion, Legendre polynomials
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/b-electromagnetism/03-boundary-value-problems/ · ~14 hours · prereqs EM-2, F2 (Fourier series) · unlocks EM-4, QM-6 (same separation, same Legendre functions)
Pair with: Griffiths ch. 3; Jackson ch. 2–3 (reference only; warn it's graduate level); MIT 8.07 notes lectures 5–9; Arfken & Weber for special functions.
Conventions: SI; Legendre polynomials P_ℓ(x) normalized with P_ℓ(1) = 1; multipole moments defined as in Griffiths (monopole Q, dipole p, quadrupole Q_ij traceless); spherical coordinates (r, θ, φ); cylindrical (s, φ, z).

§1: Laplace's equation (1782) governs gravity, electrostatics, steady heat, incompressible flow, membranes; Dirichlet, Green, and Thomson (Kelvin, 1845: images) developed the solution methods; Legendre (1782) found his polynomials studying exactly the gravitational potential of a spheroid; the forcing problem: given conductors held at known potentials, find V everywhere — Poisson's equation with boundary conditions, which is a different kind of problem from "add up Coulomb's law" because the charges on the conductors are unknown. §2: a harmonic function has no local maxima (mean-value property: V at a point is the average on any sphere around it), so the solution is "as smooth as possible" given the boundary; separation of variables is "guess a product, get ODEs, superpose" — the student did this for the heat equation in F2; images are "put a fake charge where it makes the boundary condition true"; multipoles are "a Taylor expansion of the potential far away, organized by angular shape".

Topics in order: properties of Laplace's equation: the mean-value property (derive in 3D via the flux argument), no interior extrema, Earnshaw's theorem (no stable electrostatic equilibrium — derive from the mean-value property; why it matters for ion traps and for atoms being quantum); UNIQUENESS THEOREMS: Dirichlet (V on the boundary determines V inside — prove via Green's first identity applied to the difference) and the conductor version (total charge on each conductor determines the field — prove); THE METHOD OF IMAGES: point charge above a grounded plane (derive V, the induced surface charge, total induced charge −q by integration, force on the charge, and the energy with the factor-of-½ subtlety — work it out honestly); point charge outside a grounded conducting sphere (derive the image charge q' = −qR/a at R²/a; verify V = 0 on the sphere; the force); the image method's logic (uniqueness justifies it); SEPARATION OF VARIABLES in Cartesian: the infinite slot/box problems (derive the sine-series solution; this IS F2; the Fourier coefficients by orthogonality; the hyperbolic functions and why they appear); completeness and orthogonality as the two properties needed; SPHERICAL COORDINATES with azimuthal symmetry: separate V = R(r)Θ(θ), derive the radial equation's solutions r^ℓ and r^{−(ℓ+1)}, derive the Legendre equation, solve it by the power-series/Frobenius method showing why ℓ must be a non-negative integer for regularity at θ = 0, π (do this derivation; it is the same argument QM-5 uses for angular momentum quantization — say so), the first few P_ℓ, Rodrigues' formula (state; verify for ℓ = 2), orthogonality ∫P_ℓP_{ℓ'} d(cos θ) = 2δ/(2ℓ+1) (derive via the Legendre ODE as a Sturm–Liouville problem — F2's self-adjoint-operator argument; LA-8), the general solution V = Σ(A_ℓr^ℓ + B_ℓr^{−(ℓ+1)})P_ℓ(cos θ); worked boundary-value problems: sphere with specified V(θ) on its surface; conducting sphere in a uniform field (derive the induced dipole and surface charge 3ε₀E₀cos θ); a spherical shell with surface charge σ(θ) = k cos θ (inside/outside potentials and the matching conditions); cylindrical coordinates with no z-dependence (derive the s^n cos nφ, s^{−n}, ln s family; a conducting cylinder in a uniform field); the general (non-azimuthal) case: spherical harmonics Y_ℓ^m introduced with their key properties stated (orthonormality, completeness, Y_ℓ^m as the eigenfunctions of the angular part of the Laplacian with eigenvalue −ℓ(ℓ+1)) and the promise that QM-5 derives them from angular momentum algebra; THE MULTIPOLE EXPANSION: expand 1/𝓇 for r ≫ r' using the generating function of Legendre polynomials 1/√(1 − 2xt + t²) = Σ P_ℓ(x)tⁿ (state the generating function; derive the first two terms directly by Taylor expansion to show where it comes from); V = (1/4πε₀)Σ_ℓ (1/r^{ℓ+1})∫(r')^ℓ P_ℓ(cos α)ρ dτ'; monopole, dipole (p = ∫r'ρ dτ'), quadrupole terms; the dipole field; origin-dependence of the dipole moment when Q ≠ 0 (derive); the quadrupole tensor and its tracelessness (LA-12: a symmetric traceless (0,2) tensor; 5 independent components ↔ ℓ = 2 has 2ℓ+1 = 5 harmonics — make this link); the physical dipole vs the pure dipole limit; the energy of a dipole in a field U = −p·E, the torque p × E, the force (p·∇)E (derive all three); dipole–dipole interaction energy (derive); applications paragraph: why molecules have dipole moments, van der Waals from induced dipoles (SM-6 preview).

Worked examples (≥6): image charge above a plane with every result (field at the plane, σ(s), total induced charge, force, energy); image in a sphere with the force; the slot problem with the first three Fourier coefficients computed for V₀ constant; the conducting sphere in a uniform field with the surface charge; the shell with σ = k cos θ; the multipole expansion of two charges +q at (0,0,d) and −q at (0,0,−d) to quadrupole order, and of a linear quadrupole (+q, −2q, +q), showing the dipole term vanishes.

Project: Legendre polynomials via the recurrence and via Rodrigues (sympy), orthogonality integrals asserted (2/(2ℓ+1)); the Frobenius series truncation demo: show the series for non-integer ℓ diverges at x = 1 numerically; solve the 2D slot problem by Fourier series and by a finite-difference Laplace solver (LA-13's iterative methods), overlay; the conducting sphere in a uniform field: potential from the closed form vs a 3D (or axisymmetric 2D) relaxation solver with the sphere as an equipotential; method-of-images visualizer: field lines of a charge above a grounded plane from the image construction, and the induced σ(s) compared to the derived formula (assert); multipole expansion: compute exact V of a cluster of point charges on a sphere of radius r and compare with the truncated expansion at ℓ = 0, 1, 2 as r/d grows (error curves with slopes −2, −3, −4 — assert slopes); the quadrupole tensor computed numerically and its trace asserted ≈ 0; spherical harmonics from scipy.special.sph_harm plotted as |Y_ℓ^m|² surfaces for ℓ ≤ 2 (QM-5 preview). "Your turn": image charges for a charge between two grounded planes (infinite series) summed numerically; the potential of a uniformly charged ring expanded in Legendre polynomials (closed-form coefficients on the axis); the field of a water molecule modeled as three point charges at large distance vs its dipole approximation.

§8 honest: this module is pattern recognition plus patience — the only way to learn which method fits which geometry is to do fifteen problems; the Frobenius argument for integer ℓ is the one derivation here that pays off later (QM-5) and the one students skip; the ½ in the image-charge energy; orientation of θ in P_ℓ(cos θ) (measured from the symmetry axis, which is often not z in a given problem); the multipole expansion converges only for r larger than the source size; students confuse the dipole moment of a distribution with the field of a physical dipole; spherical harmonics' normalization and sign conventions differ across books (Condon–Shortley phase) — we follow Griffiths, QM-5 notes the difference.
