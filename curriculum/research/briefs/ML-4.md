# Brief: ML-4 — Kernels, support vector machines, and Gaussian processes
Folder: curriculum/04-machine-learning/04-kernels-svm-gp/ · ~12 hours · prereqs ML-3, ML-2 (duality, KKT, the SVM dual), LA-8 (PSD matrices, inner product spaces), ST-5 (Bayesian regression), F5 (MVN conditionals) · unlocks ML-6 (kernel PCA, spectral clustering), ML-9 (attention as a kernel)
Pair with: CS229 notes §5–6 (kernels, SVMs); Bishop PRML ch. 6–7; Rasmussen & Williams *Gaussian Processes for Machine Learning* (free) ch. 2–5; Schölkopf & Smola *Learning with Kernels* ch. 1–2; Shawe-Taylor & Cristianini ch. 3; Duvenaud's thesis ch. 2 ("The Kernel Cookbook").
Conventions: kernel k(x, x') = ⟨φ(x), φ(x')⟩; Gram matrix K_ij = k(x_i, x_j); RKHS H_k; representer theorem f(x) = Σα_ik(x_i, x); SVM primal with slack ξ_i and C; dual variables α_i; GP f ~ GP(m, k); noise σ_n²; predictive mean and variance; hyperparameters (length scale ℓ, signal variance σ_f²); marginal likelihood.

§1: Aronszajn (1950) characterized reproducing-kernel Hilbert spaces; Aizerman et al. (1964) used kernels in the perceptron; Boser, Guyon and Vapnik (1992) combined kernels with the maximum-margin classifier and the SVM dominated the 1990s–2000s; Cortes and Vapnik (1995) added soft margins; Krige (1951) and Matheron (1960s) did GP regression under the name kriging for mining; Williams and Rasmussen (1996) brought GPs into ML and Neal (1996) showed infinitely wide neural networks are GPs — the forcing problem: ML-3's feature maps give linear models the power to fit curves, but the feature space has to be huge, and the kernel trick makes it free: every algorithm that touches data only through inner products can run in an infinite-dimensional feature space at the cost of an n×n matrix; and the Bayesian version of the same trick — put a Gaussian prior on functions — gives uncertainty for free. §2: a kernel is a similarity function that happens to be an inner product in some feature space, and LA-8's PSD condition is exactly what's needed; the SVM finds the widest street between classes and only the points on the curb matter (support vectors); a Gaussian process is ST-5's Bayesian linear regression with infinitely many features, and its predictions are F5's MVN conditioning formula with the kernel as the covariance — the same formula as ST-8's Kalman update.

Topics in order: THE KERNEL TRICK: the representer form of ML-3's perceptron and ridge regression (derive the dual ridge solution α = (K + λI)⁻¹y and the prediction f(x) = kₓᵀα — the "kernel ridge regression" that every later model rests on), the observation that only inner products appear, replace ⟨x, x'⟩ by k(x, x'); §8 honest: "the kernel trick" is a one-line idea that takes a module to appreciate — the representer theorem is the real content; PSD-ness is not optional and students invent "kernels" that aren't; the SVM dual's three kinds of points via complementary slackness is the ML-2 payoff and the thing to be able to recite; SMO is fiddly and the point is that it exists, not its details; GP regression IS the MVN conditional formula, and if that's not automatic by now, F5 and ST-8 need revisiting; the marginal likelihood's local optima are real and the "noise-explaining" optimum is a trap; O(n³) means GPs stop at n ~ 10⁴ without approximations; kernel methods lost to deep learning on representation learning, not on principle, and ML-9 will show attention bringing them back.
