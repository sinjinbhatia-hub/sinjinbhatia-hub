# Brief: SM-5 — Grand canonical ensemble and quantum statistics: chemical potential, Bose–Einstein, Fermi–Dirac, blackbody radiation, Debye solids, Fermi gases
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/c-thermo-statistical-mechanics/05-quantum-statistics/ · ~14 hours · prereqs SM-4, QM-3 (energy levels of a particle in a box and the oscillator), QM-7 (identical particles) helpful · unlocks SM-6, QM-1 (blackbody as the origin of QM), condensed-matter and astrophysics pointers
Pair with: Schroeder ch. 7; Kittel & Kroemer ch. 5–7; Feynman *Statistical Mechanics* ch. 1; Sethna ch. 7; MIT 8.044 lectures on quantum gases; Baierlein *Thermal Physics* ch. 8–9 for the chemical potential (the clearest treatment).
Conventions: μ chemical potential; grand partition function 𝒵 = Σ_s e^{−β(E_s − μN_s)}; Gibbs factor e^{−β(ε−μ)}; occupation numbers n̄; fugacity; Bose–Einstein n̄ = 1/(e^{β(ε−μ)} − 1), Fermi–Dirac n̄ = 1/(e^{β(ε−μ)} + 1); density of states g(ε); Fermi energy ε_F; spin degeneracy explicit.

§1: Planck (1900) fitted the blackbody spectrum by assuming energy comes in quanta hν, calling it "an act of desperation" — the birth of quantum mechanics from a thermodynamics problem (the ultraviolet catastrophe); Einstein (1905) took photons seriously; Bose (1924) counted photon states a new way and Einstein extended it to atoms, predicting condensation 70 years before it was seen (1995); Fermi and Dirac (1926) found the other possibility, which explains why matter is stable, why metals conduct, and why white dwarfs don't collapse; the forcing problem: classical counting (SM-3/4) gives the wrong answer whenever λ_th is comparable to the interparticle spacing, because identical quantum particles don't have labels, and because the number of particles may not be fixed (photons). §2: the chemical potential as the "price" of a particle, the quantity that equalizes when particles can flow (like T for energy and P for volume); a reservoir of particles and energy; bosons as party guests who prefer occupied states, fermions as exclusive (one per state); at high T and low density both reduce to Boltzmann; the blackbody spectrum as the thermal distribution of photons, which have μ = 0 because they can be created freely.
