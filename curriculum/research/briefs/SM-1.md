# Brief: SM-1 — Thermodynamics I: state variables, temperature, the first law, ideal gas, heat capacities, processes
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/c-thermo-statistical-mechanics/01-thermodynamics-1/ · ~8 hours · prereq multivariable calculus · unlocks SM-2
Pair with: Schroeder *An Introduction to Thermal Physics* ch. 1; Kittel & Kroemer ch. 1 (skim); Fermi *Thermodynamics* ch. 1–2 (short, classic); Feynman Lectures I ch. 39–40; for an ME, Çengel & Boles ch. 1–4 as the engineering counterpart (say explicitly how the notation maps).
Conventions: SI; k_B explicit; N particles, n moles; the first law dU = Q + W with W the work done ON the system (Schroeder's sign; warn that engineering texts use W done BY the system and flip the sign); state functions in capitals; ideal gas PV = Nk_BT = nRT.

§1: steam engines came before thermodynamics (Watt 1776); Carnot (1824) asked what limits an engine's efficiency and answered it without knowing what heat was; Joule (1840s) showed heat is energy; Clausius and Kelvin (1850s) formulated the two laws; the forcing problem for this module: energy is conserved, yet "heat" and "work" are not state functions — the first law's content is exactly the distinction between them. For the ME student: this is a review of the thermodynamics course from a physicist's angle, with the goal of making SM-3's statistical derivation land. §2: a gas as a huge number of molecules whose average kinetic energy is temperature; pressure as momentum transfer to walls (derive the kinetic-theory pressure formula in §3); heat as energy transfer by random molecular collisions, work as energy transfer by organized motion of a boundary; the state of a system as a point in (P, V) and processes as paths; the key picture: U depends only on where you are, Q and W depend on how you got there.

Topics in order: thermal equilibrium and the zeroth law (temperature as the thing that's equal at equilibrium; thermometers; the ideal-gas and absolute scales); the IDEAL GAS LAW from kinetic theory: derive P = (1/3)(N/V)m⟨v²⟩ from momentum transfer, hence ⟨½mv²⟩ = (3/2)k_BT — the first appearance of equipartition (state the theorem, prove it in SM-4); the Maxwell speed distribution stated (derived in SM-4); the Boltzmann constant and Avogadro's number; EQUIPARTITION stated with the quadratic-degrees-of-freedom rule, and the heat capacities it predicts for monatomic/diatomic/solid (Dulong–Petit), with the experimental failures (vibrational freeze-out; the Einstein solid — SM-3/SM-5) flagged as the first sign of quantum mechanics; the FIRST LAW: U as a state function, Q and W as path functions; compression work W = −∫P dV (derive for quasistatic processes; sign conventions boxed); heat capacities C_V = (∂U/∂T)_V and C_P = (∂H/∂T)_P, enthalpy H = U + PV and why it's the natural variable at constant pressure (derive C_P − C_V = Nk_B for an ideal gas); latent heat; the ideal-gas PROCESSES with full derivations: isothermal (W = Nk_BT ln(V_i/V_f)), isobaric, isochoric, adiabatic (derive PV^γ = const from dU = −PdV and dU = C_V dT, with γ = C_P/C_V = (f+2)/f), and the P–V diagrams; a worked Diesel/Otto cycle as the engineering connection (efficiency derived in SM-2 after entropy; here just the work and heat for each leg); the MECHANISMS of heat transfer in one page (conduction and the heat equation — F2's PDE — with the Fourier law, convection, radiation with the Stefan–Boltzmann law stated; SM-5 derives it); the thermal expansion coefficient and compressibility as partial derivatives; the mathematics of partial derivatives in thermodynamics: the cyclic rule and the reciprocity relations (derive; this is the multivariable-calculus skill the subject lives on); exact vs inexact differentials (dU exact, đQ and đW not — the criterion ∂²/∂x∂y symmetry, and why that makes U a state function and Q not — derive with a worked path-dependence computation); real gases briefly: the van der Waals equation (motivate the two corrections; SM-6 uses it for the liquid–gas transition).

Worked examples (≥6): pressure from kinetic theory for air at STP (⟨v²⟩, v_rms); C_P − C_V for an ideal gas; an adiabatic compression of air by a factor of 10 (final T and P); work and heat for each leg of an Otto cycle with numbers; path dependence of Q: two paths between the same states with different Q and W but the same ΔU; the cyclic rule verified for the ideal gas (∂P/∂T)_V(∂T/∂V)_P(∂V/∂P)_T = −1.

Project: a hard-sphere gas simulation in 2D (elastic collisions among N disks in a box; event-driven or small-step) — measure pressure from wall momentum transfer vs Nk_BT/V (assert within statistical error), show the speed distribution relaxing to Maxwell–Boltzmann from a non-equilibrium start (histogram vs the 2D MB formula), and the equipartition of energy between x and y; isothermal and adiabatic compressions of the simulated gas by a moving piston (slow piston → PV^γ with γ = 2 in 2D, assert; fast piston → irreversible heating, SM-2 preview); P–V diagram of the Otto cycle with work computed numerically vs analytic; path-dependence demo: numerically integrate Q and W along two paths; the heat equation on a rod (F2) as the conduction example with a Fourier-law flux. "Your turn": the van der Waals isotherms plotted for CO₂ with the critical point found numerically (SM-6 preview); the Diesel cycle; a 3D hard-sphere version and γ = 5/3.

§8 honest: sign conventions for W differ between physics and engineering and the ME student has already learned the other one — state the map once and stick to it; "heat" is a verb, not a substance, and students write "the heat in the gas"; the adiabatic derivation depends on quasistatic reversibility and students apply it to free expansions; equipartition's failure is not a footnote — it's the crack that became quantum mechanics; partial-derivative bookkeeping (what's held fixed) is where most thermo errors live and the cyclic rule must be drilled.
