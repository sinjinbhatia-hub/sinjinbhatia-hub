# Brief: EM-8 — Potentials, radiation, and relativity: retarded potentials, dipole radiation, Lorentz covariance, the field tensor
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/b-electromagnetism/08-radiation-and-relativity/ · ~10 hours · prereqs EM-7, LA-12 (tensors, index notation) · unlocks QM-1 (the stability-of-atoms problem), QM-8 (spontaneous emission context), SM-5 (blackbody context)
Pair with: Griffiths ch. 10–12; Purcell & Morin ch. 5 and appendix H (the field of an accelerated charge via the Thomson construction); Feynman Lectures I ch. 15–17, 34, II ch. 21, 25–26; Taylor *Classical Mechanics* ch. 15 for special relativity basics; MIT 8.07 notes lectures 25–30.
Conventions: SI; retarded time t_r = t − 𝓇/c; Lorenz gauge; metric signature (−,+,+,+) as in Griffiths (η_μν = diag(−1,1,1,1)), x^μ = (ct, x, y, z), β = v/c, γ = 1/√(1−β²); field tensor F^μν with F^{0i} = E_i/c, F^{ij} = ε_ijk B_k (Griffiths' convention; state it, warn others differ); Einstein summation (LA-12).

§1: three problems that closed classical physics: (i) how does a changing source's influence propagate? — retardation (Lorenz 1867, Liénard–Wiechert 1898–1900); (ii) why do accelerated charges radiate, and how much? — Larmor (1897), which then predicted that Rutherford's atom collapses in 10⁻¹¹ s (QM-1's crisis); (iii) Maxwell's equations are not Galilean invariant, so in which frame do they hold? — Lorentz, Poincaré, and Einstein (1905): they hold in all frames, and space and time must bend to make that true. This module does the first two completely and the third at the level of showing that E and B are one object. §2: the potentials "know about" the source at the retarded time, as if information travels at c; an accelerating charge's field lines kink and the kink propagates outward as radiation (the Thomson/Purcell picture — draw it in words); a moving charge's E field is squashed, and in another frame some of that E looks like B; the field tensor is the matrix whose rows and columns mix E and B under boosts the way x and t mix.

Topics in order: the inhomogeneous wave equations for V and A in the Lorenz gauge (EM-6 recap); the RETARDED POTENTIALS V = (1/4πε₀)∫ρ(r', t_r)/𝓇 dτ', A likewise (derive: show they satisfy the wave equations — the derivation with ∇ acting on both 𝓇 and t_r; Griffiths' "it's easy to get wrong" computation done carefully); the advanced potentials and why we discard them (causality; one paragraph); JEFIMENKO'S EQUATIONS for E and B (state; derive E from the retarded potentials; the point: the fields depend on the retarded ρ, J, and J̇); the LIÉNARD–WIECHERT POTENTIALS for a point charge in arbitrary motion: the "retarded volume" factor 1/(1 − 𝓇̂·v/c) derived (the famous Doppler-like factor from the integral over a moving source), V and A; the fields of a point charge in arbitrary motion stated (Griffiths 10.72) with the velocity field and the acceleration (radiation) field identified; the field of a charge in uniform motion (derive from the L–W result or quote; §8 honest: retarded-time derivatives are the error magnet of the module — ∇ acts on t_r through 𝓇 and students drop it; the three dipole approximations are easy to apply in the wrong order; "radiation" has a precise meaning (1/r fields) that students conflate with "any changing field"; index notation and the metric signature will cost a day — LA-12 is the prerequisite for a reason, and sign conventions for F^μν differ between Griffiths, Jackson, and most QFT books; special relativity is covered here at the minimum needed to see E and B unify, not as a full course — Taylor ch. 15 or Morin's relativity book for more; the Abraham–Lorentz pathologies are real, not a pedagogical omission.
