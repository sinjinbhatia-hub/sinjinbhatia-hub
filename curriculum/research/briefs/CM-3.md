# Brief: CM-3 — Lagrangian mechanics: generalized coordinates, constraints, multipliers, worked systems

Pair with: Taylor ch. 7; Goldstein, Poole & Safko ch. 1–2; MIT 8.223 lectures 1–6.
Conventions: L = T − V; q_i, i = 1..n; constraints f_k(q,t) = 0; multipliers λ_k; SI.

§1: CM-2 gave the principle; this makes it a method. d'Alembert (1743): constraint forces do no virtual work; Lagrange's equations; the multiplier method returns constraint forces. §2: constrained system lives on a surface in configuration space; constraint force ⟂ surface (d'Alembert); coordinates on the surface make it vanish; a multiplier is the "price" of the constraint = the constraint force.

Topics: configuration space, DOF; holonomic vs non-holonomic (rolling without slipping; simple Lagrange needs holonomic); generalized coordinates on the constraint surface; d'Alembert's principle and the SECOND DERIVATION of Lagrange's equations from Newton + virtual work, complete, including d/dt(∂T/∂q̇_i) − ∂T/∂q_i = Σ_a m_a r̈_a·∂r_a/∂q_i; generalized forces Q_i and the non-conservative form; structure of T = ½ΣM_ij(q)q̇_iq̇_j + …, M symmetric PD (derive; CM-6 mass matrix, LA-12 metric); MULTIPLIERS: derive d/dt(∂L/∂q̇_i) − ∂L/∂q_i = Σ_k λ_k ∂f_k/∂q_i and λ_k∇f_k = constraint force; the two uses; Rayleigh dissipation function (derive for linear drag); boxed PROCEDURE; WORKED SYSTEMS with full derivations: (1) simple pendulum; (2) Atwood with and without multipliers (tension); (3) bead on a hoop rotating at fixed ω about a vertical diameter: effective potential, pitchfork bifurcation at ω² = g/R, stability of each branch — the showpiece; (4) double pendulum: full L, both E–L equations expanded, small-angle normal modes (CM-6 preview), chaotic (project shows); (5) pendulum with horizontally oscillating support (parametric; Mathieu/Kapitza in a sentence); (6) block on a frictionless sliding wedge; (7) spherical pendulum: p_φ conserved, effective potential (CM-5 technique preview); (8) charged particle in E and B, p = mv + qA highlighted; rotating frame from L in rotating coordinates: centrifugal and Coriolis fall out (cleaner than Taylor ch. 9); when Lagrange is the wrong tool (impulses, friction with unknown normal, non-holonomic) — honest paragraph.

Worked examples: the eight systems ARE the examples with every step; plus a fully numerical one: bead-on-rotating-hoop small-oscillation frequency about the off-axis equilibrium at ω = 2√(g/R).
