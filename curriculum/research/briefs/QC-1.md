# Brief: QC-1 — Qubits: bits vs qubits, state space, measurement, global and relative phase, the Bloch sphere, single-qubit gates as rotations
Folder: curriculum/05-quantum-computing/01-qubits-and-single-qubit-gates/ · ~8 hours · prereqs LA-8 (Hermitian/unitary operators, spectral theorem, Pauli matrices), QM-5 (spin-1/2, Bloch sphere, Rabi) ideal, QM-1 acceptable; LA-12 appendix (SU(2)) · unlocks QC-2
Pair with: Mermin *Quantum Computer Science* ch. 1; Nielsen & Chuang §1.2–1.3, §2.1–2.2, §4.2; Preskill Ph219 ch. 2 (free); the Qiskit textbook's single-qubit-gates chapter (as a visual aid; no Qiskit in the code); Mermin's "From Cbits to Qbits" (AJP 2003).
Conventions: |0⟩ = (1, 0)ᵀ, |1⟩ = (0, 1)ᵀ (the computational/Z basis); |±⟩ = (|0⟩ ± |1⟩)/√2; |±i⟩; the Bloch vector (sin θ cos φ, sin θ sin φ, cos θ) for cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^{iφ}sin(θ/2)|1⟩; Pauli X, Y, Z; Hadamard H; phase S = diag(1, i); T = diag(1, e^{iπ/4}); rotations R_n(θ) = e^{−iθ n·σ/2}; measurement in the Z basis unless stated; circuit diagrams read left to right, matrices compose right to left (state this contrast explicitly); global phase ignored.

§1: Feynman (1982) observed that simulating quantum systems on classical computers is exponentially hard and asked whether a quantum computer could do better; Deutsch (1985) defined the universal quantum computer; Shor (1994) and Grover (1996) gave the algorithms that made the field; the qubit as a word is Schumacher's (1995); the forcing problem for this module: the classical bit has two states and classical computation is Boolean logic on them, but a two-level quantum system (QM-5's spin-1/2, EM-7's photon polarization, an atom's two levels) has a continuum of states and a different set of allowed operations, and the entire subject is working out what computation looks like when the hardware is quantum. The quantum computer is QM-4's postulates with a specific Hilbert space (C²)^⊗n and nothing else. §2: a qubit is a unit vector in C² up to phase, i.e., a point on a sphere (QM-5's Bloch sphere); a classical bit is the two poles; measurement in a basis projects onto that basis's two points with probabilities given by the Born rule, and destroys the rest; a gate is a rotation of the sphere (unitary = rotation, LA-8/LA-12's SU(2) → SO(3)); relative phase between |0⟩ and |1⟩ is physical (it's the longitude), global phase isn't; the Hadamard gate is the rotation that takes the poles to the equator and is why superposition can be created and uncreated.

Topics in order: classical bits and reversible logic (NOT, the irreversibility of AND, Toffoli as the reversible universal gate — Mermin's Cbit setup; Landauer's principle in one paragraph), classical bits as the computational basis vectors; THE QUBIT: the state space, normalization, the Born rule for Z-basis measurement, measurement in an arbitrary orthonormal basis (project onto |b⟩: p = |⟨b|ψ⟩|²; QM-4), the post-measurement state (collapse) and the no-repeat-measurement fact; GLOBAL VS RELATIVE PHASE: prove that e^{iγ}|ψ⟩ is operationally indistinguishable from |ψ⟩ (all measurement probabilities agree, all gates commute with the phase), and that the relative phase is measurable (the X-basis measurement of |+⟩ vs |−⟩ — compute both); THE BLOCH SPHERE: the parametrization derived from normalization + phase freedom (QM-5), the correspondence of orthogonal states with antipodal points (prove), the X, Y, Z axes and their eigenstates, measurement along an axis n̂ as the projection onto the eigenstates of n̂·σ with p(±) = (1 ± n̂·r)/2 for Bloch vector r (derive), the expectation value ⟨n̂·σ⟩ = n̂·r (derive), the picture of mixed states filling the ball (QM-7; previewed here as "what happens when you lose track"); SINGLE-QUBIT GATES: unitaries as the allowed operations (why: norm preservation + linearity + reversibility; QM-4's time evolution), the Pauli gates and their action on the computational basis and on the Bloch sphere (X = bit flip = π rotation about x; Z = phase flip = π rotation about z; Y = both), the Hadamard (H|0⟩ = |+⟩, H² = I, H as a π rotation about the (x+z)/√2 axis — derive; H X H = Z and H Z H = X — derive and interpret as a change of basis, LA-4), the phase gates S and T (rotations about z by π/2 and π/4;
