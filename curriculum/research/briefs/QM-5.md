# Brief: QM-5 — Angular momentum and spin: the commutator algebra, ladder operators, spherical harmonics, spin-1/2, Pauli matrices, Stern–Gerlach, the Bloch sphere

Conventions: ℏ explicit; L̂ = r̂ × p̂; [L̂_i, L̂_j] = iℏε_ijk L̂_k; L̂_± = L̂_x ± iL̂_y; |ℓ, m⟩ with L̂²|ℓ,m⟩ = ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1)|ℓ,m⟩, L̂_z|ℓ,m⟩ = ℏm|ℓ,m⟩; spherical harmonics Y_ℓ^m with the Condon–Shortley phase (state; EM-3 used Griffiths' convention, note any sign difference); spin Ŝ = (ℏ/2)σ; the Bloch sphere parametrization cos(θ/2)|↑⟩ + e^{iφ}sin(θ/2)|↓⟩.

§1: the problem: hydrogen's spectral lines split in a magnetic field (Zeeman 1896) in ways that needed half-integer quantum numbers, which Pauli (1925) proposed as a "two-valuedness not describable classically"; Uhlenbeck and Goudsmit (1925) called it spin; Stern and Gerlach (1922) had already seen it in silver atoms without knowing; Pauli (1927) wrote the matrices; Dirac (1928) found that spin falls out of relativity. The structural problem this module solves: rotations in 3D don't commute (LA-1 Ex 4.3, CM-7), so the operators generating them can't either, and the commutator algebra alone — with no wavefunctions — determines every possible angular momentum spectrum, including ones (half-integer) that no orbital motion can produce. §2: angular momentum generates rotations (CM-4/CM-8: L is the Noether charge of rotational symmetry, and {L_i, L_j} = ε_ijk L_k); quantizing the Poisson brackets gives the commutators; the ladder operators raise and lower m just as QM-3's oscillator ladders raised n; the spectrum is a finite ladder because L² bounds L_z; spin-1/2 is the smallest nontrivial representation: two states, which is why it's a qubit; the Bloch sphere is the picture of all the states of a two-level system, and the Pauli matrices rotate it.

Topics in order: ORBITAL ANGULAR MOMENTUM: L̂ = r̂ × p̂ in components, derive [L̂_x, L̂_y] = iℏL̂_z (full computation using [x̂_i, p̂_j] = iℏδ_ij) and the cyclic relations; [L̂², L̂_i] = 0 (derive); so L̂² and L̂_z form a compatible pair (QM-4) and we can seek simultaneous eigenstates; THE ALGEBRAIC DERIVATION OF THE SPECTRUM (the showpiece, every step): define L̂_±, derive [L̂_z, L̂_±] = ±ℏL̂_±, [L̂², L̂_±] = 0, show L̂_± raise/lower the L̂_z eigenvalue by ℏ, show the ladder must terminate (because ⟨L̂_x² + L̂_y²⟩ ≥ 0 bounds m), derive the top and bottom rungs, conclude the eigenvalues are ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1) and ℏm with m = −ℓ..ℓ and ℓ integer or HALF-integer, the matrix elements L̂_±|ℓ,m⟩ = ℏ√(ℓ(ℓ+1) − m(m±1))|ℓ,m±1⟩ (derive via the norm), the (2ℓ+1)-fold degeneracy as CM-4's symmetry-degeneracy promise kept; §8 honest: the algebraic derivation of the spectrum is the one derivation in QM everybody should be able to reproduce from memory, and it takes most people three attempts; the half-integer puzzle (why the algebra allows it but functions on the sphere don't) is the key conceptual point and is often fudged; Condon–Shortley phases and the sign of Y_ℓ^m differ across books and will make numerical checks fail by a sign — we flag it; the θ/2 in the Bloch parametrization is where every beginner writes θ; raising and lowering with the wrong √ factor; angular momentum addition is pure bookkeeping and only practice makes it fast; "spin is the electron spinning" is false and the module says why (a rotating charged sphere would need its surface to exceed c).
