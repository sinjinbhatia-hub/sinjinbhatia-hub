# Brief: EM-1 — Vector calculus toolkit: grad/div/curl, integral theorems, delta functions, Helmholtz theorem, curvilinear coordinates
Folder: curriculum/02-physics/b-electromagnetism/01-vector-calculus-toolkit/ · ~10 hours · prereq multivariable calculus · unlocks EM-2 (and every later EM module), CM-1's conservative-force theorem
Pair with: Griffiths *Introduction to Electrodynamics* 4th ed. ch. 1; Schey *Div, Grad, Curl, and All That*; Purcell & Morin appendix; 3Blue1Brown "divergence and curl".
Conventions: SI units; ∇ as a vector operator; r for the field point, r' for the source point, 𝓇 = r − r' (Griffiths' script-r) with 𝓇̂ = 𝓇/|𝓇|; right-handed coordinates; dτ for volume element, da for area element with outward normal.

§1: Maxwell's 1865 equations were twenty equations in components; Heaviside and Gibbs (1880s–90s) invented modern vector notation to compress them to four, and the integral theorems of Gauss (1813), Green (1828), Stokes (1854, really Kelvin's) turned local statements into global ones; the module exists because E&M is a vector-calculus endurance test and the student knows the pieces but not as a toolkit. §2: a vector field as arrows; divergence as "how much is flowing out of a tiny box" (source density), curl as "how much a tiny paddlewheel spins" (circulation density); the integral theorems as "add up all the tiny boxes/paddlewheels and only the boundary survives"; the delta function as "the divergence of 𝓇̂/𝓇² is zero everywhere except it isn't".

Topics in order: vector algebra recap (dot, cross, BAC-CAB, triple products with geometric meaning; position/separation vectors); how vectors transform under rotation (one page; a vector is what transforms like one — LA-12 link); the gradient as the direction of steepest ascent and df = ∇f·dl (derive); divergence and curl defined, computed in Cartesian, with the geometric interpretations DERIVED from flux through a small box and circulation around a small loop (do both limits explicitly); product rules (list the six; derive two); second derivatives: Laplacian, ∇·(∇×A) = 0 and ∇×(∇f) = 0 (prove), ∇×(∇×A) = ∇(∇·A) − ∇²A (derive via index notation with ε_ijk — LA-12); line, surface, volume integrals and how to set them up (worked recipe); the FUNDAMENTAL THEOREMS: for gradients ∫∇f·dl = f(b) − f(a); divergence theorem (Gauss) — derive from the small-box picture (sum of fluxes telescopes); Stokes' theorem — derive from the small-loop picture; corollaries (∮∇f·dl = 0; ∮(∇×A)·da = 0; the integral of a curl over a closed surface vanishes); Green's identities (derive the first two from the divergence theorem; they are the workhorses of EM-2/EM-3); CURVILINEAR COORDINATES: spherical and cylindrical; derive the scale factors and the volume elements; state grad/div/curl/Laplacian in both (derive the spherical gradient and divergence fully; cite Griffiths' inside cover for the rest); the Laplacian in spherical coordinates and why ∇²(1/r) = 0 for r ≠ 0; THE DIRAC DELTA in 3D: the paradox ∇·(𝓇̂/𝓇²) = 0 yet the flux through any sphere is 4π; resolution δ³(𝓇) with ∇·(𝓇̂/𝓇²) = 4πδ³(𝓇) (derive via the flux argument) and ∇²(1/𝓇) = −4πδ³(𝓇) (F3 link: δ is a functional); properties and the sifting property in 3D; THE HELMHOLTZ THEOREM: a vector field vanishing at infinity is determined by its divergence and curl; F = −∇U + ∇×W (state; sketch the proof via Poisson's equation with the Green's function 1/4π𝓇; this is why Maxwell's equations, which specify div and curl of E and B, determine the fields); POTENTIALS: curl-free ⇔ gradient of a scalar (prove via Stokes; the three equivalent conditions of CM-1 completed), divergence-free ⇔ curl of a vector (state; construct); gauge freedom teaser (adding a constant to U, a gradient to W).

Worked examples (≥6): divergence and curl of r, r̂/r², and the "whirlpool" field (−y, x, 0) with pictures in words; verify the divergence theorem for F = (x², 2xz, y²)... pick a field and a unit cube and compute both sides fully; verify Stokes for a hemisphere or a square loop; the spherical divergence of r̂/r² and the delta-function flux; the Laplacian of 1/r at r ≠ 0 by direct computation; construct the potential U for a given conservative field by line integration along a convenient path.

Project: numerical div and curl via finite differences on a grid, compared with sympy's analytic results for several fields; a divergence-theorem checker: flux through the faces of a box by numerical surface integration vs the volume integral of div (assert); a Stokes checker for a loop in a plane; visualize the 𝓇̂/𝓇² field and the flux through concentric spheres (constant 4π — assert) vs through spheres not enclosing the origin (0); a regularized delta (narrow Gaussian) and ∇²(1/r) approaching −4πδ³ numerically; curvilinear coordinate scale factors checked symbolically with sympy (Jacobian determinants r² sin θ and s); Helmholtz decomposition of a 2D or 3D field via FFT (F3: ∇· and ∇× are algebraic in Fourier space) — split a random smooth field into gradient and curl parts, assert the parts have the right curl/div, reassemble. "Your turn": verify a product rule numerically; compute the solid angle subtended by a disk; show the line integral of a curl-free field is path-independent on three paths.

§8 honest: the delta-function argument feels like cheating the first time and is the single most used fact in E&M; sign of the Laplacian; spherical-coordinate curl is unlearnable by memory, look it up; Stokes' theorem orientation (right-hand rule relating da and dl) is wrong in half of first attempts; students know the pieces and still freeze on setting up a surface integral — the recipe matters; index notation for ∇×(∇×A) is a skill from LA-12 that saves pages.
