# LA-14 Exercises

Conventions as in the notes: gradients are columns, $\nabla_X f$ has the shape of $X$, Jacobians are $m \times n$ with $J_{ij} = \partial f_i/\partial x_j$, and every derivative is computed by differentials and the identification rule (3.8). Write the shape next to every object you define.

## A. Warm-up (do all; 5–10 minutes each)

**A1.** Let $f(x_1, x_2) = 3x_1^2 - 2x_1x_2 + x_2^2 + 5x_1$. Write $f$ as $\tfrac12\mathbf{x}^\top A\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{x}$ with $A$ symmetric. Use (3.9) and (3.22) to write $\nabla f$ and $H$, evaluate both at $\mathbf{x} = (1, 2)^\top$, and confirm $\nabla f$ by computing the two partial derivatives directly. Is $f$ a bowl, a dome, or a saddle?

**A2.** Let $\mathbf{F}(x, y) = (x^2y,\; \sin x + y)^\top$. Write the Jacobian $J(x, y)$ and evaluate it at $(1, 2)$. Compute the linear approximation $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{p}) + J\mathbf{h}$ for $\mathbf{p} = (1, 2)^\top$, $\mathbf{h} = (0.1, -0.1)^\top$, compare with the exact $\mathbf{F}(1.1, 1.9)$, and check that the error is of size $\|\mathbf{h}\|^2$ rather than $\|\mathbf{h}\|$.

**A3.** Prove the three trace identities of §3.3 by writing out the sums: (a) $\operatorname{tr}(AB) = \operatorname{tr}(BA)$ for $A$ $m \times n$ and $B$ $n \times m$; (b) $\mathbf{u}^\top\mathbf{v} = \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{v}\mathbf{u}^\top)$; (c) $\operatorname{tr}\big(A^\top(B \odot C)\big) = \operatorname{tr}\big((A \odot B)^\top C\big)$ for $A, B, C$ of the same shape. Then use (a) to show $\operatorname{tr}(ABC) = \operatorname{tr}(CAB)$ but that $\operatorname{tr}(ABC) \ne \operatorname{tr}(ACB)$ in general (find a $2 \times 2$ counterexample).

**A4.** Compute $\mathbf{s} = \operatorname{softmax}(1, 2, 3)$ to four decimals and write out the Jacobian (3.20). Check that every row sums to zero and that $J\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}$. What is $\nabla_{\mathbf{z}}L$ for cross-entropy with target $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{e}_1$?

**A5.** For $f(x) = e^x$ at $x = 0$, compute the central-difference estimate $(f(h) - f(-h))/2h$ in Python for $h = 10^{-1}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-6}, 10^{-10}$ and record the error from the true value $1$. For each $h$, say whether truncation or rounding dominates, using the estimates $h^2|f'''|/6$ and $\epsilon|f|/h$ from §3.8. Which $h$ is closest to $\epsilon^{1/3}$, and is it the most accurate?

## B. Core (do all)

**B1.** (The rules from the definition.) (a) Starting from Definition 3.7, prove the product rule (3.5) including the bound on the discarded term: show $\|(dA)(dB)\|_F \le \|dA\|_F\|dB\|_F$. (b) Derive (3.6) for $d(A^{-1})$. (c) Compute $d(A^2)$ and $d(A^3)$. Show with $A = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\0 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ and $dA = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0\\1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ that $d(A^2) \ne 2A\,dA$. (d) Derive $\nabla_A\operatorname{tr}(A^2)$ and $\nabla_A\operatorname{tr}(A^3)$.

**B2.** (Ridge regression, ST-3 preview.) Let $f(\mathbf{x}) = \|A\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b}\|^2 + \lambda\|\mathbf{x}\|^2$ with $\lambda > 0$. (a) Derive $\nabla f$ with differentials and show the stationarity condition is $(A^\top A + \lambda I)\mathbf{x} = A^\top\mathbf{b}$. (b) Compute the Hessian and prove it is positive definite for every $\lambda > 0$, even when $A^\top A$ is singular (use LA-8: $A^\top A \succeq 0$). Conclude the minimizer is unique. (c) For $A = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 1\\1 & 1\end{pmatrix}$, $\mathbf{b} = (2, 0)^\top$, $\lambda = 1$, solve for $\mathbf{x}$. Then let $\lambda \to 0$ and identify the limit with the pseudoinverse solution $A^+\mathbf{b}$ of LA-9.

**B3.** (Code.) Derive, with differentials, $\nabla_A\|AX - B\|_F^2 = 2(AX - B)X^\top$ and $\nabla_X\|AX - B\|_F^2 = 2A^\top(AX - B)$, with $A$ $m \times p$, $X$ $p \times n$, $B$ $m \times n$ (check the shapes). Setting the second to zero gives $A^\top AX = A^\top B$: explain in one sentence why this is $n$ copies of the LA-5 normal equations, one per column. Then add both identities to the `identities` dictionary in Part 2 of `project.py` and confirm they pass `grad_check`.

**B4.** (Second-derivative test and Newton on a saddle.) Let $g(x, y) = x^2 + 4xy + y^2$. (a) Compute $\nabla g$ and $H$, find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $H$, and classify the origin. Along which unit direction does $g$ increase fastest from the origin, and along which does it decrease? (b) Take one Newton step (3.23) from $(1, 1)$. Where does it land, and what does this tell you about Newton's method on non-convex functions? (c) Let $h(x, y) = x^3 - 3xy^2$. Show the origin is a critical point with $H = 0$, and show by looking at $h(t, 0)$ for $t$ of both signs that it is neither a minimum nor a maximum.

**B5.** (Why backprop exists.) A network has $k$ layers of width $d$ followed by a scalar loss, so $J = J_k J_{k-1}\cdots J_1$ with $J_k \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d}$ and the rest $d \times d$. (a) Derive the flop counts of §3.6 for: (i) the right-to-left matrix product, (ii) reverse mode, (iii) forward mode for the full gradient. Give the leading term in each case. (b) Evaluate all three for $d = 1000$, $k = 10$. (c) Reverse mode must store the forward values; how many numbers is that, in terms of $k$ and $d$? (d) Now suppose instead $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ (one input, $d$ outputs: the sensitivity of a simulation to one parameter). Which mode is cheaper and by what factor? State the general rule for $f : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$.

**B6.** (Three-layer backprop.) Let $\mathbf{z}_1 = W_1\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}_1$, $\mathbf{a}_1 = \sigma(\mathbf{z}_1)$, $\mathbf{z}_2 = W_2\mathbf{a}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2$, $\mathbf{a}_2 = \sigma(\mathbf{z}_2)$, $\mathbf{z}_3 = W_3\mathbf{a}_2 + \mathbf{b}_3$, $L = \tfrac12\|\mathbf{z}_3 - \mathbf{y}\|^2$, with $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$, hidden widths $h_1, h_2$, output $m$. (a) Write the shape of every weight, bias, pre-activation and activation. (b) Derive all six gradients by differentials, defining $\boldsymbol{\delta}_3, \boldsymbol{\delta}_2, \boldsymbol{\delta}_1$ along the way; state the shape of each line. (c) Replace the loss by softmax-cross-entropy, $L = -\mathbf{y}^\top\log\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_3)$. Which of your formulas change?

**B7.** (What Gauss–Newton drops.) For the fit of Example 4.5, $r_i(a, b) = ae^{-bt_i} - y_i$. (a) Compute the $2 \times 2$ Hessian $\nabla^2 r_i$ of each residual. (b) Show the off-diagonal entry of the dropped term $\sum_i r_i\nabla^2 r_i$ equals $(J_r^\top\mathbf{r})_2/a$, and hence vanishes at every stationary point of $f = \tfrac12\|\mathbf{r}\|^2$. (c) Evaluate the dropped term and $J_r^\top J_r$ numerically at the starting point $(1.5, 0.5)$ (data $t = 0, 1, 2$; $y = 2.0, 1.2, 0.8$). Is the Gauss–Newton approximation good there? Why did the method still converge?

**B8.** (The Gaussian MLE.) For an i.i.d. sample $\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_n$ from $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma)$ in $\mathbb{R}^p$: (a) Write the log-likelihood $\ell(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma)$ and its gradients using (3.38)–(3.39). (b) Solve $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\ell = \mathbf{0}$ and $\nabla_\Sigma\ell = 0$ simultaneously to get $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}$ and $\hat\Sigma$. (c) Compute the Hessian of $\ell$ with respect to $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ (with $\Sigma$ fixed) and show it is negative definite, so $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}$ is a maximum. (d) For the sample $(1, 0), (3, 2), (2, 4)$, compute $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}$ and $\hat\Sigma$ and verify that $\nabla_\Sigma\ell$ vanishes there.

## C. Challenge (do at least one)

**C1.** (Jacobian of inversion.) (a) Using (3.6) and the `vec` identity $\operatorname{vec}(AXB) = (B^\top \otimes A)\operatorname{vec}X$, show that the Jacobian of $X \mapsto X^{-1}$ with respect to column-major $\operatorname{vec}$ is $-(X^{-\top} \otimes X^{-1})$. Write it out as a $4 \times 4$ matrix for $X = \begin{pmatrix}2 & 1\\1 & 3\end{pmatrix}$ and check one entry by differentiating $(X^{-1})_{11} = x_{22}/\det X$ by hand. (b) Without `vec`, derive $\nabla_X\operatorname{tr}(AX^{-1}) = -X^{-\top}A^\top X^{-\top}$ and $\nabla_X\operatorname{tr}(X^{-1}) = -(X^{-2})^\top$. (c) Add a numerical check of (a) to `project.py`.

**C2.** (Forward mode, timed.) Implement `jvp(F, x, v)` returning $J_F(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{v}$ by a central difference along $\mathbf{v}$. For $f(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{1}^\top\tanh(W\mathbf{x})$ with $W$ a random $1000 \times 1000$ matrix, compute $\nabla f$ (i) by 1000 JVPs in the coordinate directions and (ii) by one reverse pass with the project's `Tensor` class, and (iii) by the closed form from (3.19). Time (i) and (ii). Relate the ratio to §3.6, then explain why a dual-number implementation of forward mode (carry $(x, \dot x)$ through every operation) would have the same count but no truncation error.

**C3.** (Second differential of $\log\det$; concavity.) (a) Starting from $d\log\det A = \operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}dA)$ and holding the increment $dA = H$ fixed, differentiate once more to show $d^2\log\det A = -\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}HA^{-1}H)$. (b) For $A \succ 0$ and symmetric $H$, show $-\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}HA^{-1}H) = -\|A^{-1/2}HA^{-1/2}\|_F^2 \le 0$ (use the symmetric square root from LA-8), so $\log\det$ is concave on the positive-definite cone. (c) Explain why this, together with B8(c), is the reason the stationary point $(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}, \hat\Sigma)$ in §3.9 is a maximum of the log-likelihood in $\Sigma$ along the line from $\hat\Sigma$ in any symmetric direction. (d) State what changes in (3.39) if $\Sigma$ is parametrized by its upper triangle only, and why the location of the maximum does not change.
