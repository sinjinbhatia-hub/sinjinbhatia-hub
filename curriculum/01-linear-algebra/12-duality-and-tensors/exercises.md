# LA-12 Exercises

Conventions as in the notes: $\mathbf{e}'_j = \sum_i P_{ij}\mathbf{e}_i$ (columns of $P$ are the new basis vectors in old coordinates), operators change by $P^{-1}AP$, bilinear forms by $P^\top BP$, complex inner products conjugate the first slot, `np.kron` ordering with qubit 0 the leftmost factor, $\operatorname{vec}$ stacks columns.

## A. Warm-up (do all; 5–10 minutes each)

**A1.** Find the dual basis to $\mathbf{e}_1 = (2,1)^\top$, $\mathbf{e}_2 = (1,1)^\top$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ using (3.3). Verify $\mathbf{e}^i(\mathbf{e}_j) = \delta^i_j$ on all four pairs. Show that the rows of $E^\top$ do **not** work. Use the dual basis to write $\mathbf{v} = (3,2)^\top$ in the basis $\{\mathbf{e}_1,\mathbf{e}_2\}$.

**A2.** (a) The functional $\varphi(x,y,z) = 2x - y + 3z$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$: write its components $\varphi_i$ in the standard dual basis, and find the vector $\mathbf{v}$ with $\varphi = \langle\mathbf{v},\cdot\rangle$ (Theorem 3.9). (b) The functional $\varphi(\mathbf{u}) = (1+i)u^1 - 2i\,u^2$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$ with the standard inner product: find $\mathbf{v}$ with $\varphi = \langle\mathbf{v},\cdot\rangle$. Then show what goes wrong if you forget the conjugate.

**A3.** Let $M = \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $P = \operatorname{diag}(2, 1)$ (new basis $\mathbf{e}'_1 = 2\mathbf{e}_1$, $\mathbf{e}'_2 = \mathbf{e}_2$). Compute $P^{-1}MP$ and $P^\top MP$. For each entry that changed, explain the factor in one sentence using "rulers and arrows" from §2: which slot ate a vector, which produced one?

**A4.** (a) Compute $\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_z$ by hand using (3.12), where $\sigma_x = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, $\sigma_z = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$. Which qubit does $\sigma_x$ act on? Check by applying it to $|00\rangle = (1,0,0,0)^\top$. (b) Write the CNOT gate with control qubit 0 and target qubit 1 as a $4\times 4$ matrix ($|10\rangle\mapsto|11\rangle$, $|11\rangle\mapsto|10\rangle$, the others fixed). Prove it is not of the form $A\otimes B$ for any $2\times 2$ matrices $A, B$. (Hint: look at the diagonal blocks of (3.12).)

**A5.** (a) Show that every $U\in SU(2)$ has the form $U = \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-\bar b&\bar a\end{pmatrix}$ with $|a|^2 + |b|^2 = 1$. (Hint: compare $U^{-1} = U^\dagger$ with the $2\times 2$ inverse formula.) (b) Evaluate (A.7) for $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{e}_z$ and check it has this form.

## B. Core (do all)

**B1.** Under $\mathbf{e}'_j = \sum_iP_{ij}\mathbf{e}_i$, prove that the dual basis changes by $\mathbf{e}'^i = \sum_k(P^{-1})_{ik}\mathbf{e}^k$ (use the uniqueness in Theorem 3.2). Deduce that the components of a fixed functional change by $\boldsymbol\varphi' = P^\top\boldsymbol\varphi$. Finally show that if the basis $\{\mathbf{e}_i\}$ is orthonormal in $\mathbb{R}^n$, the dual basis vectors (as rows) are the basis vectors themselves, so "the dual basis is the transpose" is true exactly in that case.

**B2.** (a) Prove from Definition 3.4 that $(ST)^* = T^*S^*$ for $T: V\to W$, $S: W\to U$. Combine with Theorem 3.5 to deduce $(BA)^\top = A^\top B^\top$. (b) Show that under the natural isomorphisms $V\cong V^{**}$ and $W\cong W^{**}$ of Theorem 3.3, the double dual $T^{**}: V^{**}\to W^{**}$ is $T$ itself: that is, $T^{**}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}) = \Lambda_{T\mathbf{v}}$. What matrix identity is this?

**B3.** The two-qubit state $\psi = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\big(2|00\rangle + |01\rangle + |10\rangle + 2|11\rangle\big)$. (a) Write its coefficient matrix $C$ and decide whether it is entangled (Theorem 3.14). (b) Compute $C^\dagger C$, the singular values, and the Schmidt coefficients. (c) Write $\psi$ in Schmidt form (3.9) explicitly and verify by expanding. (d) Compute the entanglement entropy $-\sum_k\sigma_k^2\ln\sigma_k^2$. (e) If both qubits are measured in the $\{|+\rangle, |-\rangle\}$ basis, what are the probabilities of the four outcomes?

**B4.** The quadratic form $q(x,y,z) = x^2 + 4xy + 2xz + 2y^2 + z^2$. (a) Write its symmetric matrix $B$. (b) Find the signature by completing squares, and exhibit an explicit invertible $P$ with $P^\top BP = \operatorname{diag}(\pm1, \pm1, \pm1)$ (or with zeros). (c) Check your signature against the signs of the eigenvalues of $B$ (compute $\det B$ at least). (d) Explain, citing Theorem 3.12, why no change of basis can make $q$ a sum of three squares with positive coefficients, and exhibit a vector with $q < 0$.

**B5.** (Transformation law, by hand and in code.) (a) Write the transformation law (3.18) for a tensor of type $(1,2)$ with components $T^i{}_{jk}$. (b) If $T^i{}_{jk} = A^i{}_j\,\varphi_k$ is the tensor product of an operator and a functional, show the law is consistent with $A' = P^{-1}AP$ and $\boldsymbol\varphi' = P^\top\boldsymbol\varphi$. (c) Implement (a) with a single `np.einsum` call and check (b) numerically on random $A$, $\boldsymbol\varphi$, $P$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Expect to spend time on index order; record the mistake you made.

**B6.** (a) Prove the $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ identity $\varepsilon_{ijk}\varepsilon_{imn} = \delta_{jm}\delta_{kn} - \delta_{jn}\delta_{km}$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ by cases on the index values. (b) Use it and (3.23) to derive $\mathbf{a}\times(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}) - \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})$ in three lines. (c) Derive $|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|^2 = |\mathbf{a}|^2|\mathbf{b}|^2 - (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})^2$ the same way.

**B7.** (Code.) The Sylvester equation $AX + XB = C$ with $A$ $n\times n$, $B$ $m\times m$, $X, C$ $n\times m$. (a) Use the vec trick (3.13) twice to show it is equivalent to $(I_m\otimes A + B^\top\otimes I_n)\operatorname{vec}X = \operatorname{vec}C$. (b) Add a function `solve_sylvester_vec(A, B, C)` to `project.py` that builds this system with `np.kron` and solves it with `np.linalg.solve`, using `order="F"` for the vectorization. Assert $\|AX + XB - C\|$ is at machine precision and compare with `scipy.linalg.solve_sylvester`. (c) Using Theorem 3.16(e) and the eigenvectors of Kronecker products, show the system matrix has eigenvalues $\lambda_i + \mu_j$ ($\lambda_i$ of $A$, $\mu_j$ of $B$), so the equation has a unique solution iff $A$ and $-B$ share no eigenvalue. (d) Write the row-major version of the identity and check it too.

## C. Challenge (do at least one)

**C1.** (Universal property, used twice.) (a) Prove $V\otimes W\cong W\otimes V$ by constructing the isomorphism from a bilinear map and the universal property, and showing the two induced linear maps are inverse. (b) Prove $(V\otimes W)^*\cong V^*\otimes W^*$: define a bilinear $\Phi: V^*\times W^*\to(V\otimes W)^*$, induce $\tilde\Phi$, and show it sends the basis $\{\mathbf{e}^i\otimes\mathbf{f}^j\}$ to the dual basis of $\{\mathbf{e}_i\otimes\mathbf{f}_j\}$.

**C2.** (No Schmidt decomposition for three particles.) (a) Count real parameters to argue that a generic state of three qubits cannot be written $\sum_{k=1}^2\sigma_k\,\mathbf{a}_k\otimes\mathbf{b}_k\otimes\mathbf{c}_k$ with $\{\mathbf{a}_k\}$, $\{\mathbf{b}_k\}$, $\{\mathbf{c}_k\}$ orthonormal. (b) Prove that the W state $\tfrac1{\sqrt3}(|001\rangle + |010\rangle + |100\rangle)$ has no such decomposition. (Hint: if it did, the Schmidt decomposition across the cut qubit 0 | qubits 12 would have *product* Schmidt vectors on the right. Compute that Schmidt decomposition; its singular values are distinct, so its Schmidt vectors are unique up to phase.) (c) Show the GHZ state $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(|000\rangle + |111\rangle)$ *does* have such a form. What property of the two-particle case was the proof of Theorem 3.15 really using?

**C3.** (The Lie algebra isomorphism and the claim of A.6.) (a) Show that the linear map $\phi: \mathfrak{so}(3)\to\mathfrak{su}(2)$ with $\phi(L_j) = -\tfrac i2\sigma_j$ is a bijection satisfying $\phi([X,Y]) = [\phi(X),\phi(Y)]$. (b) For $U(\theta) = \exp(-\tfrac{i\theta}2\mathbf{n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)$, differentiate $U(\theta)(\mathbf{v}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)U(\theta)^\dagger$ at $\theta = 0$ and show the result is $(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{v})\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$. Conclude that the derivative at $\theta = 0$ of $R(U(\theta))$ (the map of A.6) is $[\mathbf{n}]_\times$. (c) Using $U(\theta + s) = U(\theta)U(s)$ and the homomorphism property of $R$, show $R(U(\theta))$ satisfies $\dot R = R\,[\mathbf{n}]_\times$, $R(0) = I$, and conclude $R(U(\mathbf{n},\theta)) = \exp(\theta[\mathbf{n}]_\times)$: the rotation by $\theta$ about $\mathbf{n}$, for every axis. This proves the claim of A.6.
