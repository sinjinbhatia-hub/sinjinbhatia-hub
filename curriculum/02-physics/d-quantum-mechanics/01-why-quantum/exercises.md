# QM-1 Exercises

Conventions as in the notes: $\hbar$ explicit, $hc = 1239.84$ eV nm, $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$ conjugate-linear in the first slot, $|{\uparrow}\rangle = (1, 0)^\top$, $|{\downarrow}\rangle = (0, 1)^\top$, $\hat{\mathbf S} = \frac\hbar2\boldsymbol\sigma$. Use CODATA constants ($m_ec^2 = 511.0$ keV, $E_R = 13.606$ eV, $h/m_ec = 2.4263$ pm). Every number in `solutions.md` is printed by `project.py` Part 8.

## A. Warm-up (do all; 5–10 minutes each)

**A1.** Compute the photon energy in eV for 400 nm light, 700 nm light, and a 1 MHz radio wave. Which of the three can eject electrons from sodium ($W = 2.3$ eV)? What is sodium's threshold wavelength? Why does a 50 kW AM radio transmitter, which emits enormous numbers of photons, never cause a photoelectric effect in sodium?

**A2.** Using (3.4)–(3.5), compute the hydrogen energies $E_2$ and $E_3$, the Lyman-$\alpha$ wavelength ($2\to1$), the H$\beta$ wavelength ($4\to2$), and the energy needed to ionize hydrogen from $n = 2$. Infinite nuclear mass.

**A3.** (a) Verify by matrix multiplication that $|{-x}\rangle = (|{\uparrow}\rangle - |{\downarrow}\rangle)/\sqrt2$ is an eigenvector of $\hat S_x$ and find its eigenvalue. (b) In the state $|{\downarrow}\rangle$, find $P(S_x = +\hbar/2)$. (c) In the state $|\chi\rangle = (|{\uparrow}\rangle + i|{\downarrow}\rangle)/\sqrt2$, find the probabilities of both $S_x$ outcomes, both $S_y$ outcomes and both $S_z$ outcomes.

**A4.** (Global and relative phase.) (a) Prove from the Born rule (3.9) that $|\psi\rangle$ and $e^{i\alpha}|\psi\rangle$ give identical probabilities for every outcome of every observable, and identical post-measurement states up to phase. (b) Show that $|{+x}\rangle$ and $|{-x}\rangle$, which differ only by the *relative* phase of the $|{\downarrow}\rangle$ component, give different probabilities for some measurement. Which one?

**A5.** Compute the de Broglie wavelength of a neutron with kinetic energy $k_BT$ at $T = 300$ K. Compare it to an interatomic spacing (0.1–0.3 nm) and say why reactor neutrons are used for crystallography.

## B. Core (do all)

**B1.** (Compton, maximum recoil.) A 100 keV X-ray photon scatters off a free electron at rest. (a) At what angle is the energy transferred to the electron largest? (b) Compute the scattered wavelength, scattered photon energy and electron kinetic energy at that angle. (c) Show that as the incident photon energy $E\to\infty$, the back-scattered photon energy tends to $m_ec^2/2$.

**B2.** (Reduced mass and the discovery of deuterium.) (a) Redo the derivation of Theorem 3.1 for an electron and a nucleus of finite mass $M$ orbiting their common center of mass, quantizing the *total* angular momentum. Show that $m$ is replaced by $\mu = mM/(m + M)$, so $R_M = R_\infty/(1 + m/M)$. (b) Compute $R_H$ and $R_D$ (deuteron mass $3.3436\times10^{-27}$ kg) and the vacuum wavelengths of H$\alpha$ and D$\alpha$. Harold Urey found deuterium in 1931 by seeing faint lines displaced by this amount. How large is the shift?

**B3.** (Bohr–Sommerfeld, its successes and failures.) Apply $\oint p\,dq = nh$ to: (a) a particle of mass $m$ in a one-dimensional box $0\le x\le L$ (it bounces between the walls with constant $|p|$); (b) a harmonic oscillator $H = p^2/2m + \frac12m\omega^2q^2$, computing the phase-space area of the ellipse $H = E$. (c) The exact quantum answers (QM-3) are $E_n = n^2h^2/(8mL^2)$, $n\ge1$, for the box and $E_n = (n + \frac12)\hbar\omega$, $n\ge0$, for the oscillator. Which does the rule get exactly right, which does it get wrong, and in what way? (d) For the oscillator, what is the ratio of the rule's prediction to the correct value of the ground-state energy?

**B4.** (The Bloch sphere.) Let $|\psi\rangle$ be the state (3.15) with Bloch vector $\hat{\mathbf n}$ and $|{+n'}\rangle$ the spin-up state along a second axis $\hat{\mathbf n}'$. (a) Show that $|\langle{+n'}|\psi\rangle|^2 = \frac12(1 + \hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}')$. (Hint: $|{+n'}\rangle\langle{+n'}| = \frac12(I + \hat{\mathbf n}'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)$; prove this projector identity first, then use Theorem 3.5.) (b) Deduce that orthogonal states sit at antipodal points. (c) Where on the sphere is $\frac{1}{\sqrt5}(|{\uparrow}\rangle + 2i|{\downarrow}\rangle)$? Give $\theta$, $\phi$ and $\hat{\mathbf n}$.

**B5.** (Sequential analyzers at $60°$.) Atoms in $|{\uparrow}\rangle$ enter an SG$\hat{\mathbf n}$ with $\hat{\mathbf n}$ at $\theta = 60°$ from $z$ in the $xz$-plane, then an SGz. (a) Find the probability of $+n$ at the first analyzer and the state afterward. (b) Find the probability that an atom ends with $S_z = -\hbar/2$ having passed through the $+n$ port; through the $-n$ port; through either (both ports open, with detectors recording which). (c) Now both ports of SG$\hat{\mathbf n}$ are recombined coherently with no record of the path. What is the probability of ending in $S_z = -\hbar/2$? Explain the difference from (b) using Feynman's rules (ii) and (iii). (d) Check (b) against `project.py` Part 3.

**B6.** (Commutators and the spin uncertainty product.) (a) Compute $[\hat S_y, \hat S_z]$ and $[\hat S_z, \hat S_x]$ by explicit matrix multiplication. (b) For the state (3.15) with Bloch vector $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$, show that $(\Delta S_x)^2 = \frac{\hbar^2}{4}(1 - n_x^2)$ and $(\Delta S_y)^2 = \frac{\hbar^2}{4}(1 - n_y^2)$. (c) Show that the Robertson inequality $(\Delta S_x)^2(\Delta S_y)^2\ge\frac14|\langle[\hat S_x, \hat S_y]\rangle|^2$ reduces to $n_x^2n_y^2\ge0$. Which states give equality? Is $|{+x}\rangle$ a minimum-uncertainty state for this pair, even though $\Delta S_x = 0$ there?

**B7.** (Larmor precession, Postulate 5.) An electron spin sits in a field $B\hat{\mathbf z}$ with $\hat H = \omega_0\hat S_z$, $\omega_0 = g\mu_BB/\hbar$, $g = 2.0023$. (a) Starting from $|{+x}\rangle$, find $|\psi(t)\rangle$ by (3.11) and compute $P(S_x = +\hbar/2)$ at time $t$. (b) Compute $\langle\hat{\mathbf S}\rangle(t)$ and describe its motion. (c) Prove directly that $U(t)$ is unitary. (d) For $B = 1$ T, compute $\omega_0$ and the precession period.

**B8.** (Partial which-path information; extends `project.py`.) (a) In (3.16) take $\phi_{1,2}(x) = A(x)e^{\mp ikx}$ with a real envelope $A$ and tags with $\langle t_1|t_2\rangle = re^{i\delta}$, $0\le r\le1$. Show that $P(x) = 2A^2[1 + r\cos(2kx + \delta)]$ and that the fringe visibility $(P_{\max} - P_{\min})/(P_{\max} + P_{\min})$ is $r$. What does $\delta$ do? (b) In `project.py` Part 5, add a curve for tags $|t_1\rangle = (1, 0)$, $|t_2\rangle = (\cos\beta, e^{i\delta}\sin\beta)$ for $\beta = 0, \pi/6, \pi/3, \pi/2$ and assert that the visibility is $|\cos\beta|$. (c) Explain in two sentences why "the photon scattered off the electron disturbed its trajectory" is not the right explanation of the lost interference.

**B9.** (Expectation values two ways.) For $|\psi\rangle = \frac1{\sqrt5}(|{\uparrow}\rangle + 2i|{\downarrow}\rangle)$: (a) compute $\langle\hat S_x\rangle$, $\langle\hat S_y\rangle$, $\langle\hat S_z\rangle$ from $\langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle$; (b) compute $\langle\hat S_y\rangle$ again as $\sum_ia_ip_i$ using the eigenvectors (3.14); (c) prove that $\langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle$ is real for every Hermitian $\hat A$ without using eigenvalues.

## C. Challenge (do at least one)

**C1.** (Wien's displacement law and the limits of Planck.) (a) Show that $u_\lambda$ in (3.2) is maximal where $x = hc/(\lambda k_BT)$ solves $x = 5(1 - e^{-x})$. Solve it numerically and compute $b = \lambda_{\max}T$. (b) Where does the Sun's spectrum peak, taking $T = 5772$ K? (c) Show that $\int_0^\infty u_\lambda\,d\lambda = aT^4$ with $a = 8\pi^5k_B^4/(15h^3c^3)$, using $\int_0^\infty x^3/(e^x - 1)\,dx = \pi^4/15$. Why does the same integral with (3.1) diverge, and at which end?

**C2.** (A hidden-variable model fails; code.) Suppose each silver atom carries a definite classical unit vector $\mathbf m$, and an SG$\hat{\mathbf n}$ outputs $+$ if $\mathbf m\cdot\hat{\mathbf n} > 0$ and $-$ otherwise, without changing $\mathbf m$. The oven produces $\mathbf m$ uniformly on the sphere. (a) Show that the model reproduces experiments 1 and 2 of §2 (repeatability, and 50/50 for SGx after SGz$+$). (b) For atoms that passed SGz$+$, show that $P(+n) = 1 - \theta/\pi$ where $\theta$ is the angle between $\hat{\mathbf n}$ and $\hat{\mathbf z}$. Compare to the quantum $\cos^2(\theta/2)$ at $\theta = 60°, 90°, 120°$. (c) Write the Monte Carlo (10–15 lines) and check (b). (d) What does the model predict for experiment 3, and can you rescue it by letting the SGx randomize $\mathbf m$? What would you have to assume, and what does QM-7 (Bell) say about all such rescues?

**C3.** (Bohr's correspondence principle.) (a) For a Bohr orbit, compute the classical orbital frequency $f_{\rm orb}(n) = v_n/(2\pi r_n)$ and show $f_{\rm orb} = 2E_R/(hn^3)$. (b) Compute the frequency of the photon emitted in $n\to n - 1$ and show that its ratio to $f_{\rm orb}(n)$ tends to 1 as $n\to\infty$. Evaluate the ratio at $n = 10$ and $n = 100$. (c) Explain why this is what one should demand of any quantum theory of the atom, given EM-8's classical radiation formula.
