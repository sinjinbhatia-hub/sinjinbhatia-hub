# SM-2 Exercises

Conventions as in the notes: $dU = đQ - đW$ with $đQ$ into and $đW$ by the system; $R = 8.314\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$; temperatures in kelvin unless marked °C ($0\ °\mathrm{C} = 273.15$ K). Equation numbers refer to `notes.md`.

**A. Warm-up** (do all; 5–10 minutes each)

**A1.** A refrigerator keeps its interior at $T_c = 275$ K in a kitchen at $T_h = 295$ K. (a) What is the Carnot bound on its COP? (b) A real unit has COP 3 and must remove heat leaking in at 300 W. What electrical power does it draw, and how much heat does it dump into the kitchen per second?

**A2.** One kilogram of liquid water ($c_P = 4186\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$, take it constant) is heated at atmospheric pressure from 20 °C to 80 °C. Find $\Delta S$ of the water. Does the answer depend on whether you heat it on a slow hotplate or by dropping in a red-hot rock?

**A3.** Two moles of a monatomic ideal gas go from $(T_1, V_1)$ to $(2T_1, 3V_1)$ by some irreversible process. Find $\Delta S$ of the gas. Explain why you did not need to know the process.

**A4.** Starting from $dU = T\,dS - P\,dV + \mu\,dN$, derive $dH$, $dF$, $dG$ (do not copy (3.22)–(3.24); derive them). From $dH$ read off $(\partial H/\partial P)_{S,N}$ and derive the Maxwell relation that $H$ gives.

**A5.** An air-standard Otto engine ($\gamma = 1.4$) has compression ratio $r = 8$. Find its efficiency. If the intake air is at 300 K, what is the temperature at the end of compression?

**A6.** A 50 g ice cube at 0 °C melts in a room at 20 °C and ends as water at 0 °C (take $L = 334$ kJ/kg). Find $\Delta S$ of the ice, of the room, and of the universe. Which sign tells you this happens spontaneously?

**B. Core** (do all; these are the module)

**B1. Carnot's theorem from Clausius.** The proof of Theorem 3.2 in the notes uses Kelvin's statement. Prove part (a) again using Clausius's statement directly: assume $\eta_X > \eta_R$, and this time size the reversed engine $R$ so that it consumes exactly the work $X$ produces. Show the net effect is heat moving from cold to hot with nothing else changing.

**B2. Entropy generated by an irreversible compression.** One mole of ideal gas at 300 K, in contact with a 300 K reservoir, has volume $V_1$. The external pressure is suddenly raised to $2P_1$ and held there; the gas is compressed to $V_1/2$ and returns to 300 K. (a) Find the work done on the gas, the heat delivered to the reservoir, $\Delta S_{\text{gas}}$, $\Delta S_{\text{res}}$, $\Delta S_{\text{universe}}$. (b) Compare the work with what a reversible isothermal compression needs, and show the excess equals $T\,\Delta S_{\text{universe}}$.

**B3. Stability and concavity.** A hypothetical substance at fixed $V$, $N$ has $S(U) = k_B(U/\varepsilon)^2$ for $U > 0$, with $\varepsilon$ a constant energy. (a) Find $T(U)$ and $C_V$. What is odd about $C_V$? (b) Split a sample with energy $U$ into two identical halves that can exchange energy. Show that the equal split is an entropy *minimum*, and say what the system does. (c) Show that $U(S)$ is concave, and explain why $F(T) = \min_S[U(S) - TS]$ then fails to describe the system.

**B4. Free expansion of a van der Waals gas.** One mole of CO₂ treated as a vdW gas ($a = 0.364$ Pa m⁶ mol⁻², $b = 4.267\times10^{-5}$ m³/mol, $c_v = \tfrac52R$) at 300 K expands freely from 1.00 L to 2.00 L in an insulated box. Using $u = c_vT - a/v$ and (3.28), find the final temperature and $\Delta S$. Compare with the ideal gas. Why does the gas cool?

**B5. $C_P - C_V$ for a van der Waals gas.** (a) Use the second form of (3.29) to show that, per mole,
$$c_P - c_v = \frac{R}{1 - \dfrac{2a(v - b)^2}{RTv^3}}.$$
(b) Check the ideal-gas limit. (c) Evaluate for vdW CO₂ at 300 K and $v = 1.00$ L/mol. (d) What happens to $c_P - c_v$ as you approach the critical point, and why does that make sense?

**B6. Joule–Thomson inversion.** (a) Starting from (3.30), derive the low-density result $\mu_{JT} \approx (2a/RT - b)/c_P$ for a vdW gas. (b) Find the low-density inversion temperature for N₂ ($a = 0.1370$, $b = 3.87\times10^{-5}$) and H₂ ($a = 0.02476$, $b = 2.661\times10^{-5}$), SI units. (c) Compute $\mu_{JT}$ at 300 K for each (take $c_P = \tfrac72R$) in K/bar. (d) Explain why a Linde liquefier works directly on nitrogen at room temperature but hydrogen must first be precooled.

**B7. Clausius–Clapeyron, both signs.** (a) A pressure cooker runs at 2.00 atm absolute. Using (3.37) with $L = 40.66$ kJ/mol, find the boiling temperature. (b) For melting ice at 273.15 K: $L = 333.6$ kJ/kg, specific volumes $v_{\text{ice}} = 1.0908\times10^{-3}$ and $v_{\text{water}} = 1.0002\times10^{-3}$ m³/kg. Find $dP/dT$ along the melting curve in bar/K, being explicit about the direction of the transition. (c) Is pressure melting the reason ice skates glide? Estimate.

**B8. The chemical potential of an ideal gas, two ways.** (a) From (3.16) form $F = U - TS$ as a function of $(T, V, N)$ and compute $\mu = (\partial F/\partial N)_{T,V}$. Show it has the form (3.34). (b) Verify $G = \mu N$ explicitly for your result. (c) Verify the Gibbs–Duhem relation (3.33) at fixed $T$. (d) By how much does the molar chemical potential rise when an ideal gas at 300 K is compressed from 1 atm to 10 atm? Which way would particles flow if the two samples were connected?

**B9. Latent heat of vdW CO₂ two ways (code).** Using `coexistence(T)` from Part 7 of `project.py`, compute the latent heat of vaporization of vdW CO₂ for $T$ from 240 K to 300 K in two independent ways: (i) Clausius–Clapeyron (3.36), $L = T(v_g - v_l)\,dP^*/dT$, with $dP^*/dT$ by central differences; (ii) $L = T(s_g - s_l)$ with $s$ from (3.28). Show they agree to better than 0.2%. Explain why they *must* agree exactly for this model (no ideal-gas approximation is involved), and what happens to $L$ as $T \to T_c$.

**C. Challenge** (do at least one)

**C1. Maximum work from two finite bodies.** The bricks of Example 4.3 ($C = 1680$ J/K each, at 400 K and 200 K) are used as the reservoirs of a reversible engine that runs until they reach a common temperature. (a) Find that temperature and the total work. (b) Show that for equal heat capacities $W_{\max} = C(\sqrt{T_1} - \sqrt{T_2})^2$, positive whenever $T_1 \ne T_2$. (c) Connect to Example 4.3: show that $W_{\max}$ is exactly the heat needed to warm both bricks from the reversible final temperature to the contact final temperature of 300 K, and that doing that warming by direct heating at the bricks' own temperatures generates exactly the $C\ln(9/8)$ of Example 4.3. Say in one sentence what this tells you about where the work went in direct contact.

**C2. Stability conditions.** Let $S(U, V)$ at fixed $N$ be the entropy of a uniform system. (a) Generalize the two-halves argument of Theorem 3.6 to fluctuations in both energy and volume, and show stability requires the Hessian of $S$ to be negative semidefinite. (b) Show this implies $C_V > 0$ and $\kappa_T > 0$. (Hint: work with $U(S, V)$, which must be convex, and use the Hessian determinant.) (c) Conclude $C_P \ge C_V$ from (3.29), and that the loop of a vdW isotherm with $(\partial P/\partial v)_T > 0$ cannot be an equilibrium.

**C3. Heat pump versus furnace.** A house needs heat. Option 1: a gas furnace burning fuel at 95% efficiency. Option 2: electricity from a gas-fired plant of efficiency 40% running a heat pump with COP 3. (a) Heat delivered per joule of fuel for each? (b) What heat-pump COP breaks even with an ideal (100%) furnace? (c) What is the Carnot COP when it is −10 °C outdoors and 20 °C inside, and why is a real unit's COP so far below it? (d) Extend Your-turn item 3 of `project.py` to find the outdoor temperature at which a COP of $0.45\times$ Carnot reaches break-even.
