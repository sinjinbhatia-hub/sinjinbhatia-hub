# CM-3 Exercises

Conventions as in the notes: $L = T - V$, angles from the downward vertical, constraint force $= \lambda\nabla f$, virtual displacements at frozen time, $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ in numerical checks. Equation numbers refer to `notes.md`.

## A. Warm-up (do all; 5–10 minutes each)

**A1.** Count the degrees of freedom and name a set of generalized coordinates for each: (a) a planar double pendulum; (b) a free rigid body; (c) two point masses joined by a rigid rod, moving in three dimensions; (d) a bead on a fixed bent wire; (e) three point masses joined pairwise by three rigid rods, moving in a plane; (f) a disk rolling without slipping along a straight line (it stays upright and in a vertical plane); (g) a sphere rolling without slipping on a plane. For (g), explain why "number of degrees of freedom" needs care.

**A2.** Holonomic or not? If holonomic, write it as $f(q, t) = 0$; if rheonomic, say so. (a) $|\mathbf{r}| = R$. (b) $|\mathbf{r}| \le R$. (c) $x\dot x + y\dot y = 0$. (d) $\dot x - R\dot\phi\cos\theta = 0$ with $\theta$ a free coordinate. (e) $\dot x\sin\theta_0 - \dot y\cos\theta_0 = 0$ with $\theta_0$ a fixed constant. (f) $z = h(t)$ for a given function $h$.

**A3.** A pendulum bob (mass $m$, length $\ell$, angle $\theta$) is pushed by a constant horizontal force $F\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ in addition to gravity. Compute the generalized force $Q_\theta$ from (3.6), write Lagrange's equation in the form (3.11), and find the equilibrium angle. Then redo it by absorbing $F$ into a potential and check you get the same equation.

**A4.** For the block-on-wedge system of Example 4.6, write the Jacobian $J = \partial(X_{\text{wedge}}, x_{\text{block}}, y_{\text{block}})/\partial(X, s)$ and the diagonal mass matrix $\mathcal{M} = \operatorname{diag}(M, m, m)$, and verify that $J^\top\mathcal{M}J$ is the mass matrix in Example 4.6. Confirm it is positive definite for every $\alpha$ by computing its trace and determinant.

**A5.** For the damped oscillator $m\ddot x + c\dot x + kx = 0$, write $L$, $\mathcal{F}$, and $E$, and verify (3.23) directly: $dE/dt = -2\mathcal{F}$. Then, for $m = 1$ kg, $k = 4$ N/m, $c = 0.5$ kg/s, $x(0) = 1$ m, $\dot x(0) = 1$ m/s, compute $dE/dt$ at $t = 0$.

## B. Core (do all; these are the module)

**B1.** (The two identities.) Let $\mathbf{r}(q_1, \dots, q_n, t)$ be twice continuously differentiable. (a) Prove the cancellation of dots (3.7), stating exactly which variables are held fixed in the partial derivative. (b) Prove the interchange (3.8), including the $\partial\mathbf{r}/\partial t$ term. (c) Verify both identities explicitly for the rotating hoop, $\mathbf{r}(\theta, t) = (R\sin\theta\cos\omega t, R\sin\theta\sin\omega t, -R\cos\theta)$: compute $\dot{\mathbf{r}}$, $\partial\dot{\mathbf{r}}/\partial\dot\theta$, $\partial\mathbf{r}/\partial\theta$, $\partial\dot{\mathbf{r}}/\partial\theta$, and $\frac{d}{dt}(\partial\mathbf{r}/\partial\theta)$ as explicit vectors.

**B2.** (Pendulum tension by multipliers; code.) Describe the simple pendulum with Cartesian coordinates $(x, y)$ and the constraint $f = x^2 + y^2 - \ell^2 = 0$. (a) Write the two equations (3.18) and identify the constraint force $\lambda\nabla f$. Which way does it point when $\lambda > 0$? (b) Differentiate the constraint twice and solve for $\lambda$ as a function of $(x, y, \dot x, \dot y)$. Convert to polar form and show the string tension is $T = m(g\cos\theta + \ell\dot\theta^2)$. (c) Evaluate $T$ for $m = 1$ kg, $\ell = 1$ m, $\theta = 0$, $\dot\theta = 2$ rad/s. (d) *Code:* in `project.py`, the function `dae_accelerations` solves the KKT system (3.21). Write a `pendulum_dae_rhs(t, y)` that calls it with $M = mI_2$, $J = (2x, 2y)$, and the correct right-hand side, integrate from $\theta_0 = 1$ rad at rest for 10 s, and plot $\lambda(t)$ against $-T/(2\ell)$ from the integrated angle. Measure the drift of $f$ over 10 s. Then add Baumgarte stabilization, replacing the constraint row's right-hand side by $-\dot q^\top(\nabla^2f)\dot q - 2\beta\dot f - \beta^2 f$ with $\beta = 5$, and measure the drift again.

**B3.** (Atwood with a massive pulley.) Give the pulley of Example 4.2 moment of inertia $I$ and radius $R$, with the string not slipping on it. Use three coordinates $x_1, x_2$ (downward) and $\phi$ (pulley angle, positive when $m_1$ descends), and two constraints $f_1 = x_1 + x_2 - \ell$ and $f_2 = x_1 - R\phi$. (a) Argue that $f_2$ is holonomic even though it came from "rolling without slipping." (b) Write the three equations (3.18) with multipliers $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$. (c) Solve for $\ddot x_1$, $\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$. (d) Identify the two string tensions $T_1$ (on $m_1$'s side) and $T_2$ (on $m_2$'s side) in terms of the $\lambda$'s, and show $T_1 - T_2 = I\ddot\phi/R$. Interpret. (e) Check the limit $I\to0$ against (4.4).

**B4.** (Bead on a rotating parabolic wire.) A bead slides on the wire $z = \frac12 kr^2$ (in cylindrical coordinates) which rotates about the $z$-axis at constant $\omega$. (a) Write $\mathbf{r}(r, t)$, compute $T$ and $V$, and show $L = \frac12 m(1 + k^2r^2)\dot r^2 + \frac12 m(\omega^2 - gk)r^2$. (b) Derive the equation of motion; explain the origin of the $\dot r^2$ term. (c) Find all equilibria as a function of $\omega$ and their stability. Compare with the hoop's pitchfork: what is different, and why? (d) What happens physically to a bead on the wire for $\omega^2 > gk$?

**B5.** (Double pendulum normal modes, general masses.) With $\ell_1 = \ell_2 = \ell$ and general $m_1, m_2$, linearize (4.10) about the hanging equilibrium, write $M_0$ and $K$, and show the normal-mode frequencies satisfy $\Omega^2 = \mu g/\ell$ with
$$\mu_\pm = \frac{1}{1 \mp \sqrt{m_2/(m_1 + m_2)}}.$$
Check $m_1 = m_2$ against Example 4.4. Discuss the limits $m_2 \to 0$ and $m_1 \to 0$: what are the frequencies and what does each limit mean physically?

**B6.** (Spherical pendulum: oscillations about conical motion.) Using $V_{\text{eff}}$ from (4.16), compute $V_{\text{eff}}''(\theta_0)$ at the conical equilibrium and show the small-oscillation frequency of $\theta$ is
$$\Omega^2 = \frac{g}{\ell\cos\theta_0}\left(1 + 3\cos^2\theta_0\right) = \dot\phi_0^2\left(1 + 3\cos^2\theta_0\right).$$
Deduce that $\Omega/\dot\phi_0$ lies strictly between 1 and 2 for $0 < \theta_0 < \pi/2$, and explain why this means the orbit, seen from above, precesses rather than closing. For $\ell = 1$ m and $\theta_0 = 40°$, compute $\dot\phi_0$ and $\Omega$.

**B7.** (Rayleigh dissipation; code.) (a) Prove (3.23) in general: for $L$ with no explicit time dependence and $Q_i^{\text{nc}} = -\partial\mathcal{F}/\partial\dot q_i$ with $\mathcal{F}$ homogeneous of degree 2 in $\dot q$, show $dh/dt = -2\mathcal{F}$. State and prove the Euler homogeneous-function identity you use. (b) For the double pendulum with linear drag $-c\dot{\mathbf{r}}_a$ on each bob, write $\mathcal{F}$ in terms of $\theta_1, \theta_2, \dot\theta_1, \dot\theta_2$ (use $T$ from (4.9) as a template) and the two generalized forces $Q_{\theta_i} = -\partial\mathcal{F}/\partial\dot\theta_i$. (c) *Code:* pass these $Q_i$ to `lagrangian_to_eom` in `project.py` (its `Q` argument), integrate from $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 2$ rad at rest with $c = 0.2$ kg/s for 60 s, plot $E(t)$, and verify numerically that $dE/dt = -2\mathcal{F}$ along the trajectory (compare a finite-difference derivative of $E$ with $-2\mathcal{F}$ evaluated on the solution).

**B8.** (Rotating frame sanity checks.) Take $\boldsymbol\omega = \omega\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ constant and $V = 0$. (a) A particle at rest in the inertial frame at $(r_0, 0, 0)$ appears to the rotating observer to move on a circle. Write $\mathbf{r}'(t)$ explicitly, compute $\dot{\mathbf{r}}'$ and $\ddot{\mathbf{r}}'$, and verify that (4.20) is satisfied term by term: show the Coriolis term is $-2m\omega^2\mathbf{r}'$ (inward) and the centrifugal term is $+m\omega^2\mathbf{r}'$ (outward), and that their sum is the required centripetal force. (b) A particle at rest in the *rotating* frame at $(r_0, 0, 0)$. What real force must act on it, in the inertial frame? Show (4.20) gives the same answer. (c) Explain in one sentence why the Coriolis force does no work in the rotating frame.

## C. Challenge (do at least one)

**C1.** (Rolling disk: non-holonomic, and the right equations.) A vertical disk of radius $R$ rolls without slipping on the plane with coordinates $(x, y, \theta, \phi)$ as in §3.2. (a) Prove the two constraints are not holonomic: show that from any configuration you can reach any other configuration by a sequence of allowed motions (roll, turn in place), so no function $f(x, y, \theta, \phi)$ can be conserved by all allowed motions unless it is constant. (Hint: the square loop of §3.2 changes $\phi$ by $4d/R$ at fixed $(x, y, \theta)$; vary $d$.) (b) The disk has mass $m$, moment of inertia $I_\phi$ about its axle and $I_\theta$ about the vertical, and $T = \frac12 m(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2) + \frac12 I_\phi\dot\phi^2 + \frac12 I_\theta\dot\theta^2$, $V = 0$. Write the Lagrange–d'Alembert equations: (3.18) with the constraint coefficient vectors $\mathbf{a}_1 = (1, 0, 0, -R\cos\theta)$ and $\mathbf{a}_2 = (0, 1, 0, -R\sin\theta)$ in place of $\nabla f_k$. (c) Solve them: show $\ddot\theta = 0$ and $\ddot\phi = 0$, so the disk rolls at constant speed while turning at a constant rate (a circle, or a line). Find $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ and interpret them as the sideways friction at the contact point. (d) (The vakonomic trap.) The free disk happens not to expose the error, so take the simplest system that does: a skate (knife-edge) of mass $m$ and moment of inertia $I$ about the vertical, sliding on a plane inclined so gravity has component $g\sin\beta$ along $+x$, with heading $\theta$ and the single constraint $\dot x\sin\theta - \dot y\cos\theta = 0$ (it moves only along its blade). $T = \frac12 m(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2) + \frac12 I\dot\theta^2$, $V = -mgx\sin\beta$. Write the Lagrange–d'Alembert equations with one multiplier and show $\ddot\theta = 0$. Then substitute $\dot y = \dot x\tan\theta$ into $L$, apply Euler–Lagrange to the result, and show it predicts $I\ddot\theta = m\dot x^2\sec^2\theta\tan\theta \ne 0$. Which step of §3.8 does the substitution violate?

**C2.** (Vertically shaken pendulum; Kapitza; code.) The pivot moves vertically, $y_p = a\cos\gamma t$. (a) Derive $\ddot\theta + \frac{1}{\ell}\left(g - a\gamma^2\cos\gamma t\right)\sin\theta = 0$ following Example 4.5. (b) Linearize about $\theta = 0$ to get the Mathieu equation, and about $\theta = \pi$ ($\theta = \pi + \epsilon$). (c) Landau and Lifshitz (*Mechanics*, §30, Problem 1) show by averaging over the fast oscillation that the inverted position is stable when $a^2\gamma^2 > 2g\ell$ (for $\gamma \gg \sqrt{g/\ell}$ and $a \ll \ell$). Sketch their argument: write $\theta = \Theta(t) + \xi(t)$ with $\xi$ the small fast response, solve for $\xi$, and average the equation for $\Theta$ to get an effective potential $V_{\text{eff}}(\Theta) = -mg\ell\cos\Theta + \frac{m a^2\gamma^2}{4}\sin^2\Theta$. Show $\Theta = \pi$ is a minimum of $V_{\text{eff}}$ exactly when $a^2\gamma^2 > 2g\ell$. (d) *Code:* integrate the full equation with $\ell = 1$ m, $a = 0.05$ m, starting from $\theta = \pi + 0.1$ at rest, for $\gamma = 150$ rad/s (criterion satisfied: $a^2\gamma^2 = 56.25 > 19.62$) and $\gamma = 60$ rad/s (criterion violated: $9 < 19.62$). Plot $\theta(t)$ for both and report whether the pendulum stays near $\pi$.

**C3.** (Slowly varying length: adiabatic invariant; code.) A pendulum's length is changed slowly by an external agent: $\ell = \ell(t)$ with $|\dot\ell|/\ell \ll \sqrt{g/\ell}$. (a) Show $L = \frac12 m\ell(t)^2\dot\theta^2 + mg\ell(t)\cos\theta$ (explain what happened to the $\frac12 m\dot\ell^2$ term) and derive $\ell\ddot\theta + 2\dot\ell\dot\theta + g\sin\theta = 0$. (b) Explain why neither $h$ nor $T + V$ is conserved. (c) For small angles define the oscillation energy $E = \frac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2 + \frac12 mg\ell\theta^2$ and compute $dE/dt$ exactly. Average over one period, assuming $\ell$ is nearly constant during it and using $\langle\frac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2\rangle = \langle\frac12 mg\ell\theta^2\rangle = E/2$, to show $\langle\dot E\rangle = -\frac{\dot\ell}{2\ell}E$. Conclude that $E/\omega$ with $\omega = \sqrt{g/\ell}$ is constant to leading order (the **adiabatic invariant**, CM-8), and that the amplitude scales as $\theta_{\max}\propto\ell^{-3/4}$. (d) *Code:* integrate (a) with $\ell(t) = 1 + 0.02t$ for $0 \le t \le 50$ s from $\theta = 0.1$ rad at rest. Plot $E/\omega$ and $\theta_{\max}\ell^{3/4}$ at the successive turning points ($\dot\theta = 0$); both should be flat to well under 1% (the reference code finds a few hundredths of a percent), while $E$ itself falls by about 30% as $\ell$ doubles.
