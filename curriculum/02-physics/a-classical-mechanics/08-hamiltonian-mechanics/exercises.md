# CM-8 Exercises

Conventions as in the notes: $H = \sum_ip_i\dot q_i - L$, $\dot q = \partial H/\partial p$, $\dot p = -\partial H/\partial q$, $\{F, G\} = \sum_i(\partial_{q_i}F\,\partial_{p_i}G - \partial_{p_i}F\,\partial_{q_i}G)$ so $\{q, p\} = +1$, $\mathsf{J} = \begin{pmatrix}0 & I\\ -I & 0\end{pmatrix}$, $J = \oint p\,dq$.

## A. Warm-up (do all; 5–10 minutes each)

**A1.** Compute the Legendre transform $g(p) = \max_v[pv - f(v)]$ and check $g'(p) = v$ for
(a) $f(v) = v^4/4$;
(b) the free relativistic particle, $f(v) = -mc^2\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$ on $|v| < c$. Show that $p = \gamma mv$ and $g = \sqrt{p^2c^2 + m^2c^4}$. Which values of $p$ are allowed?

**A2.** Write $H(q, p)$ and Hamilton's equations for
(a) an Atwood machine: masses $m_1$, $m_2$ on a massless string over a massless pulley, with $x$ the distance $m_1$ has descended;
(b) a bead of mass $m$ sliding without friction on the fixed wire $z = \frac12kx^2$ in uniform gravity, using $x$ as the coordinate.
In (b), is $p = m\dot x$?

**A3.** Using only (3.15) and properties (i)–(iii) of §3.6, compute $\{q^2, p^2\}$; $\{q, H\}$ and $\{p, H\}$ for $H = p^2/2m + V(q)$; and $\{qp, H\}$ for the oscillator $H = p^2/2m + \frac12m\omega^2q^2$. Use the last result and (3.14) to explain why $\langle T\rangle = \langle V\rangle$ on average over a period (the virial theorem used in §3.9).

**A4.** Which of these one-degree-of-freedom maps are canonical? Use $\det M$.
(a) $Q = q\cos\alpha - p\sin\alpha$, $P = q\sin\alpha + p\cos\alpha$. (b) $Q = 2q$, $P = p/2$. (c) $Q = q^2$, $P = p/2q$ ($q > 0$). (d) $Q = q$, $P = p + q^2$. (e) $Q = p$, $P = q$.

**A5.** For $H = \frac12p^2 + \frac13x^3 - x$, find the fixed points and classify each as elliptic or hyperbolic. Find the separatrix energy and the two points where the separatrix crosses $p = 0$. Sketch the phase portrait and say which energies give bounded motion.

**A6.** A mass $m = 2$ kg on a spring oscillates at $\omega = 3$ rad/s with amplitude $0.5$ m. Compute $E$, the action $J$, and $\nu = dE/dJ$. If Bohr–Sommerfeld ($J = nh$) applied, what would $n$ be? What does that say about seeing quantization in a lab spring?

## B. Core (do all)

**B1.** (Canonical vs kinetic momentum.) For $H = |\mathbf{p} - q_e\mathbf{A}|^2/2m + q_e\Phi$ (3.9):
(a) Show that Hamilton's equations give $m\ddot{\mathbf{r}} = q_e(\mathbf{E} + \dot{\mathbf{r}}\times\mathbf{B})$, with $\mathbf{E} = -\nabla\Phi - \partial_t\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$. Remember that $\mathbf{A}$ depends on $\mathbf{r}(t)$.
(b) For a uniform field $B\hat{\mathbf{z}}$, compare the gauges $\mathbf{A} = (0, Bx, 0)$ and $\mathbf{A} = \frac{B}{2}(-y, x, 0)$. In which is $p_y$ conserved? Is $m\dot y$ conserved in either? What does this say about whether canonical momentum is "physical"?

**B2.** (Area and Euler's methods.) For the oscillator with $m = \omega = 1$:
(a) Write the one-step matrices of explicit Euler ($q' = q + hp$, $p' = p - hq$) and symplectic Euler (3.31).
(b) Compute both determinants. For $h = 0.1$, by what factor does explicit Euler multiply phase-space area after 1000 steps?
(c) Explain the outward spiral of explicit Euler in the language of §3.7.

**B3.** (Hidden symmetry.) For the 2D isotropic oscillator $H = \frac12(p_x^2 + p_y^2 + x^2 + y^2)$ define $A = \frac12(p_x^2 - p_y^2 + x^2 - y^2)$, $B = p_xp_y + xy$, $L = xp_y - yp_x$.
(a) Show that $A$, $B$, $L$ are conserved.
(b) Compute $\{A, B\}$, $\{B, L\}$, $\{L, A\}$, and explain how (a) and Poisson's theorem predict that the answers are conserved.
(c) Show $A^2 + B^2 + L^2 = H^2$. What does the existence of three conserved quantities beyond $H$ (in two degrees of freedom) say about the orbits?

**B4.** (When $H \ne T + V$.) A bead of mass $m$ slides on a frictionless hoop of radius $R$ that is driven to rotate at constant $\Omega$ about its vertical diameter; $\theta$ is measured from the bottom. $L = \frac12mR^2(\dot\theta^2 + \Omega^2\sin^2\theta) + mgR\cos\theta$ (CM-4 Example 4.3).
(a) Find $p_\theta$ and $H$. Show that $H$ is conserved and $H - (T + V) = -mR^2\Omega^2\sin^2\theta$. Why is $T + V$ not conserved?
(b) Find the fixed points of the $(\theta, p_\theta)$ flow and show that the bottom changes from elliptic to hyperbolic at $\Omega^2 = g/R$.

**B5.** (A generating function.) Find the canonical map generated by the type-1 function $F_1(q, Q) = \frac12m\omega q^2\cot Q$. Show that it is (3.21), and find $K$ for the oscillator.

**B6.** (Liouville for a Gaussian.) A free particle ($H = p^2/2m$) has initial phase-space density $\rho_0$ Gaussian with independent spreads $\sigma_q$, $\sigma_p$.
(a) Solve the Liouville equation (3.11) for $\rho(q, p, t)$.
(b) Show the covariance matrix evolves as $\Sigma(t) = M\Sigma_0M^\top$ with $M = \begin{pmatrix}1 & t/m\\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$. Find $\sigma_q^2(t)$, $\operatorname{cov}(q, p)(t)$ and $\det\Sigma(t)$. Evaluate for $\sigma_q = 0.7$, $\sigma_p = 0.4$, $m = 1$, $t = 3$.
(c) $\sigma_q$ grows without bound. Explain why that does not contradict Liouville's theorem.

**B7.** (Hamilton–Jacobi, once more.) For the free particle:
(a) Solve (3.28) by separation, $S = W(q) - Et$, and recover $q(t)$ from $\beta = \partial S/\partial E$.
(b) Verify that $S(q, t) = m(q - q_0)^2/2t$ also solves (3.28). Show that $\partial S/\partial q$ is the momentum of the straight path from $(q_0, 0)$ to $(q, t)$ and that $S$ equals $\int_0^tL\,dt$ along it.

**B8.** (Code: extend Part 5 of `project.py`.) Rerun the $\theta_0 = 2$ pendulum for $10^3$ periods with 50 and with 100 steps per period. Record RK4's final relative energy error and Verlet's maximum relative energy error for each.
(a) By what factors do they fall when $h$ is halved? Explain Verlet's factor from §3.11.
(b) RK4 is fourth order, yet its error falls by about 32, not 16. Explain using the RK4 amplification factor $R(z) = 1 + z + z^2/2 + z^3/6 + z^4/24$ at $z = i\omega h$ for the linearized pendulum.

## C. Challenge (do at least one)

**C1.** (Pendulum action and period.)
(a) Use the substitution $\sin(\theta/2) = k\sin\varphi$, $k = \sin(\theta_0/2)$, to show $T = 4\sqrt{l/g}\,\mathrm{K}(k)$.
(b) Show $J = 16\,ml\sqrt{gl}\,[\mathrm{E}(k) - (1 - k^2)\mathrm{K}(k)]$, and verify $dJ/dE = T$ using $\frac{d\mathrm{E}}{dk} = \frac{\mathrm{E} - \mathrm{K}}{k}$ and $\frac{d\mathrm{K}}{dk} = \frac{\mathrm{E}}{k(1 - k^2)} - \frac{\mathrm{K}}{k}$.
(c) Using $\mathrm{K}(k) \approx \ln(4/k')$ as $k' = \sqrt{1 - k^2} \to 0$, show $T \approx 2\sqrt{l/g}\,\ln\!\big(32\,mgl/(E_s - E)\big)$ near the separatrix. Compare with the exact $T$ at $E = 0.99$ and $0.999$ ($m = g = l = 1$).

**C2.** (Kepler action-angle variables.) For $H = \frac{p_r^2}{2m} + \frac{p_\phi^2}{2mr^2} - \frac{k}{r}$ with $E < 0$:
(a) Show $J_\phi = \oint p_\phi\,d\phi = 2\pi\ell$ and $J_r = \oint p_r\,dr = \pi k\sqrt{2m/|E|} - 2\pi\ell$.
(b) Hence $E = -2\pi^2mk^2/(J_r + J_\phi)^2$. Show $\nu_r = \nu_\phi$ and explain why this means every bound orbit closes. Check $1/\nu$ against Kepler's third law.
(c) Check $J_r$ and the radial period numerically for $m = k = 1$, $E = -0.3$, $\ell = 0.8$ (Your turn 2).

**C3.** (The standard map; Your turn 3.) $p' = p + K\sin\theta$, $\theta' = \theta + p'$ (mod $2\pi$).
(a) Show it is canonical, and that it is symplectic Euler with $h = 1$ for a rotor kicked once per unit time.
(b) Find its fixed points and the range of $K$ for which each is elliptic.
(c) Iterate 50 random initial points for 2000 kicks at $K = 0.5$, $0.97$, $1.5$ and plot $(\theta, p \bmod 2\pi)$. Track $\max|p_n - p_0|$. Explain the change between $K = 0.97$ and $K = 1.5$ using KAM curves.
