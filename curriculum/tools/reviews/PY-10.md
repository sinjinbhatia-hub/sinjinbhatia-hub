### M1 — notes.md §2 line 25 and Build 1 "What just happened" line 127 — MEDIUM
**FIXED (both places now say seventy bytecodes, about twenty of them arithmetic on boxed floats; `dis.dis(accel_loop)` named as the check).**
Claim: one pair term of eq. (3.1) is "some twenty bytecodes". Measured with `dis.get_instructions(accel_loop)`: the inner-loop body (lines 35–44 of forces.py) is 67 bytecodes, plus FOR_ITER/STORE_FAST per iteration, i.e. about 70 per pair term; about 20 of them are the arithmetic and subscript operations that dispatch on boxed floats (3 subtractions, 6 subscripts, 9 mul/add, 1 div, 1 sqrt call). 339 ns / 70 ≈ 5 ns per bytecode is the realistic picture.
Fix: "some seventy bytecodes, about twenty of them arithmetic on boxed floats" in both places.

### M3 — project.py line 173 — MEDIUM (fragile assert on an environment-dependent quantity)
**FIXED (`assert faults_work < 5 and faults_numpy >= faults_work`); project.py re-run: exit 0, 38.7 s, 593/0 faults printed.**
`assert faults_work < 5 <= faults_numpy` requires `accel_numpy` to pay ≥5 minor page faults per call. The notes (Build 3) say that count depends on allocator history the user cannot see: it was 0 in the notes session, 2 in a fresh process, 593 in project.py's process only because an earlier `import matplotlib` moved glibc's mmap threshold. On another allocator (macOS, musl, a different glibc) or a different import order the script exits 1 for a non-physics reason. What the notes actually claim is: the workspace pays none, and never more than the allocating version.
Fix: `assert faults_work < 5 and faults_numpy >= faults_work`; keep the print.

### M5 — notes.md Build 2, line 173 — MEDIUM (wrong mechanism)
**FIXED (broadcasts and reductions along the length-3 axis are the slow passes, 3–8×; elementwise passes cost the same).**
Claim: in the (N, N, 3) layout "every pass runs in loops of three elements and pays the loop overhead 40,000 times". Measured pass by pass at N = 200 (min of 7×50): the elementwise pass `d * d` costs 43 µs in both layouts (NumPy collapses a contiguous array to one loop). The slow passes are those that broadcast or reduce along the length-3 axis: the broadcast difference 424 vs 95 µs, `(d*d).sum(axis=-1)` 747 vs 109 µs, `w[:, :, None] * d` 208 vs 63 µs, `wd.sum(axis=1)` 846 vs 101 µs.
Fix: say that the broadcasts and reductions along the length-3 axis run their inner loop over three elements, 40,000 times, while a plain elementwise pass is unaffected.
